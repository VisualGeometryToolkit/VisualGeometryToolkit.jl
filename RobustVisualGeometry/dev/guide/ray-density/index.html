Ray Density Correction for Posterior Weights

In scale-free marginal RANSAC (the Student-t + uniform marginal score, RansacScoringDoneRight's StudentTUniformScoring).

1. Setup

Consider a geometric model $\theta$ with $d_g$-dimensional algebraic constraint $\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}, \theta) = 0$, where $\boldsymbol{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the measurement. With Gaussian noise $\boldsymbol{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}, \sigma^2 I_d)$, the first-order approximation gives:

\[\boldsymbol{g} \approx G_x (\boldsymbol{x} - \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}) \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\; \sigma^2 \tilde{\Sigma}_g), \qquad \tilde{\Sigma}_g = G_x G_x^\top.\]

The whitened constraint is $\boldsymbol{w} = \tilde{\Sigma}_g^{-1/2} \boldsymbol{g} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_{d_g})$, and the squared Mahalanobis distance is:

\[q_i = \boldsymbol{g}_i^\top \tilde{\Sigma}_{g_i}^{-1} \boldsymbol{g}_i = \|\boldsymbol{w}_i\|^2, \qquad \frac{q_i}{\sigma^2} \sim \chi^2(d_g).\]

The scalar Sampson distance is $r_i = \sqrt{q_i} \geq 0$.

2. The Two Density Spaces

2.1 Whitened-space (d_g-dimensional) density

The density of $\boldsymbol{w} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_g}$ under the inlier model is:

\[p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}(\boldsymbol{w} \mid \sigma) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d_g/2} \exp\!\bigl(-\|\boldsymbol{w}\|^2/(2\sigma^2)\bigr).\]

The outlier density in $d_g$-dimensional whitened space:

\[p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(d_g)} = (2a)^{-d_g}.\]

2.2 1D ray density (Shekhovtsov, Section 3.2)

Transforming to polar coordinates $r = \|\boldsymbol{w}\|$, the volume element is $d\boldsymbol{w} = S_{d_g} \, r^{d_g - 1} \, dr$ where $S_{d_g} = 2\pi^{d_g/2}/\Gamma(d_g/2)$ is the surface area of the unit sphere. The ray density (marginal density of the scalar distance $r$) given known $\sigma$ is the $\chi_{d_g}$ distribution:

\[p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(1)}(r \mid \sigma) = \frac{2}{\Gamma(d_g/2)\,(2\sigma^2)^{d_g/2}} \; r^{d_g-1} \exp\!\bigl(-r^2/(2\sigma^2)\bigr).\]

Predictive ray density after marginalizing $\sigma^2$. In the scale-free marginal framework, $\sigma^2$ is integrated out under the Jeffreys prior using the sufficient statistics $\hat\sigma^2 = \text{RSS}/\nu$ and $\nu = (k-m)\,d_g$ from the sweep partition. The predictive density for a new point is a multivariate Student-$t$ with $\nu$ degrees of freedom, which in 1D ray-density form becomes:

\[p_{\mathrm{in,pred}}^{(1)}(r \mid \nu, \hat\sigma^2) = \frac{2}{\Gamma(d_g/2)} \cdot \frac{\Gamma\!\bigl(\tfrac{\nu+d_g}{2}\bigr)} {\Gamma\!\bigl(\tfrac{\nu}{2}\bigr) \, (\nu\,\hat\sigma^2)^{d_g/2}} \; r^{d_g-1} \Bigl(1 + \frac{r^2}{\nu\,\hat\sigma^2}\Bigr)^{\!-(\nu+d_g)/2}.\]

This reduces to the Gaussian $\chi_{d_g}$ as $\nu \to \infty$ and captures the heavier tails from scale uncertainty at finite $\nu$.

The outlier ray density (from a uniform distribution in $\mathbb{R}^d$) is constant:

\[p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}(r) = \frac{1}{2a}.\]

Key point: For $d_g = 1$, the Jacobian factor is $r^{d_g-1} = r^0 = 1$, so the two formulations coincide. For $d_g = 2$, the ray density has an extra factor of $r$, which changes the relative weighting of inliers at different distances.

3. Effect on Posterior Weights

The posterior inlier probability for point $i$ is:

\[w_i = \frac{\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}(r_i)} {\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}(r_i) + (1-\gamma) \, p_{\mathrm{out}}(r_i)}.\]

3.1 Whitened-space (d_g-dimensional) formulation

Both densities are evaluated in $d_g$-dimensional space:

\[w_i^{(d_g)} = \frac{\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}(r_i)} {\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}(r_i) + (1-\gamma) \, p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(d_g)}} \tag{1}\]

where $p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}$ is evaluated at $\|\boldsymbol{w}\| = r_i$ and $p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(d_g)} = (2a)^{-d_g}$.

3.2 Correct 1D ray density

Both densities are 1D ray densities:

\[w_i^{(1)} = \frac{\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(1)}(r_i)} {\gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(1)}(r_i) + (1-\gamma) \, p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}} \tag{2}\]

where $p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(1)}(r) = S_{d_g} \, r^{d_g-1} \, p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}(r)$ and $p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)} = (2a)^{-1}$.

3.3 The discrepancy

The ratio of inlier-to-outlier likelihood in the two formulations:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{$d_g$-dimensional:} \quad \Lambda_i^{(d_g)} &= \frac{p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(d_g)}(r_i)}{p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(d_g)}} = (2a)^{d_g} \, (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d_g/2} \exp(-r_i^2/(2\sigma^2)), \\[6pt] \text{Correct:} \quad \Lambda_i^{(1)} &= \frac{p_{\mathrm{in}}^{(1)}(r_i)}{p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}} = 2a \cdot S_{d_g} \, r_i^{d_g-1} \, (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-d_g/2} \exp(-r_i^2/(2\sigma^2)). \end{aligned}\]

Their ratio is:

\[\frac{\Lambda_i^{(1)}}{\Lambda_i^{(d_g)}} = \frac{S_{d_g} \, r_i^{d_g-1}}{(2a)^{d_g - 1}}.\]

For $d_g = 1$: the ratio is $S_1 \cdot r^0 / 1 = 2$, a constant that cancels in the posterior sigmoid. No effect.

For $d_g = 2$: the ratio is $S_2 \cdot r / (2a) = 2\pi r / (2a)$, which is proportional to $r_i$. This means:

  • Points very close to the model ($r_i \ll 1$): the $d_g$-dimensional formulation overestimates their posterior $w_i$ relative to the correct ray density formulation.
  • Points far from the model ($r_i \gg 1$): the $d_g$-dimensional formulation underestimates their posterior.

4. Impact

4.1 Model selection (scoring evaluation)

For the sweep-based score $S(\theta) = \max_k S(k)$, the ray density correction enters as an additive term $(d_g - 1) \sum_{i \in I} \log r_i$ in the inlier log-likelihood after marginalizing $\sigma^2$. This term penalizes models whose inliers have very small residuals.

Empirically, this correction hurts model selection: the corrected "Ray-Density" score ranks models worse than the uncorrected sweep. The $d_g$-dimensional formulation (without the Jacobian) is more discriminative for ranking models because it directly rewards small residuals.

4.2 Local optimization (RANSAC LO)

When the posterior weights $w_i$ are used as soft IRLS weights for the weighted DLT refit, the discrepancy means:

  • For $d_g = 1$ (F-matrix): no effect.
  • For $d_g = 2$ (homography): points near the model get too much weight (missing $r_i$ factor), while moderate-distance inliers get too little. This biases the refit toward going exactly through nearby points rather than balancing all inliers.

The practical impact depends on the inlier noise distribution. With typical inlier distances of 0.5–2 px, the correction factor $r_i$ is of order 1 and the effect is moderate.

4.3 Scale estimation

The weighted scale estimate $\hat\sigma^2 = \sum w_i q_i / (d_g \sum w_i)$ is also affected: overweighting near-zero residuals pulls $\hat\sigma^2$ down. The Newton update for $\nu$ (degrees of freedom) in the EM M-step similarly uses the biased weights.

5. Summary

In the $d_g$-dimensional formulation:

$d_g = 1$ (F-matrix)$d_g = 2$ (homography)
Sweep score (model selection)correctcorrect*
Posterior weights (LO)correctbiased (missing $r_i$ factor)
Posterior mixture scorecorrectwrong (space mismatch)

*The sweep score is correct in both the $d_g$-dimensional and 1D ray density formulations — they differ by a model-dependent additive term, but cross-validation over $a$ compensates. Empirically, the $d_g$-dimensional version ranks models better.

Conclusions:

  1. The sweep score works well for model selection in the $d_g$-dimensional formulation.

  2. Posterior weights for $d_g \geq 2$ are corrected by using the 1D predictive Student-$t$ ray density $p_{\mathrm{in,pred}}^{(1)}(r)$ (which includes the Jacobian $r^{d_g-1}$) and the 1D outlier density $p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)} = (2a)^{-1}$.

  3. The predictive ray density mixture gives an alternative soft scoring function:

    \[S_{\mathrm{soft}}(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^n \log\!\Bigl( \gamma \, p_{\mathrm{in,pred}}^{(1)}(r_i \mid \nu, \hat\sigma^2) + (1-\gamma) \, p_{\mathrm{out}}^{(1)}(r_i) \Bigr),\]

    where $\gamma = k^*/n$, $\hat\sigma^2 = \text{RSS}/\nu$, and $\nu = (k^* - m)\,d_g$ are all derived from the sweep partition. This combines the sweep's scale estimation with GaU-style soft weighting.
