Lenses API

The lens families — two lens kinds, the projections, the terms and their combinators — the lens contract on rays with everything derived from it, the coefficient chart and record, and the batch surfaces. See the Lens Families and Back-projection and Undistortion guides.

Projection and Lens Models

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.AbstractDistortionTerm — Type
AbstractDistortionTerm

A distortion term in a projection's frame: a MAP x ↦ f(x) (Radial, Tilt, FieldOfView) or a DELTA x ↦ Δ(x) added to the point (Tangential, ThinPrism) — see Additive.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.AbstractLensModel — Type
AbstractLensModel

Base type for all lens models.

A lens model determines how 3D rays through the optical center map to points on the sensor, including both the projection geometry and any aberrations.

Interface — the lens contract is on rays

A lens is a map between RAYS and NORMALIZED image points, and the pair project(lens, X) (a ray → its image point) and backproject(lens, xd) (an image point → its unit ray) is the whole contract (geometry/cameras/lens_rays.jl): NaN where the lens does not image, generic in the number type, allocation-free. There are two kinds of lens: a forward Lens — a projection onto a frame followed by a distortion in that frame — and a backward LiftLens, whose image point lifts to its ray in closed form (lenses.jl). Everything else is derived from the pair, for every lens: transform_deriv(lens, X), transform_deriv_params(lens, X), project_with_jacobians, the radial profile (projected_radius / view_angle), and the perspective views distort_normalized / undistort_normalized with the functor lens(x).

A lens is a CoordinateTransformations.Transformation, so its derivative is that package's transform_deriv(lens, x) (ForwardDiff through the perspective view), and its back-projection is a map too: inv(lens) is an InverseLens whose call is undistort_normalized and whose transform_deriv is ForwardDiff through it. Compositions inherit both — transform_deriv(K ∘ inv(lens) ∘ K⁻¹, x) is the chain rule. The other generic defaults are imaging_transform(intrinsics, lens) and the capability trait has_distortion(lens). Every lens model here is a central projection (single center of projection); finite-aperture models are downstream.

Subtyping CoordinateTransformations.Transformation makes every imaging chain a ComposedTransformation regardless of lens (the Pinhole and distorted paths used to produce DIFFERENT composition types, breaking structural pattern-matches like pnp_pixel_whitening).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.AbstractProjection — Type
AbstractProjection

A ray → frame-point projection: the first stage of a forward Lens. The five fixed projections (Perspective, Equidistant, Stereographic, Equisolid, Orthographic) each land on a retinal frame (projection_frame); the parametric ones (DoubleSphere, Unified, ExtendedUnified) carry coefficients and land on a frame their coefficients define.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Additive — Type
Additive(m, Δ₁, …, Δₙ)
Additive(Δ₁, …, Δₙ)

Terms evaluated at the SAME point and summed: m(x) + (Δ₁(x) + … + Δₙ(x)), the deltas left to right; without a leading map, x + (Δ₁(x) + …). OpenCV's projectPoints and COLMAP's Distortion() are of this form.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Chain — Type
Chain(D₁, D₂, …)

Distortions applied in sequence, in the projection's frame: Dₙ(…D₁(x)). Each stage is a map term or an Additive. Aria's Fisheye624 (COLMAP RAD_TAN_THIN_PRISM_FISHEYE) is a radial factor followed by tangential and thin-prism deltas of the radially distorted point.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Division — Type
Division(k₁, …, k_Q)

The division model (Fitzgibbon 2001): x_u = x_d / (1 + k₁ρ² + … + k_Qρ²Q), the lift (x_d, 1 + k₁ρ² + …): LiftLens(Radial((), (k₁, …))).

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.DoubleSphere — Type
DoubleSphere(xi, alpha) <: AbstractProjection

Usenko, Demmel & Cremers' double-sphere projection (3DV 2018): with d₁ = ‖X‖, z_s = ξ·d₁ + X₃ and d₂ = √(X₁² + X₂² + z_s²), x = X₁₂ / (α·d₂ + (1 − α)·z_s). ξ = α = 0 is the perspective projection. Valid for rays inside the paper's field-of-view bound; the closed-form inverse has the paper's domain. α moves through its logit in LensManifold.

Equidistant projection r = θ, onto the EquidistantFrame; θ < 180°.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Equisolid — Type

Equisolid-angle projection r = 2 sin(θ/2), onto the EquisolidFrame; θ ≤ 180°.

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Even — Type

The power basis of a Radial factor: Even — ρ², ρ⁴, … (the default); Full — ρ², ρ³, ρ⁴, ….

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.ExtendedUnified — Type
ExtendedUnified(alpha, beta) <: AbstractProjection

The enhanced unified camera model (Khomutenko et al. 2016; Kalibr's eucm): with d = √(β(X₁² + X₂²) + X₃²), x = X₁₂ / (α·d + (1 − α)·X₃).

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.FieldOfView — Type
FieldOfView(omega)

Devernay & Faugeras' field-of-view model (COLMAP FOV, Kalibr fov): x ↦ x·atan(2r·tan(ω/2)) / (r·ω).

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Full — Type

The power basis of a Radial factor: Even — ρ², ρ⁴, … (the default); Full — ρ², ρ³, ρ⁴, ….

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.KannalaBrandt — Type
KannalaBrandt(k₁, …, k_N)

Kannala & Brandt's fisheye (2006) in OpenCV's/COLMAP's form, θ_d = θ·(1 + k₁θ² + … + k_Nθ²ᴺ): Lens(Equidistant(), Radial((k₁, …, k_N))).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Lens — Type
Lens(projection[, distortion]) <: AbstractLensModel

A forward lens: the ray is projected onto projection's frame and the frame point is distorted by distortion (nothing, a term, an Additive or a Chain). Projection is closed form; back-projection inverts the distortion (backproject).

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.LiftLens — Type
LiftLens(radial::Radial) <: AbstractLensModel

A backward lens: the image point x_d lifts to the ray (x_d·N(ρ), D(ρ)), ρ = |x_d| — closed-form back-projection; projection solves the image radius as a root on the ray. D crossing zero is the 90° circle and D < 0 the field behind it, so the lens images past 90°.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Orthographic — Type

Orthographic projection r = sin θ, onto the OrthographicFrame; θ ≤ 90°.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Perspective — Type

Perspective projection x = X₁₂/X₃ (r = tan θ), onto the GnomonicFrame; θ < 90°.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Pinhole — Type

The ideal perspective lens, Lens(Perspective()).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Radial — Type
Radial{B, P, Q, T}(k::NTuple{P}, d::NTuple{Q})
Radial(k, d = ())                  # B = Even
Radial{Full}(k, d)

The radial factor F(ρ) = N(ρ)/D(ρ) with N = 1 + Σ kᵢ φᵢ(ρ) and D = 1 + Σ dⱼ φⱼ(ρ), where the basis B sets φᵢ: Even — ρ²ⁱ; Full — ρⁱ⁺¹ (no linear term: the factor is smooth on the axis).

In a Lens it is the map x ↦ F(|x|)·x in the projection's frame — OpenCV's rational radial factor (k₁…k₃ / k₄…k₆) in the perspective frame, Kannala–Brandt's θ-polynomial in the equidistant frame. In a LiftLens it is the undistortion of the image point, x_u = F(ρ_d)·x_d, represented as the ray (x_d·N, D) — the division model, BabelCalib's rational model, Scaramuzza's polynomial.

Coefficient names: k1…kP then d1…dQ.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Scaramuzza — Type
Scaramuzza(a₂, …, a_{N+1})

Scaramuzza's omnidirectional model (Scaramuzza, Martinelli & Siegwart 2006; MATLAB's fisheyeIntrinsics), normalized: the lift (x_d, 1 + a₂ρ² + a₃ρ³ + …) — LiftLens(Radial{Full}((), (a₂, …))). The coefficients are named d1, d2, … for a₂, a₃, ….

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Stereographic — Type

Stereographic projection r = 2 tan(θ/2), onto the StereographicFrame; θ < 180°.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Tangential — Type
Tangential(p1, p2)

Brown's decentering delta in OpenCV's convention: Δ = (2p₁uv + p₂(r² + 2u²), 2p₂uv + p₁(r² + 2v²)).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.ThinPrism — Type
ThinPrism{N, T}(sx::NTuple{N}, sy::NTuple{N})

The thin-prism delta Δ = (Σ sxᵢ r²ⁱ, Σ syᵢ r²ⁱ): OpenCV's (s₁, s₂; s₃, s₄) is N = 2, COLMAP's (sx₁; sy₁) is N = 1.

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.Tilt — Type
Tilt(tx, ty)

OpenCV's tilted-sensor map (CALIB_TILTED_MODEL, τx, τy): the projective map of computeTiltProjectionMatrix(τx, τy) applied to (x, 1).

Unified(xi) <: AbstractProjection

The unified camera model (Geyer & Daniilidis; Mei & Rives 2007; Kalibr's omni, OpenCV's omnidir): x = X₁₂ / (X₃ + ξ·‖X‖).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.radial — Method
radial(lens) -> Radial | nothing

The lens's radially symmetric factor: a lone Radial distortion, the leading Radial of an Additive, the lift of a LiftLens; nothing otherwise. Its folds and poles bound what the lens images (monotone_radius, view_angle_limit), and it is what a regression onto a radially symmetric family fits.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.InverseLens — Type
InverseLens{L} <: CoordinateTransformations.Transformation

The back-projection of a lens as a first-class map: inv(lens) returns one, inv(inv(lens)) === lens, its call is undistort_normalized(lens, xd), and its transform_deriv is ForwardDiff through that map (lensrays.jl). Composes like any transformation, so the pixel-domain undistortion of a camera is `inv(distortionmap(model))with the chain rule for its derivative. APinhole` is its own inverse.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.has_distortion — Method
has_distortion(lens) -> Bool

Does the lens bend rays at all? (false ⇒ undistort_normalized is the identity.) Relation to the framed imaging chain (0.10): has_distortion(lens) == false means the distortion is the identity in the lens's defining frame, so imaging_chain COLLAPSES the ray frame onto DistortedFrame — the chain has no distortion stage and K consumes the ray frame directly (see the collapse rule at DistortedFrame and distortion_frame). Every frame here assumes a single center of projection.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.imaging_transform — Method
imaging_transform(intrinsics, lens) -> ComposedTransformation
imaging_transform(model::CameraModel)

The composed camera-space → pixel-space map as a value: AffineMap(K) ∘ lens, the lens applied to the ray (its functor is project on 3-vectors). It is project(model, X) for every lens and every ray — past 90° for a lens that images there, NaN where the lens does not (for a Pinhole, every Z ≤ 0). Its derivative is the chain rule through transform_deriv(lens, X). The fully-typed, junction-checked counterpart is imaging_chain, which mints the framed composite {ViewFrame{id} → ImageFrame{id}} through the frame chain documented at DistortedFrame.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.monotone_radius — Method
monotone_radius(lens) -> Real

The distorted radius up to which the lens's perspective view is a monotone bijection — the bound a whole-extent perspective undistortion clips to (undistorted_rect, valid_rect, division_bounded). Beyond it the view folds or diverges and project answers NaN. The profile's first fold or pole, whichever comes first, mapped to the distorted radius; both are found exactly, as first positive polynomial roots. Inf for a lens whose profile never folds.

  • the radial factor (radial) of a forward lens: the fold or pole of its profile r ↦ r·F(r²) in the projection's frame, capped at the frame's edge (a ray cannot leave the sphere); decentering terms do not enter, as in view_angle_limit.
  • a LiftLens: the first pole of D (the 90° circle) or fold of the lift — 1/√|k| for Division(k).
source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.radial_part — Method
radial_part(lens) -> AbstractLensModel

The radially symmetric part of lens: its projection with only its radial factor (radial) — the decentering terms dropped; a lift is its own radial part. What view_angle_limit bounds and what a regression onto a radially symmetric family can represent.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.view_angle_limit — Method
view_angle_limit(lens; cap) -> θ_max

The largest view angle the lens's radial profile honestly represents: where the profile stops being finite and strictly increasing (a fold, a pole, or the frame's own edge). Closed forms where the lens has one — the fold or pole of a forward lens's radial factor read through its frame, never past the frame's edge (π/2 − 10⁻³ for the perspective frame); a lift's fold; the double sphere's FOV bound — and a monotonicity scan of projected_radius otherwise. cap bounds the answer: the generic scan runs to it (default π − 10⁻³); the closed forms clamp to it (default none).

VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.project! — Method
project!(u, m, X; exec = Auto()) -> u
backproject!(X, lens, u; exec = Auto()) -> X
distort_normalized!(xd, lens, x; exec = Auto()) -> xd
undistort_normalized!(x, lens, xd; exec = Auto()) -> x

project / backproject / distort_normalized / undistort_normalized of every point of a batch, into u / X / … — arrays of static vectors on any backend. m is a lens or anything else project takes (a camera). A point the lens does not image comes back NaN. exec chooses how a host batch is split across threads (pointwise!); every policy gives identical results.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.project_with_jacobians — Method
project_with_jacobians(lens, X::StaticVector{3}) -> (u, ∂u/∂X, ∂u/∂p)

The image point and both Jacobians — transform_deriv(lens, X) and transform_deriv_params(lens, X) — in ONE ForwardDiff evaluation over (X, p): the entry point for a bundle's or a PnP solver's per-observation residual.

This is GTSAM's project(point, Dpose, Dpoint, Dcal), with the Jacobians RETURNED rather than written into optional output arguments; the Ceres analogue is what a CostFunction::Evaluate fills.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.projected_radius — Method
projected_radius(lens, θ) -> ρ_d

A lens's radial projection function, read off the contract: the image radius of the ray at view angle θ (the classical r(θ) — equidistant r = θ, stereographic 2·tan(θ/2), Kannala–Brandt's θ-polynomial, …), i.e. project at azimuth 0, so it holds past 90° wherever the lens does. NaN where the lens does not image; θ ≥ π — the exact backward ray, a circle rather than a point — never is. Its inverse is view_angle.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.radial_ratio — Method
radial_ratio(r::Radial, u) -> N(u)/D(u)

The radial factor of r at its basis variable u (ρ² for the Even basis): for a LiftLens, the undistortion x_u = x_d · radial_ratio(radial(lens), ρ_d²).

undistort_normalized(lens, xd::StaticVector{2}) -> Vec2

The perspective point of the ray behind the image point xd — the view of backproject(lens, xd) on the plane Z = 1. NaN where the lens does not image xd, and where its ray has Z ≤ 0 (no perspective point exists).

VisualGeometryCore.backproject — Method
backproject(lens, xd::StaticVector{2}) -> Vec3

The UNIT ray behind the normalized image point xd — the other half of the lens contract, with project(lens, X). NaN where the lens does not image the point (outside its frame's domain, past a fold, a non-converged inverse), never a throw.

source
VisualGeometryCore.backproject — Method
backproject(lens::LiftLens, xd) -> Vec3

The lift (x_d·N, D), normalized: D = 0 is the 90° ray and D < 0 a ray behind it; only the Euclidean point x_d·N/D has a pole there, not the direction. Past the lift's first fold (where its view angle first stops increasing) the point is not an image of the lens and the ray is NaN — found exactly, so a point past a second fold is NaN too: the same branch project solves on.

source
VisualGeometryCore.project — Method
project(lens, X::StaticVector{3}) -> Vec2

The NORMALIZED image point of the ray through X (any positive scale) — half of the lens contract, with backproject(lens, xd). NaN where the lens does not image the ray, never a throw; allocation-free and generic in the number type (Float32 kernels, ForwardDiff Duals).

A Lens projects the ray onto its projection's frame and distorts the frame point there; no perspective hop is involved unless the projection IS the perspective one, whose domain genuinely ends at 90°. A LiftLens solves the image radius as a root on the ray.

source
VisualGeometryCore.project — Method
project(lens::LiftLens, X) -> Vec2

The image point of a ray under the lift, solved ON THE RAY: with (r, z) = (sin θ, cos θ) the image radius ρ is the root of h(ρ) = ρ·N·z − r·D on the lift's monotone branch. NaN past the fold, where the lens images nothing.

source
VisualGeometryCore.Cameras.LensManifold — Type
LensManifold(lens) <: Manifold

The lens's manifold anchored at lens: lens_parameters' coordinates as offsets from the anchor, except that a coefficient bounded to (0, 1) — the double sphere's α — moves through its logit, so no step leaves the interval and every fitter (RobustVisualGeometry's fit_lens, a calibration bundle) keeps the model valid without knowing why. action_jacobian(ch, x) is the lens's own transform_deriv_params(lens, x) through that chart. minimal_manifold(lens) is this manifold; restrict it to a target's free coefficients by mask or by name (restrict(ch, (:k1, :k2))).

lens_record(lens) -> Dict{String, Any}

The lens as a record: {"equidistant": [], "radial": [[k₁, …], []]}, {"perspective": [], "add": [{"radial": …}, {"tangential": [p₁, p₂]}]}, {"lift": {"radial": [[], [k₁]]}} — keys are the leaves' tags, arities are array lengths, nothing is named or padded. lens_from_record inverts it exactly: written with shortest round-trip floats, a Float64 lens reads back bitwise identical.
