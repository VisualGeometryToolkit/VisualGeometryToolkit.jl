Calibration API

Camera calibration from planar targets: the 1-D radial camera and its minimal solver, intrinsics from homographies (zhang_intrinsics), and each lens's honest view-angle domain. The iterative calibration built on them — the radial camera's fit, the division-camera focal stack, lens-model regression — lives in VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation. Split from the Cameras API page. See the Cameras guide.

Radial Camera, Intrinsics

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RadialCamera — Type
RadialCamera{T}

The 1-D radial camera of one planar board: the 2×3 block P such that the image point of a plane point X̃ lies on the radial line through center in direction P·X̃ (rows 1–2 of the plane homography taken about that centre, up to scale — invariant to f and to any radially symmetric distortion about it).

The centre is part of the camera, as it is for RadialHomography. A radial camera without one is only half a model: its P predicts a direction, and a direction is not a locus in the image until you know where the pencil is centred. Carrying it here is what lets project return the Line2D the pixel must lie on, and lets constraint_with_jacobians take the RAW pixel — so no caller has to pre-centre its correspondences and keep that convention in its head.

RadialCamera(H) reads it off a HomographyMat (about the origin unless a centre is given); HomographyMat(P) returns the homography with the placeholder third row (0, 0, 1) that the focal solve accepts (it never reads row 3), carrying the centre translation.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RadialCamera — Method
RadialCamera(cam::Camera) -> RadialCamera

The 1-D radial camera of a POSED camera viewing the plane z = 0 of its extrinsics' source frame: rows 1–2 of the induced homography H = K·[r₁ r₂ t], read about K's principal point.

An IDENTITY, not a fit — no correspondences, no least squares, the pose taken exactly as it stands. That is what makes this the safe way to hand a calibrated arm's Extrinsics3 to VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's division_camera_step: re-estimating a radial camera from the same board's correspondences re-runs the metric upgrade's sign decision on a slightly different matrix, and can flip the board's pose.

The lens is ignored by construction, not by omission. A radially symmetric distortion about that same principal point moves an image point along its own ray from the centre, so it cannot change the ray — and the ray is all this model claims (see RadialCamera). That invariance is why a camera with ANY radially symmetric lens is admissible here, while HomographyMat(::PinholeCamera) stays Pinhole-only: the plane map of a distorted camera is not a homography, but its radial camera is exact.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RadialCamera5pt — Type
RadialCamera5pt(; center = Point2(0, 0))

Minimal 5-point radial-camera solver (solve verb over the radial_camera_5pt kernel) on plane_point => raw_pixel correspondences. The pencil's center belongs to the solver, not the data: it is the problem's geometry, carried onto every returned camera. sample_size 5, max_solutions 1; a degenerate sample (collinear basis, dependent equations) gives count == 0.

Deliberately NOT entered in the minimal_solvers registry: generic Cspond{Point2, Point2} data carries no pencil centre (the same reason Homography5ptRadial stays out). Construct it where the centre is known.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.radial_camera_5pt — Method
radial_camera_5pt(pts; center = Point2(0, 0)) -> RadialCamera{Float64} | nothing

The exactly determined radial camera from five plane_point => raw_pixel pairs about center — the minimal solver a RANSAC over board correspondences samples. Closed form and homogeneous, the way homography_4pt is: the first three plane points form a projective basis B, so P·B = [λ₁y₁ λ₂y₂ λ₃y₃] with yⱼ the pixels' ray directions and three unknown scales; the fourth and fifth points, written in that basis (c = B⁻¹X̃), each give one linear equation in λ (their pixel must be parallel to Σⱼ cⱼλⱼyⱼ), and λ is the cross product of the two equation rows. Then P = [λⱼyⱼ]·B⁻¹, unit Frobenius norm. Two 3×3 static solves and a cross product — no SVD, no scale fixed by hand, no root to pick. nothing when the basis points are collinear or the two equations are dependent (the configuration does not determine P).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.zhang_intrinsics — Method
zhang_intrinsics(Hs, pitch::Size2; center, weights = nothing) -> LogicalIntrinsics{Float64}

Known-sensor variant: with the pixel pitch given, the aspect ratio is fixed (fy/fx = pitch.width / pitch.height) and only a single focal length remains. Each homography's two constraints collapse onto one unknown 1/fx², solved by weighted least squares — this stays well-posed even for view sets that cannot separate fx from fy. The metric focal length is fx * pitch.width.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.zhang_intrinsics — Method
zhang_intrinsics(Hs; center, weights = nothing) -> LogicalIntrinsics{Float64}

The calibration from plane→image homographies by Zhang's IAC constraints — one linear least-squares step, assuming zero skew and the principal point at the known center (pixels, required). Each H maps its plane to the caller's pixels; they are read about center internally, and the result is in the caller's pixels: K = [fx 0 cx; 0 fy cy; 0 0 1] with (cx, cy) = center. Each homography contributes two linear equations on (1/fx², 1/fy²); the overdetermined system is solved by (optionally weighted) least squares. weights holds one non-negative vote strength per homography (e.g. inlier counts). Throws ArgumentError when the set is degenerate — all views near fronto-parallel leave the IAC unobservable.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.project — Method
project(P::RadialCamera, X) -> Line2D

The 1-D radial camera's image of the plane point X: the radial LINE through the camera's centre in direction P·X̃. The camera knows where a point's ray points, not how far along it the point lands, so a point of the plane images to a line — and the RAW pixel of X lies on it under any radially symmetric distortion about that centre. constraint_with_jacobians is that statement as a residual.

direction gives the bare P·X̃ for callers working up to scale.
