Generic Camera Models API

Generic camera models: a spline grid of rays over the image, central or noncentral, with its window interface and undistortion. Split from the Cameras API page. See the Cameras guide.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.AdditivePoints — Type
AdditivePoints() <: ControlPointChart

A point control moves additively, p + (Δx, Δy, Δz): 3 coordinates, the identity as its block of retract_jacobian. The default chart of a ControlPatchManifold.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.CentralGenericModel — Type
CentralGenericModel{T} <: AbstractCamera

The central generic camera model: a grid of observation-direction CONTROLS (Matrix{Vec3{T}}, indexed [gy, gx]), interpolated by a uniform cubic B-spline over the calibrated image area (calib_min_x, calib_min_y)– (calib_max_x, calib_max_y) (inclusive integer pixel indices, COLMAP frame). backproject is the normalized spline value; project is the reference implementation's pixel-space LM (started at the calibrated area's center, or at a seed pixel: project(m, X, p₀)).

CentralGenericModel(width, height, min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y, grid;
                    unit_controls = true, nominal = nothing, domain = WholePlane())

unit_controls = true re-normalizes every control at construction (the reference's Read() behavior: its optimizer keeps the controls on the sphere). false keeps them as given: the spline value is normalized by backproject anyway, so a control's norm is a weight in the interpolation, not a gauge — a quasi-interpolated start or an optimizer stepping in each control's tangent plane has controls off the sphere. nominal is a pinhole LogicalIntrinsics the model reports with (the parametric camera it was built from; nominal_intrinsics) — projection never reads it. domain is the region of the sensor (pixels) the camera images — a supplied image circle (a datasheet's image_circle or a measured one), or the WholePlane(); imaged_region(m) is the calibrated area ∩ domain.

CentralGenericModel(source, (width, height); grid_size, calib_area, init, ...)

Initialize FROM a source camera — see the initialization section below: init = :interpolate (default) solves the cubic-prefilter tridiagonal system per axis so the spline INTERPOLATES the source's backprojection at the grid nodes; init = :sample takes each node's sample as its control (Schoenberg's variation-diminishing approximation, which smooths curvature by (h²/6)·f″); init = :quasi_interpolate is the 4th-order local quasi-interpolant on a lattice extended one node past the area (quasi_interpolant), with its border rule for a source whose domain ends near or inside the area. The first two clamp node sampling to where the source can be evaluated — sampling_margin (default 0.97) times its lens model's valid_domain when the lens has a limit, otherwise times its imaging domain — so the global prefilter solve never rings against near-fold curvature or extrapolation past the formula. A model built from a parametric camera does NOT inherit that valid domain as its domain (where the formula holds is not where light falls): its domain is the one supplied, WholePlane() by default; one built from a generic model keeps that model's. The bridge from the parametric zoo into the generic family.

The grid may be any matrix of Vec3{T} (a device array inside a kernel, through Adapt); T is the float type of the controls. Adapt carries the domain to the device only when it is an isbits region; any other (a mask, a region holding arrays) is replaced by WholePlane() on the device copy — the domain is a host-side bookkeeping region (node supports, imaged_region), which no device evaluation reads.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ControlPatchManifold — Type
ControlPatchManifold(directions[, points[, point_chart = AdditivePoints()]]) <: Manifold

A patch of a generic grid model's controls as a manifold: a PX × PY block of its direction controls (an SMatrix of 3-vectors, in the caller's layout: the coordinates follow its column-major order) and, for a non-central model, the matching block of its point controls — the block a per-tile refinement moves (an NLLSsolver variable through the manifold adapter). A direction control d moves on its own sphere by the reference's chart,

‖d‖ · normalize(d + δ₁t₁ + δ₂t₂),   (t₁, t₂) = direction_tangents(d / ‖d‖),

2 coordinates that keep its norm (the norm is a weight of the spline's blend, not a gauge) and whose derivative at δ = 0 is (t₁, t₂). A point control moves by point_chart (a ControlPointChart): additively, 3 coordinates (AdditivePoints), or across its own ray, 2 (RayNormalPoints). The coordinates are per control, column-major over the block: (δ₁, δ₂) each, (δ₁, δ₂, Δx, Δy, Δz) with additive points or (δ₁, δ₂, a, b) with ray-normal points — restrict them by mask to hold some fixed (a pupil-plane model's Δz, with additive points). model(ch) is (; directions, points) (points is nothing for a central patch, whose point_chart is unused); retract_jacobian(ch) is block diagonal, (t₁ t₂) per direction and the point chart's block per point.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ControlPointChart — Type
ControlPointChart

How a ControlPatchManifold moves its point controls: AdditivePoints (3 coordinates per point) or RayNormalPoints (2, across the point's own ray).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.GenericModelWindow — Type
GenericModelWindow

A view onto a rectangular block of a generic grid model's controls: the model's lattice (its image and calibrated area, its FULL grid size) with only the controls of rows offset[1] + 1 : offset[1] + size(directions, 1) and columns offset[2] + 1 : … present, possibly replaced (window(m, rows, cols; directions, points)). Every evaluation of the model's spline that reads only controls inside the block — interpolated_direction, interpolated_point, spline_support, spline_weights — gives the same bits on the window as on a model holding those controls. A pixel whose 4×4 support leaves the block is not imaged by the window: interpolated_direction and interpolated_point are NaN there, spline_support is nothing and spline_weights of such a support is an ArgumentError — never a read of controls outside the block. It is what a per-tile cost block of a refinement evaluates: a few hundred controls instead of the whole grid. Not a camera (it projects nothing); points is nothing for a central model's window.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.NoncentralGenericModel — Type
NoncentralGenericModel{T} <: AbstractCamera

The non-central generic camera model: per-grid-point observation LINES — directions (direction controls, re-normalized at construction unless unit_controls = false) and points (a point on each line), both Matrix{Vec3{T}} indexed [gy, gx] and interpolated by the same uniform cubic B-spline. backproject returns a Ray3; project is the pixel-space LM (_pixel_lm) on the reference's tangent-plane point-to-line residual. With an all-zero points grid the model is exactly central.

The points are plain metres in the camera frame. This is an exception to the units layering that the type forces: its grid of Vec3{T} is interpolated in the inner loop and cannot hold quantities, so the unit is stated here instead. A reader or writer of the model converts at its boundary. A PosedCamera of the model therefore takes physical extrinsics (EuclideanMap(R, t .* m), or with_unit(E, mm) of a map in mm) and projects scene points that are lengths; a plain extrinsics is an ArgumentError, since it would be read in the caller's unit against the grid's metres. ustrip(m, camera) is the plain camera, in metres only.

NoncentralGenericModel(width, height, min_x, min_y, max_x, max_y, directions, points;
                       unit_controls = true, nominal = nothing, domain = WholePlane())
NoncentralGenericModel(source, (width, height); grid_size, calib_area, init, ...)

The keywords as for CentralGenericModel. From a (central) source: directions as the central model's init, points all zero.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.Ray3 — Type
Ray3(origin, direction)

A 3D ray: origin + t·direction, direction unit by contract. The unprojection currency of NoncentralGenericModel — a non-central pixel observes a LINE in the camera frame, not a direction through a common center.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RayNormalPoints — Type
RayNormalPoints() <: ControlPointChart

A point control moves across its own ray, p + a·t₁ + b·t₂ with (t₁, t₂) = direction_tangents(normalize(d)) of the same node's direction control d at the anchor: 2 coordinates, (t₁ t₂) as its block of retract_jacobian, and local_coordinates the offset's dot products with t₁ and t₂. A move along the ray leaves the node's line where it is, so this chart drops the one coordinate of AdditivePoints that does not move the line: local_coordinates of a point moved along its ray is the move's projection onto (t₁, t₂), the component along the ray dropped. The tangents are off the z = 0 plane where the ray is off axis, so a pupil-plane model's points leave their plane under this chart; hold them on it with AdditivePoints and a restrict mask on Δz.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.backproject_with_jacobian — Method
backproject_with_jacobian(m::CentralGenericModel, u) -> (f, M)

The unit bearing f of the pixel u (COLMAP frame) and its back-projection Jacobian M = ∂f/∂u (3×2), the derivative of the normalized spline value — tangent to the sphere (fᵀM = 0) by construction. The parametric cameras' backproject_with_jacobian for the generic model: what puts a bearing residual's threshold in pixels (the essential and rotation problems' M₁/M₂ columns). (NaN, NaN) outside the calibrated area; never throws.

backproject_with_jacobian(m::NoncentralGenericModel, u) -> (f, M)

The unit DIRECTION f of the pixel u's observation line (COLMAP frame) and its Jacobian M = ∂f/∂u (3×2), analytic from the direction spline's derivative weights — tangent to the sphere (fᵀM = 0). The line's point is not differentiated: backproject gives it. (NaN, NaN) outside the calibrated area; never throws.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.cubic_bspline_weights — Method
cubic_bspline_weights(u) -> NTuple{4}

The four weights of a uniform cubic B-spline span at its local coordinate u ∈ [0, 1): the value on the span is w₁c₁ + w₂c₂ + w₃c₃ + w₄c₄ over its four consecutive controls,

((1 − u)³, 3u³ − 6u² + 4, −3u³ + 3u² + 3u + 1, u³) / 6,

a partition of unity. Outside [0, 1) the same cubics continue (extrapolate). Kernel-lawful: in u's own type (a Float32 coordinate gives Float32 weights, a ForwardDiff.Dual dual ones), no allocation and no branch, so a device kernel can call it. Evaluated in Horner form in u, which keeps Float32 within 1e-7 of the exact weights. The weights the generic grid models' windows (spline_window) and ChartSpline read.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.default_generic_grid — Method
default_generic_grid(width, height) -> (gw, gh)

The control grid the reference lays over a width × height pixel area: one node every 64 px plus the three border nodes the cubic window needs (ceil(width / 64) + 3, likewise in y; at least 4). Apply it to the CALIBRATED AREA, not the frame, when the two differ — a circular fisheye's lit field is smaller than its sensor.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.direction_tangents — Method
direction_tangents(d) -> (t1, t2)

The reference's orthonormal tangent basis of the unit direction d — the 2-dof chart a grid control moves in (normalize(d + δ₁t1 + δ₂t2)), and the plane a direction residual is measured in. Generic in the element type: the basis is in d's type (a Float32 direction gives Float32 tangents, a ForwardDiff.Dual direction dual tangents), so a device kernel or a differentiated residual is never promoted.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.generic_lattice — Method
generic_lattice(G, source, size; grid_size, calib_area, nominal, domain) -> G

The layout a generic model of type G (CentralGenericModel or NoncentralGenericModel) built from source over an image of size ((width, height) or a Size2) takes — the same grid_size, calib_area, nominal and domain, with the same defaults, as the constructor from a source — WITHOUT sampling the source: every direction control is the optical axis (0, 0, 1) and every point the origin. What a fit that solves for every control needs (its spline_windows, its nominal pinhole), with none of the node interpolation, which it would discard, and none of its failures on a source the fit itself handles.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.interpolated_direction — Method
interpolated_direction(m, pixel)

The direction spline of a generic grid model or a GenericModelWindow at pixel (COLMAP frame), NOT normalized — backproject is its normalization inside the calibrated area. Outside the area the outermost cell's cubic pieces continue (spline_window) instead of the nothing of backproject. Generic in the element types (Float32 on a device, any control matrix the model holds) and allocation-free. On a window, a pixel whose 4×4 support leaves the window's block is not imaged by that window: the result is NaN (the controls it would read are not the window's).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.quasi_interpolant — Method
quasi_interpolant(samples::AbstractMatrix) -> controls

The separable 4th-order quasi-interpolant of uniform cubic B-splines: node samples f (a matrix of values with + and scalar *, laid out [gy, gx] as a generic model's grid) become controls c_j = (−f_{j−1} + 8 f_j − f_{j+1}) / 6, along x (the second index) first, then along y; at the border the outermost sample is replicated. Exact for cubics at interior nodes, local (each control reads three samples per axis), no linear solve. The stencil of init = :quasi_interpolate, where the samples extend one node past the grid on every side so that no grid control reads a replicated sample.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_support — Method
spline_support(m, pixel) -> (ix, iy) | nothing

The window pixel (COLMAP frame) reads on a generic grid model or a GenericModelWindow: the 4×4 controls grid[iy - 3 + r, ix - 3 + c], r, c ∈ 1:4 (row = y tap, column = x tap) of the FULL grid — the index pair spline_weights takes. nothing outside the calibrated area, and on a window when that support leaves the window's block (the pixel is not imaged by the window).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_value — Method
spline_value(controls, area::Rect2, pixel)

The uniform cubic B-spline surface of controls (any matrix of values with + and scalar *: scalars, 3-vectors) laid over the closed rectangle area as a generic model lays its grid ([gy, gx], cells = size − 3 per axis), at pixel, continued past the area as spline_window. The generic models' evaluation, for a lattice that is not a camera (a per-position aperture radius, a field over the image).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_weights — Method
spline_weights(m, ix, iy, pixel)
    -> (w::SMatrix{4, 4}, wx::SMatrix{4, 4}, wy::SMatrix{4, 4})

The weights of window (ix, iy)'s 16 controls at pixel — w[r, c] multiplies m.grid[iy - 3 + r, ix - 3 + c] — and their derivatives with respect to the pixel's x and y. The window is the caller's: for a pixel that has drifted off the window's own cell the cubic pieces extrapolate smoothly, which is what a refinement holding the support fixed between rebuilds needs. sum(w .* grid_window) is the unnormalized direction backproject normalizes. On a window, a window (ix, iy) whose support leaves the window's block is an ArgumentError.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_window — Method
spline_window(m, pixel) -> (ix, iy, wx, wy)
spline_window(area::Rect2, grid_size, pixel) -> (ix, iy, wx, wy)

The window pixel reads on the lattice of a generic grid model (or a GenericModelWindow), or on the uniform cubic lattice of grid_size = (rows, cols) controls laid over the closed rectangle area (cells = size − 3 per axis, the models' convention): the controls [iy - 3 + r, ix - 3 + c], r, c ∈ 1:4, of the FULL grid and the four weights per axis (wx[c] along x, wy[r] along y; w[r, c] = wy[r] * wx[c], spline_weights' matrix). Outside the area the outermost cell's cubic pieces continue — the same continuation interpolated_direction evaluates.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.undistort — Method
undistort(m::CentralGenericModel, img, Kr::StaticMatrix{3,3}, rect;
          scale = 1, fillvalue = zero(eltype(img))) -> FramedImage

Render img (taken by the generic camera m) undistorted into the TARGET PINHOLE camera Kr: for each output pixel of the window rect (of the target pinhole's frame), the ray Kr⁻¹·ũ is projected through m and the source sampled bilinearly. A generic model has no K/lens split, so the caller names the pinhole it rectifies into — supply the source parametric camera's K to land in the SAME frame as its own undistort, making the panels directly comparable.

Projection has no closed form (the reference's per-point LM), so the undistortion map – the inverse of a smooth spline diffeomorphism, and therefore exactly as smooth as the model itself – is solved only at a coarse grid (oversample x the model's own grid density, warm-started project calls) and bilinearly interpolated to full output resolution before sampling. Verified against a per-pixel LM sweep (retired after verification; see f6e92fa): max positional error 0.19 px at the default oversample = 4 on a 3 MP hallway calibration, O(h^2) in oversample. Pixels whose ray falls outside the calibrated area fill with fillvalue.

Central only: a NON-central pixel observes a line, so its undistortion is depth-dependent (parallax) — there is no single answer per pixel, and no method is provided on purpose.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.project — Method
project(m::CentralGenericModel, rays::AbstractVector{<:StaticVector{3}},
        p₀s::AbstractVector; iters = 10, tol = 1.0e-6) -> (; px, ok)

Batched projection: one LM per ray (at most iters iterations) with the ANALYTIC spline Jacobian, on any KernelAbstractions backend — supply device arrays (rays, seeds, and a device copy of m.grid uploads automatically) and the solve runs on the GPU; host arrays run the identical kernel threaded on the CPU. The backend is the rays' own: every output is allocated beside them. p₀s holds one warm-start pixel per ray (the caller's structure — scanlines, previous frames — is what makes a few iterations enough; see the reference's ProjectWithInitialEstimate). The seeds are positional per-ray data; iters and tol are configuration. Returns pixel positions and a convergence mask (‖residual‖ < tol).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.project — Method
project(m::CentralGenericModel, X) -> SVector{2} | nothing
project(m::CentralGenericModel, X, p₀) -> SVector{2} | nothing

Project the camera-frame point X to a pixel (COLMAP frame): the reference's Levenberg–Marquardt in pixel space, minimizing the mismatch between the pixel's unprojected direction and normalize(X). The two-argument form starts cold, at the calibrated area's center; the three-argument form starts at the seed pixel p₀. The seed is a positional argument because it is per-call data, not configuration (keywords are configuration only; the model leads, then its data). nothing when the point does not project into the calibrated area (or the optimization fails to reach the reference's 1e-12 cost), or when p₀ is not finite.

project(m::NoncentralGenericModel, X) -> SVector{2} | nothing
project(m::NoncentralGenericModel, X, p₀) -> SVector{2} | nothing

Project the camera-frame point X — plain metres, the point grid's unit — to a pixel: LM on the reference's tangent-plane point-to-line residual — the two components of origin(p) − X in the tangent basis of the pixel's line direction — so a converged pixel's observation line goes through X. The two-argument form starts at the calibrated area's center, the three-argument form at the seed pixel p₀; for a non-central model a good seed matters (the reference exposes the same knob). The seed is positional because it is per-call data, not configuration (keywords are configuration only).

A forward map as a spline on a chart

A grid model's project is a small LM per ray. Its forward map can instead be fitted as a SplineProjection: the grid models' lattice as a ChartSpline on the equidistant direction chart, ray → pixel in closed form (VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_camera(SplineProjection, source, region; tolerance)).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ChartSpline — Type
ChartSpline(area::Rect2, controls::AbstractMatrix{<:StaticVector{K}})

A tensor-product uniform cubic B-spline from a 2-D chart to ℝᴷ: the controls laid over the closed rectangle area of the chart as a generic grid model lays its grid over its calibrated area (controls[row, column], the row along the chart's second coordinate, size − 3 cells per axis, so the knot spacing along axis i is widths(area)[i] / (nᵢ − 3)). Evaluated with spline_value, continued past area by the outermost cell's cubic pieces.

A CoordinateTransformations.Transformation: cs(c) is the value at the chart point c, transform_deriv(cs, c) its K × 2 Jacobian (the differentiated cubic pieces, no automatic differentiation), and cs(points) on a vector of chart points evaluates the batch with a KernelAbstractions kernel on the points' own backend (the controls are uploaded beside them).

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.SplineProjection — Type
SplineProjection(chart, spline::ChartSpline{2}, region)

A camera's forward map, ray → pixel, as a spline on a direction chart: the pixel of a ray X is spline(chart(X)). chart is the equidistant direction chart about the optical axis, Lens(Equidistant()) ∘ LinearMap(R) with R a rotation whose third row is the axis (its first two an orthonormal basis of the plane orthogonal to it): a ray at angle θ from the axis lands at radius θ (radians). region is the region of the sensor the map was fitted over, in the pixel frame of the camera it was fitted to (an imaged_region, an Ellipse, a Rect, their ∩). Built by VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_camera(SplineProjection, source, region; tolerance).

A CoordinateTransformations.Transformation: sp(X) is the pixel (the spline continued past its chart area, never nothing), transform_deriv(sp, X) its 2 × 3 Jacobian, so it composes in image_warp chains. project(sp, X) is the camera semantics: nothing when the ray's chart point lies more than one knot cell outside the spline's chart area (where the continuation would extrapolate, and can fold back into the region), or when the pixel does not lie in region (or is not finite).

Its device form (Adapt.adapt(backend, sp)) moves the spline's controls to the device; an isbits region travels with it, any other (one holding arrays) is left behind as WholePlane(), as a grid model's domain is. sp(X) does not read the region, so a warp through it is unchanged.
