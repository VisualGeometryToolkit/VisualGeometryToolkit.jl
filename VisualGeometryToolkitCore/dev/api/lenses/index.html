Lenses API

The lens families — two lens kinds, the projections, the terms and their combinators — the lens contract on rays with everything derived from it, the coefficient chart and record, and the batch surfaces. convert_eltype rebuilds a lens, a LogicalIntrinsics, a CameraModel or a grid model in another float type (Float32 for a GPU launch); it is the conversion the batched and fused kernels apply. See the Lens Families and Back-projection and Undistortion guides.

A distortion term in a projection's frame: a MAP x ↦ f(x) (Radial, Tilt, FieldOfView) or a DELTA x ↦ Δ(x) added to the point (Tangential, ThinPrism) — see Additive. A term is a CoordinateTransformations Transformation, callable on a frame point (t(x), a delta summed with the point, as a lone Additive); terms applied in sequence are its ∘, D₂ ∘ D₁ (D₁ first).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.AbstractLensModel — Type
AbstractLensModel

Base type for all lens models.

A lens model determines how 3D rays through the optical center map to points on the sensor, including both the projection geometry and any aberrations.

Interface — the lens contract is on rays

A lens is a map between RAYS and NORMALIZED image points, and the pair project(lens, X) (a ray → its image point) and backproject(lens, xd) (an image point → its unit ray) is the whole contract (geometry/cameras/lens_rays.jl): NaN where the lens does not image, generic in the number type, allocation-free. There are two kinds of lens: a forward Lens — a projection onto a frame followed by a distortion in that frame — and a backward LiftLens, whose image point lifts to its ray in closed form (lenses.jl). Everything else is derived from the pair, for every lens: transform_deriv(lens, X), transform_deriv_params(lens, X), project_with_jacobians, the radial profile (projected_radius / view_angle), and the perspective views distort_normalized / undistort_normalized with the functor lens(x).

A lens is a CoordinateTransformations.Transformation, so its derivative is that package's transform_deriv(lens, x) (ForwardDiff through the perspective view), and its back-projection is a map too: inv(lens) is an InverseLens whose call is undistort_normalized and whose transform_deriv is ForwardDiff through it. Compositions inherit both — transform_deriv(K ∘ inv(lens) ∘ K⁻¹, x) is the chain rule. The other generic defaults are imaging_transform(intrinsics, lens) and the capability trait has_distortion(lens). Every lens model here is a central projection (single center of projection); finite-aperture models are downstream.

Subtyping CoordinateTransformations.Transformation makes every imaging chain a ComposedTransformation regardless of lens (the Pinhole and distorted paths used to produce DIFFERENT composition types, breaking structural pattern-matches like pnp_pixel_whitening).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.DoubleSphere — Type
DoubleSphere(xi, alpha) <: AbstractProjection

Usenko, Demmel & Cremers' double-sphere projection (3DV 2018): with d₁ = ‖X‖, z_s = ξ·d₁ + X₃ and d₂ = √(X₁² + X₂² + z_s²), x = X₁₂ / (α·d₂ + (1 − α)·z_s). ξ = α = 0 is the perspective projection. It images the rays out to the fold (or pole) of its radial profile, in closed form — not the paper's conservative field-of-view bound — and the closed-form inverse has the same domain. The coefficients' domain is ξ > −1, α ∈ [0, 1] (parameter_bounds), which LensManifold keeps.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.Lens — Type
Lens(projection[, distortion]) <: AbstractLensModel

A forward lens: the ray is projected onto projection's frame and the frame point is distorted by distortion (nothing, a term, an Additive or terms in sequence, CoordinateTransformations' D₂ ∘ D₁ — each stage a map term or an Additive). Projection is closed form; back-projection inverts the distortion (backproject).

A composed distortion is stored right-associated, whatever association it was built in: Lens(p, D₃ ∘ D₂ ∘ D₁) (Base folds ∘ to the left) and Lens(p, D₃ ∘ (D₂ ∘ D₁)) are one value, with distortion the latter, the form lens_from_record builds.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.Radial — Type
Radial{B, P, Q, T}(k::NTuple{P}, d::NTuple{Q})
Radial(k, d = ())                  # B = Even
Radial{Full}(k, d)

The radial factor F(ρ) = N(ρ)/D(ρ) with N = 1 + Σ kᵢ φᵢ(ρ) and D = 1 + Σ dⱼ φⱼ(ρ), where the basis B sets φᵢ: Even — ρ²ⁱ; Full — ρⁱ⁺¹ (no linear term: the factor is smooth on the axis).

In a Lens it is the map x ↦ F(|x|)·x in the projection's frame — OpenCV's rational radial factor (k₁…k₃ / k₄…k₆) in the perspective frame, Kannala–Brandt's θ-polynomial in the equidistant frame. In a LiftLens it is the undistortion of the image point, x_u = F(ρ_d)·x_d, represented as the ray (x_d·N, D) — the division model, BabelCalib's rational model, Scaramuzza's polynomial.

Coefficient names: k1…kP then d1…dQ.

The constructor also stores where the factor's imaged branch ends, in the basis variable u (ρ² for Even): u_fold, the first positive root of the profile's derivative (the fold), and u_end, the smaller of the fold and the pole (the first positive root of D); Inf when there is none. They are not parameters, and a per-point branch test is one comparison against them; every other reader of the branch's end (monotone_radius, view_angle_limit, the inverses' brackets) reads them too.

The ends follow from the coefficients in the precision they were built in. A WIDENING rebuild — convert_eltype to a wider type, the Duals of a parameter Jacobian and their primal — casts them instead of sweeping again, so a Float32 factor seen at Float64 keeps its own branch (and _primal of a Float64 differentiation of a Float32 lens is _precision(Float64, lens)); such a factor can differ from one constructed at Float64 from the same coefficients by the narrow precision's rounding of the end. A narrowing conversion recomputes them. An integer factor is not a supported stored form: the outer constructors and every rebuild float their coefficients, and only a direct call of the inner Radial{B, P, Q, <:Integer} reaches one; it constructs (the ends stored rounded down, typemax for Inf) but is internal only — the lens contract and the branch readers (_branch_end) take float factors.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.monotone_radius — Method
monotone_radius(lens) -> Real

The distorted radius up to which the lens's perspective view is a monotone bijection — the bound a whole-extent perspective undistortion clips to (undistorted_rect, valid_rect, division_bounded). Beyond it the view folds or diverges and project answers NaN. The profile's first fold or pole, whichever comes first, mapped to the distorted radius; both are found exactly, as first positive polynomial roots. Inf for a lens whose profile never folds. A point EXACTLY at this radius can be NaN (it rounds onto the closed end of the branch); a caller that needs a finite value steps inside, as the rect builders' margin does.

  • the radial factor (radial) of a forward lens: the fold or pole of its profile r ↦ r·F(r²) in the projection's frame, capped at the frame's edge (a ray cannot leave the sphere); decentering terms do not enter, as in view_angle_limit.
  • a LiftLens: the first pole of D (the 90° circle) or fold of the lift — 1/√|k| for Division(k).
  • a composed distortion (CoordinateTransformations' ∘ of stages): its FIRST radial stage (a Radial, or an Additive led by one), wherever it sits — the stage project tests — read as above at that stage's output; the stages around it do not enter (for a stage behind a tilt, the frame's edge caps the stage's input).
  • a SplineLens: the largest normalized radius about x = 0 (its gauge's principal point, the pixel imaging ẑ) whose disc lies inside the lens's support — its backward area and the lattice cells whose 4 × 4 window holds present nodes only, where the grid is defined (and, for a calibrated grid, one-to-one); 0 when x = 0 is outside it.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.view_angle_limit — Method
view_angle_limit(lens; cap) -> θ_max

The largest view angle the lens's radial profile honestly represents: where the profile stops being finite and strictly increasing (a fold, a pole, or the frame's own edge). Closed forms where the lens has one — the fold or pole of a forward lens's radial factor read through its frame, never past the frame's edge (π/2 − 10⁻³ for the perspective frame); a lift's fold; the double sphere's FOV bound — and a monotonicity scan of projected_radius otherwise. A composed distortion (∘) with a radial stage is bounded by that stage, tested at its own input as project tests it: the angle where project first turns NaN, bisected to rounding. When a stage precedes the radial one (a tilt) that angle depends on the azimuth, and the limit is its minimum over azimuths, so every ray within it is imaged; a lens whose bound is radially symmetric reads it at azimuth 0. cap bounds the answer: the generic scan runs to it (default π − 10⁻³); the closed forms clamp to it (default none). A ray projected EXACTLY at the limit can be NaN (a lift's root there is √eps-conditioned); a caller that needs a finite value steps inside, as the valid domain's inscribed radius and the spline lattice's sampler do.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.Cylindrical — Type
Cylindrical()

The central cylindrical projection of directions onto the unit cylinder about the up axis, in the camera frame's axes (x right, y down, z forward, up = −y): the longitude about the axis, and the height h = tan φ at which the ray through the origin meets the cylinder of radius 1 (φ the latitude). A cylindrical panorama raster is an affine map of (λ, h): its columns are the longitude, its rows the height, so verticals stay vertical and straight.

  • project(Cylindrical(), X) — the direction X (any 3-vector off the up axis; its length does not enter) to SVector(λ, h): the longitude λ = atan(X₁, X₃) ∈ (−π, π] (0 forward, +π/2 right) and h = −X₂ / √(X₁² + X₃²) (+ up). NaN for a zero, non-finite or polar (X₁ = X₃ = 0) vector: the poles have no height.
  • backproject(Cylindrical(), p) — p = (λ, h) to the unit direction (sin λ, −h, cos λ) / √(1 + h²), defined for every finite p (periodic in λ).

Its longitude is Equirectangular's, and h = tan φ of its latitude. An AbstractPanoramaProjection.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.CylindricalEqualArea — Type
CylindricalEqualArea()

Lambert's cylindrical equal-area projection of directions, in the camera frame's axes (x right, y down, z forward, up = −y): the longitude and v = sin φ of the latitude — the height at which the ray's parallel meets the unit cylinder when projected ALONG the planes of constant height (orthogonally onto the axis' cylinder), so equal areas of the raster are equal solid angles (dΩ = cos φ dφ dλ = dv dλ): a uniform raster's pixels all subtend the same solid angle.

  • project(CylindricalEqualArea(), X) — X (any nonzero 3-vector; its length does not enter) to SVector(λ, v), λ = atan(X₁, X₃) and v = −X₂ / ‖X‖ ∈ [−1, 1]. NaN for a zero or non-finite vector; the poles are v = ±1 with atan's longitude.
  • backproject(CylindricalEqualArea(), p) — p = (λ, v) to the unit direction (√(1 − v²) sin λ, −v, √(1 − v²) cos λ); NaN for |v| > 1.

An AbstractPanoramaProjection.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.Equirectangular — Type
Equirectangular()

The equirectangular (latitude/longitude) projection of directions, in the camera frame's axes (x right, y down, z forward, up = −y).

  • project(Equirectangular(), X) — the direction X (any nonzero 3-vector; its length does not enter) to SVector(λ, φ) in radians: the longitude λ = atan(X₁, X₃) ∈ (−π, π] (0 forward, +π/2 right) and the latitude φ = atan(−X₂, √(X₁² + X₃²)) ∈ [−π/2, π/2] (+π/2 up). VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.image_circle — Method
image_circle(camera, sensor::Sensor, diameter::Quantity) -> Ellipse{Float64}
image_circle(camera::BoundedCamera, diameter::Quantity) -> Ellipse{Float64}

The image circle of a lens: the region of the sensor it lights, from a lens datasheet's image-circle diameter, a length, carried into pixels by the sensor's pitch — semi-axes diameter / (2 · pitch.width) and diameter / (2 · pitch.height) px about the principal point, axis-aligned, in the camera's pixel frame (the frame of its calibration matrix K; Rect(extent) is the COLMAP-convention extent of the same frame). The datasheet circle is physical, on the sensor plane, and the pitch defines the pixel axes, so K's skew and focal lengths do not enter. A DimensionError when diameter is not a length. A BoundedCamera bounded by its physical Sensor takes the two-argument form; one bounded by a bare extent is an ArgumentError (it has no pitch).

The image circle is never predicted from the lens model: where the model's formula is valid is valid_domain, a different region. A measured circle (an Ellipse segmented from images) serves wherever this one does. A SplineLens camera's circle is centred on its gauge principal point, the pixel imaging ẑ. Where the camera images is imaged_region(camera).

cam = BoundedCamera(CameraModel(K; lens = KannalaBrandt(-0.135)), sensor("Sony", "IMX250"))
q = image_circle(cam, 6.0mm)                # from the datasheet, through the 3.45 μm pitch
Rect(extent(cam)) ∈ q                       # is the whole sensor lit?

imaged_region(camera::BoundedCamera) -> region

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.valid_domain — Method
valid_domain(camera) -> Union{Ellipse{Float64}, WholePlane}

Where the camera's lens MODEL is valid, in pixels in the camera's own pixel frame (the frame of its calibration matrix K): the image of the cone of view angles θ ≤ θ_max =view_angle_limit(lens), on which the lens's radial profile is finite and strictly increasing, so the model is radially injective. The conic K⁻ᵀ · diag(1, 1, −ρ²) · K⁻¹ with ρ the lens's image radius at θ_max (projected_radius for a radially symmetric lens): an Ellipse centred at the principal point, a circle (equal axes) only when K is isotropic without skew and the pitch square.

This is a property of the formula, not where light falls on the sensor: a fitted lens's fold can lie well outside the lit disc, or inside the extent where the sensor is dark. Where light falls is the lens's image_circle(camera, sensor, diameter) from its datasheet, or a measured circle; a bounded camera holds either as its lens_coverage.

For a lens that is not radially symmetric — a composed distortion (∘) with a tilt stage, tangential or thin-prism terms — ρ is the smallest image radius of the θ_max cone over the azimuths (360 samples, the least refined by golden section) less a relative margin of 10⁻⁹, so the ellipse is INSCRIBED: every point inside it is the image of a ray inside the cone.

The WholePlane when the lens has no limit of its own: view_angle_limit reports only the edge it stops at by construction — the perspective or orthographic chart's edge, or the sphere's π − 10⁻³ (an undistorted equidistant lens, a lens whose profile stays monotone, a lift that never folds). That edge is a cap, not the lens: a conic there would measure the cap constant. A lens WITH a limit — a fold, a pole, the double sphere's bound, a chain's bounded stage — always gets its conic, even one larger than the extent. The rule is generic over AbstractLensModel: any lens with a view_angle_limit (every lens has one, by the generic scan) and a project gets an answer. A PosedCamera or BoundedCamera forwards to its model.

Related radii, different quantities: horizon_radius is fx times monotone_radius, the radial factor's perspective-monotone bound (decentering ignored, fx only, Inf without a fold) — equal to this ellipse's radius for an isotropic, skew-free K and a radially symmetric folding lens; the imaging domain (where backproject turns NaN) may be wider than a whole-plane answer admits, e.g. r ≤ π·f for an undistorted equidistant lens.

Rect(extent(cam)) ∈ v                       # is the model valid over the whole sensor?

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.backproject — Method
backproject(lens::AbstractLensModel, xd::StaticVector{2}) -> Vec3

The default backproject of a lens that defines only project: Newton's method on project(lens, X(m)) = x_d over the equidistant chart m = θ·(cos φ, sin φ) of the ray X(m) = (sin θ·m̂, cos θ) — so view angles past 90° are reached — started at the pinhole ray's chart point atan(|x_d|)·x̂_d, each step capped at 0.3 rad, a fixed 30 trips (kernel-safe: no throw, no allocation), the 2 × 2 Jacobian by forward-mode differentiation of project. The unit ray, or NaN unless the iterate converged (the residual within the solve tolerance of its precision), the forward map keeps orientation there (det J > 0) and θ < π. Every lens of this package has its own inverse (closed form, or a solver that follows the imaged branch), which takes precedence; this one is for a downstream lens type, and agrees with each of them in its domain (tested to 95% of each lens's view_angle_limit; within a few percent of a fold Newton converges too slowly and the answer is NaN).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.backproject — Method
backproject(lens, xd::StaticVector{2}) -> Vec3

The UNIT ray behind the normalized image point xd — the other half of the lens contract, with project(lens, X). NaN where the lens does not image the point (outside its frame's domain, past a fold, a non-converged inverse), never a throw.

Where the in-plane distortion folds, a point has several preimages, and the answer is the one on the imaged branch: det J > 0 there and every Radial factor before its fold, reached from the axis. Two consequences:

  • project tests only the radial factors, so a ray past a fold of the deltas of an Additive or of a composition's Tilt projects to a finite point whose backproject is the imaged branch's preimage (a different ray), or NaN — never that ray.
  • The acceptance test is pointwise. Where the map overlaps itself INSIDE the axis-connected component, a point has several preimages on the branch and backproject returns the first the solver reaches — rare (0.05 % of the interior points of deliberately strong OpenCV chains), but an undistortion map built from backproject may jump between preimages from one pixel to the next there. Whether a lens is injective over a sensor is a property of the LENS, for an optional check over the sensor rectangle, not something tested per pixel.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.project — Method
project(lens, X::StaticVector{3}) -> Vec2

The NORMALIZED image point of the ray through X (any positive scale) — half of the lens contract, with backproject(lens, xd). NaN where the lens does not image the ray, never a throw; allocation-free and generic in the number type (Float32 kernels, ForwardDiff Duals).

A Lens projects the ray onto its projection's frame and distorts the frame point there; no perspective hop is involved unless the projection IS the perspective one, whose domain genuinely ends at 90°. A LiftLens solves the image radius as a root on the ray.

A ray past the fold (or pole) of a radial factor is NaN: every Radial factor is tested at its own input — a lone one, an Additive's head, each radial stage of a composed distortion (∘). The other folds are NOT tested forward, since that would cost a Jacobian per projected point: where the deltas of an Additive (tangential, thin prism) or a composition's Tilt fold the map (det J ≤ 0 inside the radial factor's disk), project returns a finite point, and backproject of that point is not the ray — it is the imaged branch's preimage, a different ray, or NaN where the branch does not reach the point. So the round trip backproject(lens, project(lens, X)) ≈ X / ‖X‖ holds on the imaged branch (the region connected to the axis where det J > 0 and every radial factor is before its fold), not past such a fold.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ParameterManifold — Type
ParameterManifold(x) <: Manifold

The manifold of a model x with the parameter protocol — a lens, a LogicalIntrinsics, a parametric CameraModel (intrinsics then lens) — anchored at x: parameter_vector's coordinates, each moved by a chart that keeps it inside its parameter_bounds, the one statement of the model's domain:

bound (open ends)chart coordinate v of the value t (anchor t₀)
none finitet = t₀ + v
at = a + (t₀ − a)·exp(v)
bt = b − (b − t₀)·exp(−v)
a and bt = a + (b − a)·logistic(logit((t₀ − a)/(b − a)) + v)

and a CLOSED endpoint — a value the model takes, such as α = 0 or ω = 0 (parameter_bounds lists them) — is a mirror: the open ends' chart (the plain t₀ + v when it has none) reflected at it, so a value past it folds back inside (with two closed ends, a fold of period 2(b − a)) and the slope keeps its magnitude with its sign flipped per reflection — never the flat residual of a clamp, at which a fixed-chart LM that overshot the end would stall. In range the value is the plain chart's, bit for bit. So no step leaves the domain and every fitter (VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's fit_camera, a calibration bundle) keeps the model valid without knowing why, and an anchor at a closed endpoint moves with slope 1, not from the far end of a logit. An anchor on (or past) an OPEN end is moved inside it by a margin of 10⁻⁶ of the interval (of max(1, |a|) for a one-sided bound); an anchor past a closed end is clamped onto it; a mirrored coordinate (ω, whose sign is not part of the map) is anchored at |ω|. ω = 0 is a stationary point of the field-of-view model itself — its map is even in ω, so ∂project/∂ω = 0 there whatever the chart — so a fit of ω starts at a small ω ≠ 0. A zero chart offset is the anchor's value bit for bit, so a coordinate restrict holds fixed never drifts across recenters. restrict it to the free coefficients by mask or by name (restrict(ch, (:k1, :k2)); a camera's restrict(ch, (:f, :cx, :cy, :k1)) fixes its skew and aspect). minimal_manifold(lens) and minimal_manifold(camera) are this manifold. action_jacobian(ch, x) is the model's parameter Jacobian of its action on x through the chart: the lens's transform_deriv_params(lens, x), a camera's ∂project(camera, X)/∂θ (ForwardDiff).

LensManifold is its name for a lens.

A solver's box

ParameterManifold(x; box) adds an optimizer's search box — the solver option parameter_bounds leaves out (a prior range such as a focal length between 0.1 and 50, not a model fact): a NamedTuple of (lo, hi) intervals keyed by parameter_names, a name not listed unboxed. The box is intersected with the domain and acts at the step: recenter clamps the stepped free parameters into it (a coordinate restrict holds fixed is never clamped), so an NLLSsolver variable on the manifold (or a restriction of it) never leaves it; retract is the chart's alone. The anchor is taken as given. parameter_box reads the intersected box.

A coordinate's scale

ParameterManifold(x; scale) scales coordinates — a NamedTuple of positive factors keyed by parameter_names, a name not listed 1: the coordinate v of a parameter with scale s moves its chart by s·v (t = t₀ + s·v unbounded, t = a + (t₀ − a)·exp(s·v) bounded below, …), so retract_jacobian carries the factor and local_coordinates divides by it. The manifold is the same set; only the units of its coordinates change. An optimizer that damps every coordinate alike (λ·I) needs them comparable: a pixel camera's principal point (pixels, with scale = (; cx = W, cy = H) one coordinate per image width) next to its log-charted focal length and its unit-free lens coefficients. coordinate_scale reads it.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.parameter_vector — Function
parameter_vector(x) -> SVector

The coefficients of a model x — a lens, a calibratable target or its calibration, or a LogicalIntrinsics — in the order of parameter_names. Inverse of with_parameters.

A lens's are the projection's, then the distortion's terms depth-first, left to right — for terms in sequence, D₂ ∘ D₁, CoordinateTransformations' order, the outer D₂'s first — each term in its own documented order (Radial: k…, d…; Tangential: p1, p2; ThinPrism: sx…, sy…; …); empty for a lens without coefficients. A PlanarTarget's are its calibration's (empty for Rigid). A LogicalIntrinsics's are f, aspect, skew, cx, cy of K = [f s cx; 0 f·aspect cy; 0 0 1], absolute and in pixels.

What a calibration is for: an intrinsics kind (the type parameter I: metric LogicalIntrinsics, or the projective ProjectiveIntrinsics of a partial calibration) and a LensSpec or a SplineLensSpec (the field model). The spline form is always metric.

toml_value(spec) is the table Dict("intrinsics" => "metric" | "projective", "lens" => …, …) (a LensSpec table plus intrinsics) or Dict("type" => "spline", "grid_size" => […], "calib_area" => […]) with the unset keys left out; CameraModelSpec(table) reads it back, and intrinsics defaults to "metric" when absent (a spline table may also name it, as "metric" only). == compares the specification.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.LensSpec — Type
LensSpec(family::Symbol; fixed = (), structure...)
LensSpec(record::AbstractDict)
LensSpec(lens::AbstractLensModel)

A lens model specified by family name and term counts, not by a record of zeros: the lens a calibrator starts from (see lens(::LensSpec)) and the coefficients it moves (free_names).

The families and their structure keywords:

  • :division, :kannala_brandt: coefficients (an integer ≥ 1), the d1…dn of Division, the k1…kn of KannalaBrandt;
  • :brown_conrady: radial (≥ 1) and tangential (a Bool, default false), the k1…kn, p1, p2 of OpenCV's perspective model;
  • :rational: numerator (≥ 0) and denominator (≥ 1), OpenCV's rational radial factor k1…k(numerator) over d1…d(denominator);
  • :perspective, :equidistant, :stereographic, :equisolid, :orthographic, :double_sphere, :unified, :extended_unified: a projection with no distortion, no keywords.

The reference values of a family are those of the member that equals its base projection: all-zero coefficients for the polynomial families, and for the parametric projections the parameters that reduce them to perspective (DoubleSphere(0, 0), Unified(0), ExtendedUnified(0, 1)).

fixed lists the coefficients a calibrator HOLDS (the stack's one parameter-holding keyword), each by parameter_names of the lens or by a group (:k for k1…; free_parameter_names); the default () moves all of them, and free_names is the complement. free is refused, naming fixed.

The record form, LensSpec(Dict("record" => lens_record(lens))), covers every composition the table does not name, and is the only form with numbers: a lens with non-zero starting or held coefficients. LensSpec(lens) is the inverse of lens(spec): the named form only when the lens's structure has a name AND its coefficients are the reference values (compared whatever their element type, so a Float32 lens at the reference values is named; a -0.0 is not the reference 0.0), else the record form, so no value is ever dropped.

toml_value(spec) is Dict("lens" => "division", "coefficients" => 3, …) or Dict("lens" => Dict("record" => …)), plus "fixed" when not empty; LensSpec(::AbstractDict) reads it back (the keys of the table the spec sits in). == compares the specification, not the lens.

Throws an ArgumentError for an unknown family (listing the known ones), a keyword the family does not take or lacks, a count out of range, a fixed name that is not a parameter name of the lens, a retired free, and a record that is not a lens record.
