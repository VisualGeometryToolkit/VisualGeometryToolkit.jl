Camera RAW API

The sensor image: a decoded Bayer RAW as RawMosaic (an AbstractMatrix{UInt16} of its photosites that knows its colour phase, its levels and where it sits on the sensor), its photosites channel-first as a method of ImageCore's channelview (a view, on the mosaic's device), and LinearRaw, its exact linear values on the 2×2 cells, with the maps of every plane and channel onto the sensor. Decoding a file is VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' (load_raw_mosaic, LibRaw), and so is the display demosaic. A cell mode's frame map is also read without an image, raw_frame_map(LinearRaw{:green_cells}), and its provenance record, raw_provenance(LinearRaw{:green_cells}), for a frame with no RAW behind it. See the Raw images guide.

The mosaic and its photosite views

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.BayerCellFrame — Type
BayerCellFrame{C} <: Frame{2}

The COLMAP pixel frame of one colour plane C of the half-resolution grid of 2×2 Bayer cells: pixel (row, column) of the plane is centred at (column − ½, row − ½) of its frame. The planes are indexed alike but sampled at different points of the cell, so each is a frame of its own:

  • :R, :B — a cell's red and blue photosite (a plane of a :bin2 LinearRaw, a channel of a mosaic's channelview);
  • :G1, :G2 — a cell's two green photosites, in the cell's column-major order (the two green channels of channelview(m));
  • :G — the mean of the two greens, which sits at the cell centre in every phase: the cell grid itself (the :green_cells image), whose map is raw_frame_map.

bayer_plane_map maps each onto the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}, and a composition that joins one plane's frame to another's is refused (MethodError), as at every frame junction of Core.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.LinearRawView — Type
LinearRawView{T, A, D, L} <: AbstractMatrix{T}

The lazy linear view of a mosaic's photosites on the raw grid, linearview(m): element (i, j) is the linear value of the held array's code there, with its channel's black level and span, computed when read. Under a sensor model whose noise has a variance law (law: a PoissonGaussian or a RelativeNoise) the element is an Uncertain{Float64}, the value with its scalar variance computed on the same read (owner ruling D14: the default uncertain image is this lazy one; copy materialises it as a StructArray); otherwise T is Float64. It holds the photosites (parent, the mosaic's held array, a device array too), the four channels' levels and, from a sensor model's Dark, the offset map over the visible raw (dark, on the same device; nothing without one) read at the mosaic's window_origin; similar, a broadcast and copy are the held array's kind (a broadcast over a device view runs on the device), adapt moves it, and a KernelAbstractions kernel reads it as it is (its indexing is two parities, the one black rule and one linearisation).

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.PhotositeColors — Type
PhotositeColors <: AbstractMatrix{UInt8}

The colour index of every photosite of a mosaic, lazily, as an isbits value: the size, the colour pattern (at most 4 × 4, in an NTuple) and its phase at the mosaic's first photosite. Indexing is the one phase rule; nothing is stored per photosite, so it is the same on the host and inside a GPU kernel or a fused broadcast with a device mosaic's arrays (it holds no array, and a device array in the same broadcast decides where it runs).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.RawMosaic — Type
RawMosaic{A <: AbstractMatrix{UInt16}, M <: NamedTuple} <: AbstractMatrix{UInt16}

A RAW file decoded and nothing more: the visible area of the sensor, as the decoder reports it (VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' load_raw_mosaic, LibRaw). It IS its photosite matrix: size, indexing and parent are the held array's, so an ordinary array or image function reads it unchanged, and what such a function makes (similar, copy, a broadcast, a reduction) is a plain array of the held array's kind — the colour pattern and the metadata stay with the mosaic. copy, ==, isequal and isapprox are the held array's own (no element read through the wrapper, so a device mosaic compares on the device), and equality is the photosite matrix's: two mosaics with the same photosites are == whatever their colour pattern, window origin or metadata (compare photosite_colors, window_origin and metadata for those), and a mosaic is == to its held array. It is read-only (no setindex!): write to parent(m). The held array may be a device array (adapt(CuArray, m), or adapt(backend, m) with a KernelAbstractions backend, moves the photosites and keeps the rest); a kernel receives parent(m), never the wrapper (nor, on a GPU, the nested view channelview(m): read its photosites by its index rule).

A unit-range index m[rows, columns] (a copy) or view(m, rows, columns) (no copy) is a window: a mosaic of its own whose colours are the parent's at those photosites (the colour phase shifts with an odd start, as a circshift of the pattern would) and whose raw_frame_map is its offset into the visible raw. Any other index is a plain array.

  • raw::A — the photosites, raw[row, column], in the sensor's orientation (no EXIF flip): pixel (row, column) is the raw's COLMAP pixel centred at (column − ½, row − ½) — the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}, the frame every calibration of the raw is in (a window's pixels are offset by window_origin).
  • pattern::Matrix{UInt8} — the smallest repeating colour pattern, square, at most 4 × 4, from the full raw frame's origin (LibRaw's COLOR read from there), in LibRaw's colour indices: 0 R, 1 G, 2 B, 3 the second G of an RGBG pattern. Each photosite's index is photosite_colors(mosaic), the pattern tiled from that origin.
  • black::NTuple{4, Float64} — the black level per colour index (the common and per-channel parts summed, LibRaw's adjust_bl).
  • white::Float64 — the white level (LibRaw's maximum): the code at which a photosite is saturated.
  • visible_origin::NTuple{2, Int} — (top_margin, left_margin), the visible area's offset into the full raw frame, which is raw_size ((height, width)).
  • metadata::NamedTuple — what the decoder read: the EXIF fields (VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures types them: exposure_settings, lens_settings, body_identity, lens_identity, capture_time), the daylight white balance, the camera's declared linear-response limit, the decoder and its library's version. Core reads none of it except in raw_provenance.
  • window_origin::NTuple{2, Int} — (rows, columns), the offset of raw[1, 1] from the visible area's first photosite: (0, 0) as decoded (the keyword of the constructor), a window's start less one otherwise.

ImageCore.channelview — Method
channelview(m::RawMosaic) -> AbstractArray{UInt16, 3}

ImageCore's channelview, for a Bayer mosaic: the photosites channel-first, 4 × H ÷ 2 × W ÷ 2, without a copy. The Bayer interpretation of "channels": each 2×2 cell is a pixel and its four photosites — spatially interleaved, each at its own position on the sensor — are its channels, so v[k, i, j] is photosite k of cell (i, j), the cell's photosites in column-major order (k = di + 2dj + 1 for photosite (2i − 1 + di, 2j − 1 + dj)): the mosaic's own phase order, whose colours are bayer_channels(m) (R, G1, G2, B for an RGGB cell; another phase permutes them, since reordering channels is not a view). It is built as ImageCore builds a view: a reshape of the photosite matrix (H × W → 2 × H/2 × 2 × W/2), its dimensions permuted (→ 2 × 2 × H/2 × W/2, a PermutedDimsArray) and reshaped (→ 4 × H/2 × W/2), views of parent(m): writing through it writes the mosaic's held array, a device mosaic's view is a view of its device array (KernelAbstractions' get_backend is the device's, adapt(Array, v) moves it whole), and a broadcast or a Cartesian copy over a whole mosaic's view runs on the device. An odd last row or column (no 2×2 cell) is left out, as in LinearRaw.

In a GPU kernel read the photosites by the view's index rule instead, parent(m)[2i − 1 + di, 2j − 1 + dj] (or the 4-D parent(v)[di + 1, dj + 1, i, j]): Base's ReshapedArray indexing compiles in a CUDA kernel only over a whole contiguous array, at about a third of the held matrix's bandwidth (509 against 1391 GB/s on a B200, 2848 × 4256), and not at all over a window or an odd-sized mosaic (an InvalidIRError); benchmark/raw_channelview.jl measures it, and LinearRaw's cell kernel reads the held matrix.

A plain AbstractMatrix{UInt16} keeps ImageCore's own method (a gray image's channel view is the array itself): this method is the mosaic's, whose phase says which photosite is which. Channel k's pixel frame is BayerCellFrame{C} (C its colour), and bayer_plane_map(m, k) maps it onto the visible raw, exactly, for every Bayer phase and either parity of a window's origin. Its inverse is photositeview. The linear values channel-first are channelview(linearview(m)).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.bayer_channels — Method
bayer_channels(m::RawMosaic) -> NTuple{4, Symbol}

The colour of each channel of channelview(m): :R, :G1, :G2, :B, in the channels' order, the cell's photosites column-major ((0, 0), (1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 1) from the cell's top-left). (:R, :G1, :G2, :B) for an RGGB cell, (:G1, :B, :R, :G2) for GRBG; the greens are numbered in that order. The cell's phase is the mosaic's own at its first photosite, so a window with an odd start reads another phase (from the same pattern, the one phase rule of photosite_colors). A pattern that is not a 2×2 Bayer cell (one R, two G, one B) is an ArgumentError.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.bayer_plane_map — Method
bayer_plane_map(m::RawMosaic, k::Integer) -> SimilarityMap{2, …, BayerCellFrame{C}, PixelFrame{:colmap}}

The map of channel k of channelview(m) onto the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}: pixel (i, j) of the channel, at (j − ½, i − ½) of its frame BayerCellFrame{C} (C = bayer_channels(m)[k]), goes onto its photosite's centre, (2j − 1 + dj − ½, 2i − 1 + di − ½) plus a window's offset, exactly, for every Bayer phase and either parity of the visible or window origin. It is the cell grid's map x ↦ 2x after the photosite's translation from the cell centre ((±¼, ±¼) cell pixels): the rule the planes of a LinearRaw share, so the R channel's map IS the R plane's of the mosaic's :bin2 image. An isbits value, usable inside a kernel. A channel past the fourth is an ArgumentError.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.linearview — Method
linearview(m::RawMosaic; sensor_model::SensorModel = SensorModel()) -> LinearRawView

The mosaic's linear values on the raw grid, lazily, as ImageCore's normedview reads codes as numbers without a copy: element (i, j) is (raw − black[c]) / (white − black[c]) of photosite (i, j), c its colour — the one linearisation, the one LinearRaw's cell kernel applies. Not clipped, no gain. It composes with ImageCore's channelview: channelview(linearview(m)) is the linear values channel-first (4 × H/2 × W/2, the channels of channelview(m)), so the green-cell value of cell (i, j) is the mean of its two green channels, bit for bit (LinearRaw(m; mode = :green_cells).image[i, j, 1], at gain 1). An odd last row or column is in the view (its photosites' colours by the same rule) and left out of the channel view.

Not normedview(N0f16, …) itself, whose code / 65535 ignores the black levels, nor a MappedArrays.mappedarray of the codes, whose function sees the value alone: the black level and span depend on the photosite's position (its colour in the phase), so the view reads its index — one parity rule, the cell sites' (k = di + 2dj + 1).

sensor_model, the frame's SensorModel, sets the levels as it does LinearRaw's, by the same resolution: its black slot's per-colour-index black (the span white − black[c]), and a Dark's offset in place of the black per photosite. Its defects are not values: they join LinearRaw's mask, not this view. Its noise is: under a PoissonGaussian or a RelativeNoise each element is an Uncertain{Float64}, the value and its scalar variance (Statistics.var(x), computed on the read by the law LinearRaw's kernel writes per plane, one site at gain 1: owner ruling D14, the default uncertain image of the photosites; the binned cell image, LinearRaw's, is materialised once by its kernel and never re-derived by a consumer), and copy of the view is the materialised form, a StructArray of a value plane and a variance plane (on the view's device). The layout is the raw grid, H × W; channelview of it is the 4 × H/2 × W/2 cells, uncertain alike. With the absent model, the default, the view is the mosaic's own levels, plain values.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.photosite_colors — Method
photosite_colors(mosaic::RawMosaic) -> PhotositeColors <: AbstractMatrix{UInt8}

Each photosite's colour index (LibRaw's: 0 R, 1 G, 2 B, 3 the second G), the size of mosaic: the pattern tiled from the full raw frame's origin, so photosite (row, column) reads pattern[mod(top + row − 1, n) + 1, mod(left + column − 1, n) + 1] with (top, left) = visible_origin .+ window_origin. Lazy and isbits (PhotositeColors): no matrix is built, and it broadcasts with a device mosaic's arrays on the device, fused, so a photosite mask is one broadcast with no host round-trip:

col = photosite_colors(m)
valid = ((col .== 0x01) .| (col .== 0x03)) .& (parent(m) .!= 0) .& .!saturationview(m)

(collect gives a Matrix{UInt8}.)

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.photositeview — Method
photositeview(v::AbstractArray{T, 3}) -> AbstractMatrix{T}

The inverse of a mosaic's channelview, in the role ImageCore's colorview plays for an image's (a name of its own: colorview makes colorants of channels, and this makes the photosite matrix of cells): a channel-first array of 2×2 cells (4 × h × w, each column a cell's photosites in column-major order) as the 2h × 2w photosite matrix, without a copy. Of a mosaic's channelview it is the mosaic's (even-sized) held array again; of any other array (per-photosite values computed channel-first, say) it is a view in the mosaic's layout, photositeview(v)[2i − 1 + di, 2j − 1 + dj] == v[di + 2dj + 1, i, j]. A first dimension other than 4 is an ArgumentError.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.saturationview — Method
saturationview(m::RawMosaic) -> SaturationView

The mosaic's saturation flags on the raw grid, lazily: true at a photosite whose code is at or above the white level, raw ≥ white with no margin — THE saturation rule, the one LinearRaw's flags apply per cell (a cell is flagged when any photosite its planes read is; under a sensor model holding a DefectList LinearRaw also masks those photosites). The Bool match of linearview(m): the same grid, and channelview of it is the flags channel-first. A mask of the usable photosites of one colour is a plain broadcast with photosite_colors:

col = photosite_colors(m)
usable_green = ((col .== 0x01) .| (col .== 0x03)) .& .!saturationview(m)

Linear values on the 2×2 cells

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.LinearRaw — Type
LinearRaw

A RAW image as linear values: (raw − black[c]) / (white − black[c]) × gain (each photosite's linearview value), camera raw colour, white balance 1, 1.0 = the white level (before the gain). Not clipped: a value above 1 (a gain above 1) or below 0 (noise under the black level) is kept.

  • image — image[row, column, plane], on the mosaic's device (an Array on the host); a plane's frame is asked of the image with its index (bayer_plane_map(L, c)). Under a sensor model whose noise defines a variance (PoissonGaussian, RelativeNoise) it is a StructArray of Uncertain{Float64} values (owner ruling D11): its value component the Float64 values, its attributes component each value's variance as a 1×1 CovMat (cov(L.image[i, j, p])[1]), both planes from the one kernel. This cell image is the MATERIALISED uncertain image (owner ruling D14, (a)): its binned variances are computed once, in the one kernel, from the one variance formula, and a consumer never re-derives them; the photosites' uncertain image, linearview(m; sensor_model), is the lazy default. Otherwise (no noise, a ReadNoise alone) it is a plain AbstractArray{Float64, 3}. A consumer dispatches on the image's element type.

  • saturated::AbstractMatrix{Bool} (a BitMatrix on the host) — the MASKED pixels: those whose value includes a photosite at the white level (raw ≥ white) or, under a sensor model holding a DefectList, a defective photosite: in :bin2 a cell is masked when ANY of its four photosites is, so a cell whose two greens straddle the white level is flagged rather than averaged into a plausible value; in :green_cells when either green is. Masked, never interpolated.

  • geometry — where the samples are, (; plane_maps, green_offsets, plane_sites):

    • plane_maps, per plane, its map onto the visible raw (bayer_plane_map, which reads it);
    • green_offsets, the cell's two green photosites, (Δx, Δy) from the cell centre in RAW pixels, in the cell's column-major order (G1, G2 of bayer_channels; green_cell_sites);
    • plane_sites, per plane, the photosites its value is the mean of, each (; color, dn): its CFA colour index (LibRaw's 0 R, 1 G, 2 B, 3 the second G; the index of a per-colour component's entry, σ[color + 1]) and its white − black[c], the codes that span 0 … 1 (before the gain) — what a value's photon and read noise scale by. The green plane's are in the order of green_offsets. black[c] is the channel's constant (the sensor model's black slot's) also when a Dark replaces it per photosite: the span is a scale, the same for every photosite of the channel, so a value is still codes / dn above its own black.

    nothing for :demosaic, whose pixels are interpolated, not samples.

  • gain (positive and finite, ArgumentError otherwise) and the mosaic's metadata (the decoder and its library's version among it).

The mode is the type's first parameter, LinearRaw{:green_cells}, LinearRaw{:bin2} or LinearRaw{:demosaic}: the geometric accessors are methods of the cell images, and the display image's are its refusals. raw_provenance gives the mode, the gain, the metadata and the frame map as plain data for a result to record.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.LinearRaw — Method
LinearRaw(mosaic::RawMosaic; mode::Symbol, gain::Real = 1, sensor_model::SensorModel = SensorModel()) -> LinearRaw

The mosaic's linear values, (raw − black[c]) / (white − black[c]) × gain, on the half-resolution grid of 2×2 Bayer cells (H ÷ 2 × W ÷ 2; an odd last row or column is dropped), arranged by mode (required: there is no neutral choice between them):

  • :green_cells — THE image for geometry and radiometry, one plane: each cell's two green photosites' mean. The greens of a cell are always diagonal, so in every Bayer phase their mean sits at the cell centre (offset (0, 0)), and the cell grid maps onto the raw exactly by raw_frame_map (x ↦ 2x in COLMAP coordinates). The footprint of a value is its two photosites, at (±½, ∓½) raw pixels from the cell centre for an RGGB or BGGR cell, (±½, ±½) for GRBG or GBRG (green_cell_sites).
  • :bin2 — the same cells with their red and blue, × 3 planes: R, the mean of the two greens (the :green_cells image, bit for bit), B. R and B sit at their photosites, (±¼, ±¼) cell pixels from the centre: each plane's map onto the raw is bayer_plane_map.

Every value is from the mosaic's linearview, read channel-first (channelview(linearview(mosaic)), the mosaic's channelview): the green-cell value of cell (i, j) is the mean of the view's two green channels there, times the gain, bit for bit. :demosaic (LibRaw's interpolation, for display figures) is VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures': load_raw(path or mosaic; mode = :demosaic).

The values are those convert_raw.py writes (--mode bin2), in the same Float64 arithmetic and order, before its clip to [0, 1] and 16-bit rounding.

THE black subtraction is here, once, for every caller: sensor_model, the frame's SensorModel (sensor_model resolves it from a characterisation), each slot by its component:

  • black: FileBlack (the absent model's) the mosaic's own per-channel mosaic.black — with the absent model SensorModel(), the default, bit for bit the values without the keyword; ChannelBlack its values (the span white − black[c]); a Dark its per-photosite offset in place of the channel constant in the subtraction ((raw − offset) / (white − black[c]), black its channel black: the per-channel scalar is the constant-map case of the same kernel path);
  • defects: a DefectList's photosites join the saturation flags (saturated is then "masked: saturated or defective"); NoDefects adds none;
  • noise: PoissonGaussian or RelativeNoise make image an uncertain image, each value with its variance from the same kernel (D11): g·max(x, 0)/(m²K)·Σ 1/d + g²/m²·Σ s/d² for a value x of m photosites (spans d, gain g, s the photosite's σ_r[c]², or under a Dark its dark variance times 1 + 1/frames: its read noise and dark current, and the error of the offset subtracted), (σ_rel·x)² under a RelativeNoise; NoNoise and ReadNoise leave it plain (a read-noise variance alone underestimates a well-exposed pixel's, ruling A).

The maps cover the visible raw and are read at the mosaic's window_origin (DimensionMismatch when they do not reach the mosaic's photosites); they are moved to the mosaic's device.

Saturation is raw ≥ white with no margin: on the Sony ARW sets this was written for, codes pile up exactly at the white level (16383) while the histogram runs smoothly through the camera's declared linear limit (15360, metadata.linear_max), so any margin would be a tuned constant. A caller who wants that stricter limit thresholds mosaic.raw itself.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.bayer_plane_map — Method
bayer_plane_map(L::LinearRaw, c::Integer) -> SimilarityMap{2, …, BayerCellFrame{C}, PixelFrame{:colmap}}

The Bayer transform of plane c of the image: the map of the plane's own frame (BayerCellFrame{C}, C its colour — :R, :G, :B for :bin2, :G for :green_cells) onto the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}. It is the plane's translation onto the cell grid (where its samples sit from the cell centre: (±¼, ±¼) cell pixels for R and B, none for G) composed with raw_frame_map(L), so pixel (row, column) of the plane, at (column − ½, row − ½) of its frame, goes onto the centre of the photosites its value is the mean of — R's or B's one, the two greens' midpoint — exactly, for every Bayer phase and either parity of the visible origin. The green plane's map IS raw_frame_map(L), and the R and B planes' maps are the R and B channels' of the mosaic's channelview (bayer_plane_map(m, k)): one rule.

A warp reads plane c of an image through inv(bayer_plane_map(L, c)) ∘ math, math landing in the raw's pixels, like any other map (image_warp; one warp per plane: image_warp!(dst, warps, L.image) with a tuple of them). A composition that joins two planes' frames is refused (MethodError). A plane past the image's, or a :demosaic image, is an ArgumentError.

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.green_cell_sites — Method
green_cell_sites(L::LinearRaw, row::Integer, column::Integer) -> NTuple{2, SVector{2, Float64}}

The footprint of the green-cell value of pixel (row, column): the centres of its two green photosites in the visible raw's COLMAP frame, G1 then G2 (the cell's column-major order). They are diagonal, at (±½, ∓½) raw pixels from the cell centre raw_frame_map(L)((column − ½, row − ½)) in an RGGB or BGGR cell and (±½, ±½) in a GRBG or GBRG one, and the value is the mean of the two — what a renderer models to predict it exactly. Each is the green channel's bayer_plane_map of the mosaic at that pixel. A :demosaic image is refused (ArgumentError).

source
VisualGeometryToolkitCore.green_plane — Method
green_plane(L::LinearRaw) -> AbstractMatrix{Float64}

The image's green cells, a view: each cell's two greens' mean — L.image's plane whose frame IS the cell grid (raw_frame_map(L)): a :green_cells image's one plane, a :bin2 image's second (G). The plane every green-cell consumer reads (a detector, VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' variance_stabilize); its photosites' colours and spans are L.geometry.plane_sites of that plane. An uncertain image's green plane is uncertain too (a StructArray view: values and variances). A :demosaic image is refused (ArgumentError).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.poisson_slope — Method
poisson_slope(L::LinearRaw, model::SensorModel) -> NTuple{P, Float64}

Per plane of L (L.image[:, :, p]), the Poisson slope α of a value's variance under model: the α of α·x + σ², gain/(m²K)·Σ 1/d over the plane's m photosites (L.geometry.plane_sites[p]), 0 without a Poisson term (a model whose noise is not PoissonGaussian). The one expression the kernel writes the variance plane with; the generalized Anscombe transform reads it beside the variance plane (VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' variance_stabilize). A :demosaic image is refused (ArgumentError).

poisson_slope(L::LinearRaw, model::SensorModel, plane) -> Float64

The slope of one plane: plane its index p, or its colour, :R, :G or :B (:G the green plane, green_plane(L)'s; a :green_cells image has :G alone). A colour the image does not hold is an ArgumentError.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.raw_coordinates — Method
raw_coordinates(img, x) -> SVector{2, Float64}

Where the point x of an image's pixel frame (COLMAP, (column − ½, row − ½) the centre of pixel (row, column); any 2-vector or tuple) lies on the sensor: in the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}, the frame of every calibration of the raw. The sensor model: the black level, the noise and the defects

Three levels: the geometric Sensor (part of a camera), a SensorCharacterisation (ONE body's components per ISO, held beside the camera like CaptureSettings) and a SensorModel (ONE frame's black, noise and defect components). Each slot's component is a type, absence included (FileBlack, NoNoise, NoDefects), and each present one states its source (SensorSource: measured, estimated, specified, file). The estimators build a characterisation's components from frames, offline: photon_transfer, master_dark, defect_onsets.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.photon_transfer — Method
photon_transfer(groups::AbstractDict{<:Quantity, <:AbstractVector{<:RawMosaic}},
                darks::AbstractDict{<:Quantity, <:ReadNoise}; sensor_model::SensorModel = SensorModel(),
                block = 8, normalize = true, significance = 0.001) -> FitResult{PoissonGaussian, PhotonTransferMetadata}
photon_transfer(mosaic::RawMosaic; sensor_model::SensorModel = SensorModel(), block = 8, significance = 0.001)
    -> PoissonGaussian

THE estimator of a sensor's Poisson–Gaussian noise for a characterisation's entry, built offline (owner ruling D1: the resolver sensor_model never estimates): the conversion gain K (electrons per code), by a photon-transfer fit of variance against mean, v = σ₀² + μ/K.

The photon-transfer series (groups, exposure => the frames at that exposure, at least 2 each, of one body at one ISO; darks, exposure => the ReadNoise of master_dark over the darks of that exposure), a MEASURED PoissonGaussian (SensorSource.measured, a dedicated capture) in a FitResult (its estimate), its metadata a PhotonTransferMetadata: per green photosite its TEMPORAL variance over the group's frames, which cancels the scene and every fixed pattern, about its mean μ. Pairs are the case of 2 frames. With normalize (the default) each frame is first scaled to its group's mean (its greens' black-subtracted mean over the group's: a source that flickers between frames adds no μ² term). Per block, the photosites' means and their pooled variance give a point, v̂/v following χ²_{p(N−1)}/(p(N − 1)) over a whole block of p photosites and N frames; every group gives its first N frames, N the smallest group's, so every block follows that one law.

  • THE INTERCEPT is fixed at each group's own exposure t: σ₀²(t), the read noise and dark-current noise of the darks at that exposure, the greens' σ[c + 1]² of darks[t]. A group whose exposure has no entry in darks (equal in seconds) is an ArgumentError: never interpolated, never substituted. The fit is the slope alone, v − σ₀²(t) = μ/K.
  • THE RANGE is derived from the data, at least 3 points: the upper end below the variance's rollover (the top point dropped while it lies significantly below the line fitted to the points under it, one-sided), then the lower end, the lowest point from which the μ² test on the line succeeds (the lowest point dropped until it does). Each search runs its tests at significance / m, m the tests it can perform (Bonferroni: the search's family-wise false-alarm rate is significance); the range (in codes and as fractions of the white level), m and the levels are in the result's metadata.
  • THE STANDARD ERROR of K is the fitted line's from the counts, multiplied by the Birge ratio R = √(χ²/dof) of the fitted points when R > 1 (the points scatter more than the counts explain); when R ≤ 1 it is unchanged, never shrunk. R is in the result's metadata.
  • The result's σr is √σ₀² of the fitted points' darks (their median), its standard error not the fit's (0); a characterisation states σr per colour from master_dark's ReadNoise.

One frame (mosaic), in the line of Foi et al. (2008), an ESTIMATED PoissonGaussian (SensorSource.estimated): unreliable on flats, scheduled for removal in the next breaking wave; use the multi-frame method. A single flat frame cannot certify its own K (its brightness range gives the μ² test little power); for flat fields use the multi-frame photon-transfer method. Each 2×2 cell's two greens see the same light, so their difference d carries no scene where the scene is flat across the cell, and Var d = 2(μ/K + σ_r²) about their black-subtracted mean μ. Per block, the cells' mean and the difference's sample variance give a point (μ̂, v̂ = s²/2), v̂/v following χ²_{n−1}/(n − 1) over a whole block of n cells; the line's intercept σr² is fitted, over every point, with its standard error (σr stated for every colour index, the greens'). A fixed pattern between the two greens (their gain or response mismatch) does NOT cancel in d: it adds a term in μ², which the μ² test REFUSES.

Both: per block of block × block cells (one KernelAbstractions work item each, on the frames' device), a sample with a photosite at the white level, or under the model's defect mask, is left out (CLIPPED, never modelled), and only whole blocks (the most usable samples any block has) enter the fit. The blocks are sorted by μ̂ into points of equal count (min(32, blocks ÷ 8); the series': per exposure, so a point never mixes two exposures' intercepts, an exposure of fewer than 8 whole blocks left out); each point's median μ̂ and median v̂ / q (q that law's median, so the ratio is median-unbiased for v). The line is fitted by weighted least squares, each point weighted by the inverse of its median's variance from the counts, v²/(4 m f(q)² q²) over a point of m blocks (f the law's density: the sample median's asymptotic variance), with v the line's own prediction (iterated to a fixed point from a start weighted at each point's own value). The median is robust to the blocks a scene edge raises. The standard errors are the fitted line's covariance from those counts; K's by first order.

THE μ² TEST: the points fitted again with a μ² term, v = … + μ/K + cμ², at the same weights, and c significant when |ĉ / se(ĉ)| exceeds the two-sided normal quantile at the test's level (one frame: significance, 3.29 at the default 0.001, over every point; the series: the lower end's search). It never returns a K biased by a μ² term it detects.

sensor_model gives what is subtracted and left out, as it does for LinearRaw: its black slot's per-colour black (a Dark's offset per photosite in its place) and its defect slot's photosites; its noise slot is not read. Throws NoiseEstimateFailed with the reason when fewer than 16 whole blocks of at least 8 usable samples remain (a saturated frame), the points hold one level only (no slope), one frame's μ² term is significant, the series' μ² test fails on every contiguous range of at least 3 points, or the fit is not physical (K, or one frame's σ_r², not positive). A group of fewer than 2 frames, frames of another size, pattern or white level, or a group's exposure without darks, is an ArgumentError.

Where a sensor component's values came from, SensorSource.T (an isbits @enum): SensorSource.measured (a record of dark frames, flats or a photon-transfer capture), SensorSource.estimated (fitted by an estimator, photon_transfer, with its standard error), SensorSource.specified (stated by a person: a datasheet, a config, TOML literals) or SensorSource.file (the RAW file's own metadata: FileBlack's). string(s) is its tag ("measured", …). Each present component holds one in its source field. A specified component wins over a measured one at the same slot (sensor_model).
