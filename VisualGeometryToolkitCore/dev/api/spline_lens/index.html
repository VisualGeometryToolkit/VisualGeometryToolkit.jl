Spline Lens API

The grid cameras of Schöps et al. (CVPR 2020): a uniform cubic B-spline of rays over the image, central (a SplineLens of a CameraModel) or non-central (NoncentralSplineModel), and the lattice they share. Split from the Cameras API page. See the Lenses guide.

The central grid as a lens

The central grid camera as an ordinary CameraModel(K, SplineLens(…)): a backward lens on the K-normalized image plane, K a gauge whose principal point is the pixel imaging ẑ. Its optional forward spline maps the equidistant chart about ẑ to the image plane. The spline lattice's shared helpers (the grid size, the quasi-interpolant, the window of a block of controls, the chart a control moves in) live beside it.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.CameraModel — Method
CameraModel(SplineLens, controls::AbstractMatrix{<:StaticVector{3}}, size; calib_area,
            intrinsics = nothing, present = nothing, unit_controls = false)
    -> CameraModel{<:LogicalIntrinsics, <:SplineLens}

THE constructor of a SplineLens camera (the source constructor and spline_lattice build through it): the camera of a pixel lattice given by its direction controls, as the reference implementation (Schöps et al.'s intrinsics0.yaml) and the stored grid records state one: controls ([gy, gx]; an absent node's control is never read, not even by the gauge) laid over the calibrated pixel area calib_area (a Rect of whole pixels, the closed rectangle of pixel corners [min, max + 1]; default Rect(size), the whole image) of an image of size, the nodes present held (nothing: every node). Its K is intrinsics (skew 0, positive focal lengths, else an ArgumentError) or, without one, the lattice's gauge. Its backward area is K⁻¹ of the calibrated rectangle, so each pixel's lattice coordinate is the reference's and the camera backprojects as the pixel grid does, whatever the K. The controls are kept as given (unit_controls = true normalizes each one unless it is unit to 1e-12, the reference reader's rule).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.CameraModel — Method
CameraModel(SplineLens, source, size; grid_size, calib_area, init = :interpolate, sampling_margin = 0.97)
    -> CameraModel{<:LogicalIntrinsics, <:SplineLens}

The SplineLens camera initialized from source — a CameraModel (its backprojection) or a function pixel -> direction (nothing or a non-finite vector where it images nothing) — over an image of size (a Size2): a lattice of grid_size = (columns, rows) controls (default default_spline_grid) over the calibrated pixel area calib_area (a Rect of whole pixels, the closed rectangle its pixels cover; default Rect(size), the whole image).

init = :interpolate (default) solves the cubic-prefilter tridiagonal system per axis so the spline INTERPOLATES the source's backprojection at the grid nodes, sampling a camera inside sampling_margin times its lens's valid domain. init = :quasi_interpolate is the 4th-order local quasi-interpolant on a lattice extended one node past the area (quasi_interpolant), with its border rule for a source whose domain ends near or inside the area; a function source takes it. The prefilter's controls are re-normalized (each unit unless it is unit to 1e-12), the quasi-interpolant's kept as it forms them.

A camera source keeps its own K (skew ≠ 0 is an ArgumentError); any other source takes the gauge of its sampled spline: the principal point is the pixel imaging ẑ (the calibrated area's centre when ẑ is not imaged inside it) and fx, fy the inverse local scale of d_xy/d_z there, skew 0.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ControlPatchManifold — Type
ControlPatchManifold(directions[, points]) <: Manifold

A patch of a spline lattice's controls as a manifold (a SplineLens's, a NoncentralSplineModel's): a PX × PY block of its direction controls (an SMatrix of 3-vectors, in the caller's layout: the coordinates follow its column-major order) and, for a non-central model, the matching block of its point controls — the block a per-tile refinement moves (an NLLSsolver variable through the manifold adapter). A direction control d moves on its own sphere by the reference's chart,

‖d‖ · normalize(d + δ₁t₁ + δ₂t₂),   (t₁, t₂) = direction_tangents(d / ‖d‖),

2 coordinates that keep its norm (the norm is a weight of the spline's blend, not a gauge) and whose derivative at δ = 0 is (t₁, t₂). A point control moves additively, p + (Δx, Δy, Δz). The coordinates are per control, column-major over the block: (δ₁, δ₂) each, (δ₁, δ₂, Δx, Δy, Δz) with points — restrict them by mask to hold some fixed (a pupil-plane model's Δz). model(ch) is (; directions, points) (points is nothing for a central patch); retract_jacobian(ch) is block diagonal, (t₁ t₂) per direction and the identity per point.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.SplineLens — Type
SplineLens(backward::ChartSpline{3}; forward = nothing, present = nothing) <: AbstractLensModel

The central grid camera's lens (Schöps et al., CVPR 2020) as a backward lens of a CameraModel: the normalized image point x = K⁻¹u maps to the unit direction normalize(backward(x)), backward a uniform cubic B-spline of 3-vectors over the closed rectangle backward.area of the normalized plane (ChartSpline; controls [row = y, column = x], size − 3 cells per axis). backproject(lens, x) is NaN outside the area's closed box [origin, origin + widths] and where the point's 4 × 4 window holds an absent node; it never throws.

forward (a ChartSpline{2} from the equidistant chart about ẑ to x, or nothing) is the optional fitted forward map. present says which nodes the lens holds: nothing (every node) or a Bool matrix of size(backward.controls) (true = present); an all-true mask is stored as nothing, an all-false one is an ArgumentError. An absent node's control is never read.

A camera of this lens has skew 0 and positive focal lengths (an ArgumentError wherever one is built otherwise), and its K is a gauge: the principal point is the pixel imaging ẑ (CameraModel(SplineLens, source, size)). Where ẑ is not imaged inside the calibrated area, the gauge falls back to the area's centre, a pixel that does not image ẑ: the methods that read the principal point as the optical axis — undistort and undistort! onto the gauge pinhole, distortion_map, valid_rect, undistorted_rect, angle_of_view, horizon_radius and monotone_radius, image_circle(camera, sensor, d), view_overlap — then throw an ArgumentError naming the off-axis gauge.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.axis_gauged — Method
axis_gauged(camera::CameraModel{<:AbstractIntrinsics, <:SplineLens}, rotation::Rotation{3}, K′) -> CameraModel
axis_gauged(camera::Union{PosedCamera, BoundedCamera}, rotation, K′)
axis_gauged(extrinsics::Union{EuclideanMap{3}, RotationMap{3}}, rotation::Rotation{3})

The same SplineLens camera in its camera frame turned by rotation (d′ = R·d), its K the skew-0 K′ (a 3 × 3 matrix or a CameraCalibrationMat). With R taking the lens's optical axis onto ẑ and K′ the radially symmetric camera fitted to it (its principal point the pixel imaging that axis, its focal lengths and aspect the fit's), the principal point images ẑ and ẑ is the optical axis.

It is an exact re-gauge, not a fit.

  • Every backward control is turned, c ↦ R·c (with_directions), so each pixel's ray is R times its old ray, to rounding (the normalization commutes with R).
  • K becomes K′: its principal point and its focal lengths, so its aspect, are K′'s. This is CameraModel(camera, K′): any skew-0 K is an exact gauge of the lattice, so no ray changes.
  • The forward spline is dropped, since it inverts the old rays.
  • The presence mask is kept.

The poses turn with the frame.

  • A world → camera map (extrinsics, X_c = R_p·X + t) becomes (R·R_p, R·t), its frame tags kept (the third form). Every projection is then kept.
  • A posed camera turns its extrinsics with its model.
  • A bounded camera turns the camera it wraps, a posed one's extrinsics included, and keeps its image and lens coverage.

A spline that is never gauged this way keeps its source's ẑ: one read from a record, or one built by CameraModel(SplineLens, source, size).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.cubic_bspline_prefilter_taps — Method
cubic_bspline_prefilter_taps(K::Integer) -> Vector{Float64}

The one-sided taps h[0], …, h[K] of the cubic B-spline interpolation prefilter on an unbounded uniform grid: the inverse of the sampled B-spline (z + 4 + z⁻¹)/6 is √3·z₁^|k| with z₁ = √3 − 2 (Unser, Aldroubi & Eden 1991), truncated at |k| ≤ K and renormalised so h[0] + 2Σₖ h[k] = 1 (a constant is reproduced exactly). The controls c[j] = Σₖ h[|k|]·f[j + k] make the spline INTERPOLATE the samples f away from any border: the prefilter of _prefilter_matrix, the tridiagonal system of a finite lattice with its boundary rows, as a local filter a kernel can apply in a window (the dropped tail is 2√3·|z₁|^(K+1)/(1 − |z₁|) of a unit sample: 2.6e-6 at K = 10). VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' continuous DoG response reads it.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.quasi_interpolant — Method
quasi_interpolant(samples::AbstractMatrix) -> controls

The separable 4th-order quasi-interpolant of uniform cubic B-splines: node samples f (a matrix of values with + and scalar *, laid out [gy, gx] as a spline lattice's controls) become controls c_j = (−f_{j−1} + 8 f_j − f_{j+1}) / 6, along x (the second index) first, then along y; at the border the outermost sample is replicated. Exact for cubics at interior nodes, local (each control reads three samples per axis), no linear solve. The stencil of init = :quasi_interpolate, where the samples extend one node past the grid on every side so that no grid control reads a replicated sample.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.static_lm — Method
static_lm(fj, x₀::SVector{N, T}, lb, ub, s, maxiter, tol2[, fs, bound, cost_floor]) -> (x, converged, J, e, rule::StopRule)

The stack's ONE device-side Levenberg–Marquardt (the spline lenses' inverse at N = 2, any static N): Marquardt-scaled, bounded to the box [lb, ub] (a step is clipped to it), with an unrolled Cholesky-type solve and explicit fused products, so it allocates nothing, never throws, and runs inside a KernelAbstractions kernel with the same bits in every compiled copy. fj(x) returns (r, J, …) (the residual SVector, its SMatrix Jacobian, anything else returned with them as e); s the per-coordinate scale its step and damping floors are read in; converged is ‖r(x)‖² < tol2. rule is the stop that fired: ExactFit (the cost floor), ToleranceMet (the gradient floor), IncrementSize (the step), CostDecrease (the relative cost decrease) or IterationCap; static_lm_termination gives the §14 record. NLLSsolver (lmsolve_chart!) remains every host fit's LM.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.project — Method

The non-central grid on the lens's directions

NoncentralSplineModel: the central grid's directions (a SplineLens's backward spline) and a point spline over the same rectangle of the normalized plane, one observation line per pixel.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.NoncentralSplineModel — Type
NoncentralSplineModel(K::LogicalIntrinsics, directions::ChartSpline{3}, points::ChartSpline{3};
                      present = nothing) <: AbstractCamera
NoncentralSplineModel(source, size; grid_size, calib_area, init, sampling_margin)

The non-central grid camera (Schöps et al., CVPR 2020): the pixel u observes the LINE through points(x) along normalize(directions(x)), x = K⁻¹u the normalized image point, both splines laid over the same rectangle of the normalized plane with the same lattice (ChartSpline; controls [row = y, column = x]). backproject returns a Ray3; project is Levenberg–Marquardt on the reference's tangent-plane point-to-line residual. With an all-zero point spline the model is exactly its central_model, a SplineLens camera.

K is the gauge of a SplineLens camera (skew 0 and positive focal lengths, else an ArgumentError). present says which nodes the model holds, as a SplineLens's (nothing, or a Bool matrix of the grid's size; an all-true mask is nothing).

The points are plain metres in the camera frame. This is an exception to the units layering that the type forces: its controls are interpolated in the inner loop and cannot hold quantities, so the unit is stated here instead. A reader or writer of the model converts at its boundary. A PosedCamera of the model therefore takes physical extrinsics (EuclideanMap(R, t .* m), or with_unit(E, mm) of a map in mm) and projects scene points that are lengths; a plain extrinsics is an ArgumentError. ustrip(m, camera) is the plain camera, in metres only.

The model carries no image extent: a stored or rig camera holds it in its BoundedCamera.

From a source (a camera or a function pixel -> direction): the directions of CameraModel(SplineLens, source, size; …) (the same keywords and K), the points all zero.

The lattice: a spline on a chart

The lattice of both, as a primitive: ChartSpline, a uniform cubic B-spline over a rectangle of a 2-D chart, its window and span weights, and the equidistant direction chart a forward spline is laid on.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.ChartSpline — Type
ChartSpline(area::Rect2, controls::AbstractMatrix{<:StaticVector{K}})

A tensor-product uniform cubic B-spline from a 2-D chart to ℝᴷ: the controls laid over the closed rectangle area of the chart (controls[row, column], the row along the chart's second coordinate, size − 3 cells per axis, so the knot spacing along axis i is widths(area)[i] / (nᵢ − 3)). Evaluated with spline_value, continued past area by the outermost cell's cubic pieces.

A CoordinateTransformations.Transformation: cs(c) is the value at the chart point c, transform_deriv(cs, c) its K × 2 Jacobian (the differentiated cubic pieces, no automatic differentiation), and cs(points) on a vector of chart points evaluates the batch with a KernelAbstractions kernel on the points' own backend (the controls are uploaded beside them).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.cubic_bspline_weight_derivatives — Method
cubic_bspline_weight_derivatives(u) -> (NTuple{4}, NTuple{4})

The first and second derivatives in u of the four cubic_bspline_weights at the span's local coordinate u: ((−(1 − u)², 3u² − 4u, −3u² + 2u + 1, u²) / 2, (1 − u, 3u − 2, −3u + 1, u)), the same cubic pieces differentiated once and twice, in the same local coordinate and Horner form as the weights. Kernel-lawful, in u's own type (a Float32 coordinate gives Float32 derivatives). The spline lattices' partials (spline_weights, spline_window_sums) and VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' continuous DoG response read it.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.cubic_bspline_weights — Method
cubic_bspline_weights(u) -> NTuple{4}

The four weights of a uniform cubic B-spline span at its local coordinate u ∈ [0, 1): the value on the span is w₁c₁ + w₂c₂ + w₃c₃ + w₄c₄ over its four consecutive controls,

((1 − u)³, 3u³ − 6u² + 4, −3u³ + 3u² + 3u + 1, u³) / 6,

a partition of unity. Outside [0, 1) the same cubics continue (extrapolate). Kernel-lawful: in u's own type (a Float32 coordinate gives Float32 weights, a ForwardDiff.Dual dual ones), no allocation and no branch, so a device kernel can call it. Evaluated in Horner form in u, which keeps Float32 within 1e-7 of the exact weights. The weights a spline lattice's windows (spline_window) and ChartSpline read.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.equidistant_chart — Method
equidistant_chart(axis::StaticVector{3}) -> Lens(Equidistant()) ∘ LinearMap(R)

The equidistant direction chart about axis (any nonzero 3-vector, normalized here): a ray at angle θ from the axis lands at radius θ (radians) in the chart plane, in the direction of its azimuth; singular only at −axis. R is the rotation whose rows are (e₁, e₂, axis), with e₁ = normalize(ŷ × axis) (x̂ × axis when the axis is within 1e-6 of ±ŷ) and e₂ = axis × e₁ — right-handed, so the chart is the camera's own equidistant lens when axis = ẑ. The chart a SplineLens's forward spline is laid on (about ẑ), and the one a direction field over a cap of the sphere (a panorama's vignetting) reads.

spline_value(controls, area::Rect2, point)

The uniform cubic B-spline surface of controls (any matrix of values with + and scalar *: scalars, 3-vectors) laid over the closed rectangle area ([row = y, column = x], cells = size − 3 per axis), at point, continued past the area as spline_window: a ChartSpline's value, for a lattice of any control type (a per-position aperture radius, a field over the image).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_weights — Method
spline_weights(cs::ChartSpline, x) -> (ix, iy, w, (∂w₁, ∂w₂))

The weights of the 16 controls a point x of the chart reads, and their partials: w[r, c] multiplies cs.controls[iy - 3 + r, ix - 3 + c] (r the second coordinate's tap, c the first's; sum(w .* window controls) is cs(x)), and ∂wᵢ = ∂w/∂xᵢ, all SMatrix{4, 4}. The window is the point's own (continued past the area, as spline_window). In the point's precision. For a SplineLens camera's backward spline, x is the normalized point K⁻¹u: the pixel partials are ∂w₁ / fx, ∂w₂ / fy (skew 0).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_window — Method
spline_window(area::Rect2, grid_size, point) -> (ix, iy, wx, wy)

The window point reads on the uniform cubic lattice of grid_size = (rows, cols) controls laid over the closed rectangle area (cells = size − 3 per axis): the controls [iy - 3 + r, ix - 3 + c], r, c ∈ 1:4, and the four weights per axis (wx[c] along x, wy[r] along y; w[r, c] = wy[r] * wx[c]). Outside the area the outermost cell's cubic pieces continue — the same continuation spline_value evaluates.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.spline_window_sums — Method
spline_window_sums(control, zero, wx, wy, dwx, dwy[, ddwx, ddwy]) -> (S, S₁, S₂[, S₁₁, S₁₂, S₂₂])

THE 4 × 4 tensor-product sweep of a uniform cubic B-spline window: the value S = Σᵣ Σ_c wy[r]·wx[c]·control(r, c) and its partials in the window's two lattice coordinates (S₁ through dwx, the first coordinate, along c; S₂ through dwy, along r), and with the second-derivative weights ddwx, ddwy the second partials S₁₁, S₁₂, S₂₂. control(r, c) returns the window's control at row tap r and column tap c (1…4), zero the accumulators' zero (a number or a static vector); the weights are cubic_bspline_weights and cubic_bspline_weight_derivatives of the span's local coordinates. One sweep, rows outer and columns inner, in plain arithmetic in that one order, so every caller sums the same bits; kernel-lawful. The spline lattices' value and partials read it (the lattice → area slope applied by the caller), as does VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' continuous DoG response.
