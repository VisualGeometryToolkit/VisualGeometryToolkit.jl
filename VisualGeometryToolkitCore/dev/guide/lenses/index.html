Lens Families

Every lens is one of two kinds, built from a handful of parts. The parts — projections and terms — are leaves, structs of numbers and tuples of numbers; everything a lens is asked (its coefficients, their names, its zero, its record, its manifold, every Jacobian, every batch) is derived from their fields by one walk, never tabulated per model. A coefficient is named by its field — a tuple field k as k1, k2, … — and a leaf's record tag is its name in snake case (DoubleSphere → double_sphere, FieldOfView → field_of_view; the Full radial basis is radial_full).

Two kinds of lens

  • Lens(projection, distortion) — forward: the ray is projected onto the projection's frame, and the frame point is distorted there, x_d = distortion(projection(X)). Projection is closed form; back-projection inverts the distortion.
  • LiftLens(radial) — backward: the image point lifts to its ray in closed form, (x_d·N(ρ), D(ρ)); projection is a root on the ray. D crossing zero is the 90° circle and D < 0 the field behind it.

Lens(projection) alone is the pure projection; Pinhole is Lens(Perspective()).

The whole sphere of directions onto the (longitude, latitude) rectangle — a panorama raster, a sky map — is Equirectangular: not a lens (it has no radial profile), a projection of its own with project/backproject. Its cylindrical family shares the longitude and changes the vertical coordinate: Cylindrical (tan φ, the central projection onto the unit cylinder), CylindricalEqualArea (sin φ: equal raster areas are equal solid angles) and Mercator (asinh(tan φ), conformal). Each is a Transformation whose inv is its Backprojection, so a panorama raster's pixel → ray map composes into an image_warp chain; an azimuthal view needs no type of its own — it is Backprojection(camera) of a virtual camera with the view's lens (Lens(Perspective()), Lens(Equidistant()), Lens(Stereographic())).

The arity of a term is its tuple lengths — a type parameter — so a four-coefficient Kannala–Brandt and a six-coefficient one are the same family. Radial{Full} uses the powers ρ², ρ³, … instead of ρ², ρ⁴, … (Scaramuzza's lift).

  • Additive(m, Δ₁, …) — every term at the same point, summed: m(x) + (Δ₁(x) + …), the deltas left to right. OpenCV's projectPoints and COLMAP's Distortion() are of this form.
  • CoordinateTransformations' ∘ — in sequence, (D₂ ∘ D₁)(x) = D₂(D₁(x)) (a term is a Transformation): Aria's Fisheye624 (COLMAP RAD_TAN_THIN_PRISM_FISHEYE) applies its tangential and thin-prism terms to the radially distorted point, Additive(Tangential(…), ThinPrism(…)) ∘ Radial(…); OpenCV's tilt follows its additive model, Tilt(τx, τy) ∘ Additive(…). Each stage is a map term or an Additive. A composition's coefficients are in CT's order, the outer stage's first, and its record lists the stages the same way ("compose", the outer first; the retired "chain", which listed them in application order, is refused).

Whether a lens is radially symmetric about the principal point — what RadialCamera(::PosedCamera) needs — is the trait RadialSymmetry, read off the lens's type: the projections, Radial, FieldOfView and LiftLens are, and an Additive, a ∘ composition or a Lens when every piece is. It fails closed: a lens type defined downstream is asymmetric until it declares itself, VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RadialSymmetry(::Type{MyLens}) = VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.RadiallySymmetric().

The adapters that read and write these conventions — and reproduce each convention's function exactly — live in VisualGeometryToolkitIO.

Coefficients

parameter_vector(lens) is the coefficient vector — projection first, then the distortion's terms depth-first, left to right; parameter_names names it, parameter_bounds states its domain (unbounded, but the double sphere's ξ > −1 and α ∈ [0, 1], the unified models' ξ > −1, α ∈ [0, 1] and β ≥ 0, the field-of-view ω ∈ [0, π)), parameters pairs the names and values in a NamedTuple, and with_parameters rebuilds the same structure from a vector of any number type. The same functions describe a calibratable target, its TargetCalibration, a LogicalIntrinsics (f, aspect, skew, cx, cy, with f, aspect > 0) and a parametric CameraModel — the intrinsics' coordinates followed by the lens's. ParameterManifold (a lens's LensManifold, minimal_manifold(lens) or minimal_manifold(camera)) moves the coefficients inside their bounds — a log for a one-sided bound, a scaled logit for an interval — and restrict(ch, names) frees a subset by name: a camera's restrict(minimal_manifold(camera), (:f, :cx, :cy, :k1)) fixes its skew and aspect. An optimizer's search box is not a model fact; it stays the solver's option, keyed by parameter_names.

A caller holds parameters with ONE keyword, fixed: the parameters held, by name or by group (:k for k1, k2, …), () by default (everything is refined; a camera's skew, the gauge, is held by construction, so naming it changes nothing). LensSpec(family; fixed) and VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation's refine_camera_and_poses take it, and free_parameter_names resolves it to the names that stay free — free_parameter_names((:f, :aspect, :skew, :cx, :cy, :k1, :k2), (:aspect, :k)) is (:f, :cx, :cy). A name or group that names nothing of the model, or a parameter named twice, is an ArgumentError, and so is the retired free (the complement), naming fixed; the refines refuse fix, fixed_params and hold alike.

radial(lens) is the lens's radially symmetric factor — its folds and poles bound what the lens images (view_angle_limit, monotone_radius) — and radial_part the lens with only that factor.

The valid domain and the image circle

Two regions of the sensor, easy to confuse and never interchangeable:

  • valid_domain(camera) is where the lens MODEL is valid: the image of the cone of view_angle_limit through the radial projection and K, inside which the lens's radial profile is finite and strictly increasing. It is a property of the formula and says nothing about light: a fitted Kannala–Brandt profile can fold far outside the lit disc.
  • image_circle(camera, diameter) is where light falls, from a lens datasheet's image-circle diameter through the sensor's pitch. A circle measured from images serves equally; it is never predicted from the model.

Both are an Ellipse in pixels about the principal point (an ellipse proper for an anisotropic K or pitch), so the region algebra treats them alike.

  • For a lens that is not radially symmetric (a composed distortion with a tilt stage, or tangential terms) the valid domain is an inscribed bound.
  • A lens with no limit of its own (a perspective lens, an undistorted equidistant lens: view_angle_limit reports only its chart's or the sphere's edge, a cap) has the WholePlane as its valid domain, the identity of ∩.
using VisualGeometryToolkitCore
using VisualGeometryToolkitCore: Pinhole, KannalaBrandt
s = sensor("Sony", "IMX250")                            # 2448 × 2048 @ 3.45 μm
k = -4 / (3π^2)                                         # r = θ(1 + kθ²) folds at 90°, r = (2/3)θ
f = ustrip(px, 6.0mm / (2 * s.pitch.width)) / (π / 3)   # a 180° lens drawing a 6 mm circle
cam = BoundedCamera(CameraModel(; focal = f, pp = (1224.0, 1024.0), lens = KannalaBrandt(k)), s)
v = valid_domain(cam)                                   # where the lens formula holds
(centroid(v), v.a, v.b)
([1224.0, 1024.0], 869.5652165217391, 869.5652165217391)
q = image_circle(cam, 6.0mm)                            # where light falls: the datasheet, through the pitch
(v.a - q.a, Rect(extent(cam)) ∈ q, rad2deg(angle_of_view(cam)), rad2deg(angle_of_view(cam; dimension = :diagonal)))
(-8.695652695678291e-7, false, 179.9999998199235, 179.9999998199235)

Here the lens was chosen so the two coincide; for a fitted lens they need not. angle_of_view is the angle of view the camera images — its extent bounded by its lens coverage, clipped by the model's valid domain; field_of_view is the size that angle spans at a distance.

rcam = BoundedCamera(CameraModel(; focal = 2000.0, pp = (1224.0, 1024.0), lens = Pinhole()), s)
(valid_domain(rcam), rad2deg(angle_of_view(rcam)), rad2deg(angle_of_view(rcam; dimension = :diagonal)))
(VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Primitives.WholePlane(), 62.933264526160734, 77.17464549362784)

A local lens model — a grid of nodes rather than a formula — has observations only where light falls: the camera's imaged_region, the sensor ∩ its lens coverage (here the datasheet's circle) ∩ where the lens model is valid:

domain = imaged_region(with_lens_coverage(cam, q))         # a grid lens's domain
nodes = [Point2(x, y) for x in range(0.0, 2448.0; length = 25), y in range(0.0, 2048.0; length = 21)]
lit = filter(n -> n ∈ domain, nodes)
(length(lit), length(nodes) - length(lit))              # lit, outside
(225, 300)

The whole example, runnable, is examples/image_circle.jl.

Which parameters a camera can see

A camera images a region of its sensor, imaged_region(camera) = Rect(extent) ∩ lens_coverage(camera) ∩ valid_domain(camera), its lens coverage (the image circle it carries, lens_coverage); pixel_mask(region, width, height) rasterizes it, the pixels whose centres lie in it. Each parameter of a model has a support on the sensor; it is free when its support meets the imaged region and frozen otherwise. A global model (a parametric lens, K) has the whole plane as every parameter's support, so free_nodes answers Colon() (all of them); a node model such as a SplineLens camera is LocalSupport and only the nodes whose footprint meets the lens coverage are free.

fish = CameraModel(; focal = 250.0, pp = (640.0, 480.0), lens = KannalaBrandt(k))
g = CameraModel(SplineLens, fish, Size2(1280, 960); grid_size = (20, 16))
circle = Ellipse(Point2(640.0, 480.0), 250.0, 250.0, 0.0)   # a measured image circle, 250 px
R = imaged_region(BoundedCamera(fish, Size2(1280, 960); lens_coverage = circle))
mask = node_mask(g, R)                                  # in the grid's shape
lit = pixel_mask(R, 1280, 960)                          # the pixels it images: 960 × 1280, ≈ π · 250² of them
(size(mask), count(mask), length(mask) - count(mask), free_nodes(fish, R), count(lit))   # free, frozen; a lens: all
((16, 20), 96, 224, Colon(), 196364)

A lattice model declares its supports through two contract generics: support_trait(::Type{<:MyLattice}) = LocalSupport(), then VisualGeometryToolkitCore.nodes(m) (the node ids, CartesianIndices of the lattice) and VisualGeometryToolkitCore.support(m, i) (the node's region on the sensor in pixels — for a uniform cubic B-spline lattice cubic_bspline_footprint(center, spacing)); free_nodes and node_mask then work unchanged.

A spline lens in a refinement

A refinement of a SplineLens camera reads it through a few primitives of its lattice. The controls of a lens may be kept off the sphere (unit_controls = false): backproject normalizes the spline value, so a control's norm is an interpolation weight, not a gauge — an optimizer stepping in each control's tangent plane (direction_tangents) leaves them there. The lens's backward is the raw spline, a ChartSpline on the normalized plane x = K⁻¹u (generic in the element type, Float32 on a device), continued past its area by the outermost cells' cubic pieces; a per-tile cost block evaluates a window — the camera of a block of controls, possibly replaced, which evaluates as the camera wherever its windows lie in the block; spline_window gives the 4 × 4 controls and weights a point reads, for the Jacobian's fan-out onto the controls; and spline_value lays the same lattice under any control type (a scalar field over the image).

init = :quasi_interpolate builds a camera from any source — a camera or a function pixel -> direction (nothing where it images nothing) — by the local 4th-order quasi_interpolant on a lattice extended one node past the area, linearly extrapolating per axis where the source images nothing and inward from the area's centre where its domain ends inside the area. A camera source keeps its own K; a function source takes the gauge of its sampled spline (the principal point is the pixel imaging ẑ). Where light falls is the bound's, not the lens's: BoundedCamera(cam, extent; lens_coverage = circle) carries a supplied image circle, and free_nodes(cam, imaged_region(bc)) are the nodes that can reach a pixel it images.

A lattice already known — Schöps et al.'s intrinsics0.yaml, a stored grid record — is built from its controls: CameraModel(SplineLens, controls, size; calib_area, intrinsics, present) lays the direction controls ([gy, gx]) over the calibrated pixel area as the reference does, so every pixel backprojects as its grid does; K is the given one (skew 0) or the lattice's gauge, and an absent node's control is never read. It is the one constructor: the source constructor and spline_lattice build through it.

A lens need not hold the other nodes. Built with with_present(cam, node_mask(cam, region)), it keeps the same lattice (spacing, basis, the dense control matrix) and holds only those nodes; every pixel of the region reads them alone, so the camera evaluates the region as the full one. present_nodes is the mask; past the fully present windows backproject is NaN and project gives a NaN pixel, as outside the area. with_directions(cam, D) replaces the direction controls (the same K, lattice and mask; the forward spline, which inverted the old directions, is dropped). Both rebuild a BoundedCamera too, keeping its bound, lens coverage and pose.

A spline's ẑ is a gauge: wherever its first calibration's frame put it, which may be far from the lens's optical axis. axis_gauged(cam, R, K′) turns the camera frame by R (every control R·c, so every ray R·d, exactly) and takes the skew-0 K′ as its gauge (no ray changes). Turn the poses by the same R with axis_gauged(extrinsics, R); a posed camera turns its own. Every image is then kept. With R taking the optical axis onto ẑ and K′ the radially symmetric camera fitted to the lens (its principal point the axis's pixel), the principal point is the optical axis.

cam = CameraModel(SplineLens, fish, Size2(1280, 960); grid_size = (35, 25), init = :quasi_interpolate)
w = window(cam, 5:14, 15:24)                       # rows (y) × cols (x) of the grid
backproject(w, SVector(700.0, 400.0)) ≈ backproject(cam, SVector(700.0, 400.0))

The lens record

lens_record writes a lens as a self-describing record, the form VisualGeometryToolkitIO stores and configurations name: every key a leaf tag or a combinator, every value a coefficient array whose length is the arity.

lens_record(KannalaBrandt(-0.043, 0.011))
# {"equidistant": [], "radial": [[-0.043, 0.011], []]}
lens_record(Division(-0.31, 0.024))
# {"lift": {"radial": [[], [-0.31, 0.024]]}}
lens_record(Lens(Perspective(), Additive(Radial((-0.28,)), Tangential(1.0e-3, -5.0e-4))))
# {"perspective": [], "add": [{"radial": [[-0.28], []]}, {"tangential": [0.001, -0.0005]}]}

lens_from_record inverts it exactly: the record determines the lens type, and with shortest round-trip floats a Float64 lens reads back bitwise.
