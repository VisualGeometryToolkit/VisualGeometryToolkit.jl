Back-projection and Undistortion

Every lens answers two questions, and only two: which image point a ray lands on, and which ray is behind an image point. project(lens, X) and backproject(lens, x_d) are that contract, on rays — so a fisheye that images 190° answers for every ray it images, past 90° included. Everything else is derived from the pair, for every lens, with no per-lens code.

The contract

  • project(lens, X::StaticVector{3}) -> Vec2 — the normalized image point of the ray X (any positive multiple of it).
  • backproject(lens, x_d::StaticVector{2}) -> Vec3 — the UNIT ray behind the normalized image point x_d.
  • Both return NaN where the lens does not image (past a fold, outside a model's field of view), never throw, never allocate, and are generic in the number type: Float32 for GPU kernels, ForwardDiff Duals for Jacobians in the point and in the lens parameters.

A Lens — a projection plus a distortion in the projection's frame — gets both maps from one generic body; a LiftLens (the division family, the rational lift, Scaramuzza's polynomial) lifts the image point to its ray in closed form and projects by a root on the ray. See Lens Families.

Derived for every lens

  • Perspective views. distort_normalized(lens, x) = project(lens, (x, 1)) — also the functor lens(x) — and undistort_normalized(lens, x_d), the perspective point of backproject(lens, x_d): NaN where that ray has Z ≤ 0, which no perspective image contains. inv(lens) is an InverseLens whose call is undistort_normalized.
  • Jacobians. transform_deriv(lens, X), transform_deriv_params(lens, X) and project_with_jacobians(lens, X) on rays; transform_deriv(lens, x) and transform_deriv(inv(lens), x_d) on the perspective views — ForwardDiff through the maps, exact at the solved roots.
  • Radial profile. projected_radius(lens, θ) and view_angle(lens, ρ).
  • Cameras. project(model, X), backproject(model, u), unproject(model, u, depth) (Z-depth: NaN where the ray has Z ≤ 0; a point at distance d along any ray is d · backproject(model, u)), and imaging_transform(model) = AffineMap(K) ∘ lens, the lens applied to the ray.

Inside the maps

The inverse of an in-plane distortion is a solve, matched to the model's structure and private to the maps:

distortionsolve
a lone Radial factor (Kannala–Brandt, COLMAP RADIAL, …), any frameNewton on the ratio g = ρ/ρ_d — no square root, exact on the axis — safeguarded on the factor's branch
an Additive led by a Radial (OpenCV's models, COLMAP's thin-prism fisheye)OpenCV's fixed point x ← (x_d − ΣΔ(x))/F(r²), finished by trust-region Newton where it stops contracting
an Additive of deltas, a lone deltaPicard peel x ← x_d − ΣΔ(x), trust-region Newton if it does not certify
FieldOfView, Tiltclosed form
a Chain, anything elsetrust-region 2-D Newton
a LiftLensproject: the root of ρ·N·z − r·D on the ray, nothing divided by z; backproject: closed form
the parametric projectionsclosed forms both ways, with the papers' validity bounds

Every solve stops by its convergence order — a Newton step at the solve tolerance of the iterate's scale, a contraction's update at rounding level — under a trip cap, and is certified (residual, and the branch where it matters); a solve that does not certify is NaN. For Dual inputs the derivative comes from one Newton step at the converged primal root (the implicit function theorem), so first derivatives are exact.

Batches

A batch of points goes through the same per-point maps by broadcasting: project!(u, lens, X), backproject!, distort_normalized! and undistort_normalized! are pointwise! of project, backproject, …: a SIMD loop on a host array (split across threads above a measured length, or as the exec policy says) and one compiled kernel on a GPU array. A camera is a per-point map too: project!(u, camera, X). A point the lens does not image comes back NaN. See Batches.

Adding a lens model

A new term or projection is a leaf: its coefficients as a tuple, how to rebuild it from one, their names, its record tag, and its map — a term's _distort (or _delta, with isdelta), a projection's _project_ray / _lift_point. Everything above follows. A lens of a new kind implements project(lens, X) and backproject(lens, x_d) directly.
