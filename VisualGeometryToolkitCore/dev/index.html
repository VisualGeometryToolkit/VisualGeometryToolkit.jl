VisualGeometryToolkitCore.jl

Computational geometry, camera modeling, and projective solvers for computer vision.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.jl is the foundation layer of the VisualGeometryToolkit stack:

  • Coordinate frames — type-level frame tags, 3D and image-plane, on maps, forms, and covariance matrices; composition, inversion, and propagation are frame-checked by dispatch.
  • Camera models — pinhole, Brown–Conrady, and the fisheye-projection family (Kannala–Brandt, equidistant, stereographic, equisolid, orthographic), one lens contract on rays (project/backproject, valid past 90°) from which every lens map is derived, and a framed, junction-checked imaging chain.
  • Two-view geometry — stereo rigs, slot-labeled epipolar forms, homography/fundamental/essential/P3P solver kernels, minimal model charts with covariance transport.
  • Geometric primitives — circles, ellipses, discs, feature frames, hyperplanes, and their homogeneous matrix representations.
  • Transforms — matrix-backed homogeneous transforms, region maps, image warping, and a batched structure-of-arrays protocol with GPU-portable kernels.
  • Robust-estimation primitives — the unified robust-loss family, block-coordinate-descent loop, and scoring interfaces shared by the downstream estimation engines.
  • Physical units — mm, μm, px, dpi throughout, via FlexUnits (a pixel is a dimensionless count).

Design principles

Labels are opt-in and never viral. Coordinate-frame tags (Src/Dst on maps, slot frames on forms, frame labels on covariance) are singleton types with zero runtime cost. Unlabeled objects compose with anything; labeled junctions are checked, and a labeled mismatch is a MethodError, never a silent fall-through. One wildcard truth table governs every junction in the package. See Coordinate Frames.

Transformations have endpoints, forms have slots, rank lives on the manifold. Maps (EuclideanMap, HomographyMap) carry endpoint frames and compose with ∘; bilinear forms (EpipolarForm, CovMat/PrecMat) carry slot or frame labels and transform by their own laws (the correlation EpipolarMap composes with collineations; propagate) — the rank-2 epipolar correlation deliberately has no inv. See Epipolar Forms.

Frames, not retinas, are the type currency of imaging. Every central camera shares one spherical retina; camera models differ in the 2D frame on it that their distortion is written in. The imaging chain is a family of framed, junction-checked stages over those frames, and a lens's action free of any 2D frame is its sphere self-map ray_map. See The Framed Imaging Chain and Ray Maps and Frame Transitions.

One lens contract, kernel-lawful numerics. Every lens is two maps on rays — project and backproject, valid past 90° wherever the lens images — and every other lens map (perspective views, Jacobians, undistortion, warps) is derived from them. The maps never throw (NaN where the lens does not image), never allocate, and are precision- and Dual-generic, so the same code runs scalar, batched, and on GPU via KernelAbstractions — backends come from the array type, not from vendor packages. See Back-projection and Undistortion and Batched Maps.

Flat composition over deep hierarchies. A single Attributed{V,A} type carries every value-plus-metadata pattern; Uncertain{V,M} is its alias for a value whose attributes are a covariance or precision. See Design: Attributed{V,A}.

Installation

pkg> add VisualGeometryToolkitCore

Julia 1.12 or later; every dependency is in the General registry. Any Makie backend (pkg> add GLMakie) loads the plotting extension.

A first example

A camera views a planar board. The example projects the board's points into the image, estimates the board-to-image homography from those correspondences, and maps a circle on the board to the ellipse it images as (the README's example; the test suite runs both).

using VisualGeometryToolkitCore

# A Sony IMX174 sensor behind a lens with a 40° horizontal field of view,
# 2 m in front of the board and looking at its centre.
s = sensor("Sony", "IMX174")
f = focal_length(s, deg2rad(40.0); dimension = :horizontal)
model = CameraModel(f, s.pitch, centroid(Rect(s)) .* px)
camera = PosedCamera(model, lookat(Vec3(0.3, 0.2, -2.0) .* m, Vec3(0.0, 0.0, 0.0) .* m, Vec3(0.0, -1.0, 0.0)))

# A 5 × 5 grid of board points (metres, on z = 0) and their images (pixels).
board = [Point2(x, y) for x in -0.2:0.1:0.2 for y in -0.2:0.1:0.2]
pixels = [project(camera, Point3(p[1], p[2], 0.0) .* m) for p in board]

# The board-to-image homography, and what it does to the board.
H = HomographyMap(homography_dlt(csponds(board, pixels)))
to_euclidean(H(board[1])) ≈ pixels[1]                # true
image = transfer_ellipse(H, Circle(Point2(0.0, 0.0), 0.05))   # a 5 cm circle's image: an Ellipse (nothing past the horizon)

Module structure

  • Cameras (submodule) — camera models, sensors, projection, stereo rigs, the imaging chain, undistortion.
  • Primitives (submodule) — geometric primitives.
  • Transforms, frames, forms, solvers, robust primitives — defined in the top-level module (not submodules, due to circular dependencies).

Commonly-used names are exported from the main module; the submodule paths (VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.PosedCamera) and the exported names (PosedCamera) are equivalent.

Role in the VisualGeometryToolkit stack

VisualGeometryToolkitCore owns the contracts the robust-estimation packages compose through (see The composable RANSAC). It is consumed by VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.jl (the problems, the one RANSAC loop and the core local optimizations), RANSACScoringDoneRight.jl (the Student-t + uniform scorer and the grid selection families, implementing the scoring and pool-selection contracts), LocalOptimizationDoneRight (the paper's local optimizers, implementing the local-optimization contract), VisualGeometryToolkitEval (datasets, pools, tuning and the composition evaluator), VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures.jl (feature detection and matching), and BlobBoards.jl (fiducial blob-board targets).

All toolkit documentation is aggregated at the portal: https://visualgeometrytoolkit.github.io.
