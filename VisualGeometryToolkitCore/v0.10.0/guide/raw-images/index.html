Raw images

The sensor image of the stack lives here, in Core: the decoded mosaic, its colour phase, its photosite views, its linear values and the exact maps of every sample onto the sensor. Decoding a RAW file is VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' (load_raw_mosaic, LibRaw through LibRaw_jll), and so is the display demosaic (load_raw(path or mosaic; mode = :demosaic)). The names below are exported by Core alone (Features 0.8.0 no longer re-exports them): using VisualGeometryToolkitCore.

Julia has no convention for raw (Bayer) images: JuliaImages reads none, and BayeredImages.jl (unregistered) wraps a plain matrix as a CFAImage <: AbstractMatrix with a periodic colour filter array. The stack keeps its own types as its one raw representation, and takes one idea from BayeredImages: the raw wrapper IS an AbstractMatrix, so an ordinary array or image function works on it unchanged. There is no dependency on BayeredImages. From ImageCore (a dependency of Core) it takes its conventions and, where the concept is the same, its functions: see ImageCore's conventions.

The representation

A decoded file is a RawMosaic{A} <: AbstractMatrix{UInt16}: the photosite matrix A (an Array, a CuArray, or a view of either), its colour pattern anchored at the full raw frame's origin, the visible area's origin in that frame, the black and white levels and the decoder's metadata.

  • size, getindex, IndexStyle and parent are the held array's.
  • What an array function makes from it is a plain array of the held array's kind: similar, copy, a broadcast (m .- black), a reduction (sum, extrema). The colour pattern and the metadata stay with the mosaic, and nothing pretends a derived array still has them.
  • A unit-range index m[rows, columns] (a copy) or view(m, rows, columns) (no copy) is a window: a RawMosaic of the same sensor whose window_origin is its offset into the visible area. Its colours are the parent's at those photosites, whatever the parity of its start (the phase shift BayeredImages makes with a circshift of the pattern is the offset here, added where the pattern is tiled). Any other index is a plain array.
  • On a device: adapt(CuArray, m) or adapt(backend, m) (a KernelAbstractions backend) moves the photosites and keeps the rest. A kernel receives parent(m) or the mosaic's channelview, never the wrapper (its metadata is not a device value).

The photosites channel-first: channelview

channelview(m) — a method of ImageCore's channelview for a mosaic — is the photosites channel-first, 4 × H/2 × W/2: each 2×2 Bayer cell is a pixel, and its four photosites, spatially interleaved, are its channels. Photosite (2i − 1 + di, 2j − 1 + dj) is v[di + 2dj + 1, i, j], the cell's photosites in column-major order, whose colours are bayer_channels(m) — (:R, :G1, :G2, :B) for an RGGB cell, (:G1, :B, :R, :G2) for GRBG.

It is built as ImageCore builds its views, with nothing copied: the photosite matrix reshaped H × W → 2 × H/2 × 2 × W/2, its dimensions permuted to 2 × 2 × H/2 × W/2 (a PermutedDimsArray), and reshaped to 4 × H/2 × W/2. A view of a device mosaic is a view of its device array: KernelAbstractions' get_backend is the device's, adapt(Array, v) moves it whole, and a broadcast over a whole mosaic's view runs on the device. A GPU kernel reads the photosites by the view's index rule instead, parent(m)[2i − 1 + di, 2j − 1 + dj], as the cell kernel of LinearRaw does: measured on a B200 (2848 × 4256, the cell kernel, benchmark/raw_channelview.jl),

Base's ReshapedArray indexing splits each index by multiplicative inverses, and over a nested wrapper (a window's SubArray inside) it does not infer in a CUDA kernel. Its inverse, photositeview, turns a 4 × h × w array of cells back into the 2h × 2w photosite matrix — of a mosaic's view, the held array itself.

Why the channels are in the cell's own order and not always R, G1, G2, B: reordering channels per phase is a gather, not a reshape or a permutation of dimensions, so it cannot be a view. The colour of each channel is one call away, bayer_channels(m), from the same phase rule as photosite_colors.

Each channel is a lattice of its own, sampled at its photosite's position in the cell: its frame is BayerCellFrame{C} (C its colour) and bayer_plane_map(m, k) its isbits SimilarityMap onto the sensor (x ↦ 2x plus the photosite's offset from the cell centre and a window's offset), exact for every Bayer phase and either parity of the visible or the window origin. The photosites' linear values channel-first are channelview(linearview(m)) (next section): the one linearisation, the one the cell kernel applies.

v = channelview(m)                      # 4 × H/2 × W/2, a view of parent(m)
g1 = findfirst(==(:G1), bayer_channels(m))
S = bayer_plane_map(m, g1)              # G₁'s lattice → the sensor's PixelFrame{:colmap}
x = S(pixel_centre(CartesianIndex(i, j)))   # where G₁ of cell (i, j) sits on the sensor
y = channelview(linearview(m))[g1, i, j]   # its linear value

Linear values on the raw grid: linearview

linearview(m) is the mosaic's linear values on the raw grid, lazily, as ImageCore's normedview reads codes as numbers: element (i, j) is (raw − black[c]) / (white − black[c]) of photosite (i, j), c its colour (the black and white levels are the mosaic's fields m.black, per colour index, and m.white). It is the one public spelling of the linearisation. It composes with channelview: channelview(linearview(m)) is the linear values channel-first, and the green-cell image is the mean of its two G channels, bit for bit. A broadcast over it, and copy, run on the mosaic's device, and a kernel reads it as it is. photosite_colors(m) is lazy and isbits (the pattern and its phase, indexed by the one rule), so it fuses into a broadcast with a device mosaic's arrays and runs on the device. saturationview(m) is the linear view's Bool match: the saturation flags on the same grid, lazily, by the one rule raw ≥ white that LinearRaw's flags apply. A photosite mask is a plain broadcast over photosite_colors — the usable greens of a radiometric fit are ((col .== 0x01) .| (col .== 0x03)) .& .!saturationview(m) with col = photosite_colors(m) — so no mask helper exists.

Linear values on the cells

A LinearRaw{Mode} (:green_cells, :bin2, :demosaic) stays a container of plain planes, L.image[row, column, plane], beside its saturation flags, geometry, gain and metadata, and is THE public path to the green-cell image: LinearRaw(m; mode = :green_cells) (its image[:, :, 1]), on the mosaic's device. Its cell images are the mosaic's linearview read channel-first: the green-cell value is the mean of the two green channels, times the gain, bit for bit (a test holds it), and :bin2's R and B planes are the R and B channels. The planes are not wrappers that each know their BayerCellFrame{C}, for three reasons:

  1. Every caller reads the planes as one array: a warp reads all of them in one call (image_warp!(dst, warps, L.image), a tuple of one warp per plane), the decode stage writes L.image as one PNG, a detection image takes a view of one plane, and a quantile reads vec(L.image). A tuple of plane wrappers would split that array or make each caller reassemble it.
  2. A plane's frame is already one call away and needs nothing new: bayer_plane_map(L, c) and raw_frame_map(L) (the green plane, the cell grid).
  3. The saturation flags, the gain and the green code spans belong to the cell, not to one plane; per-plane wrappers would hold copies of them or point back at the container.

The mosaic, by contrast, is one matrix, and the wrapper costs its callers nothing.

One phase rule

Every colour, offset and map above comes from one rule in src/raw/raw_mosaic.jl: the colour of a photosite is the pattern tiled from the raw origin, shifted by a window's offset (photosite_colors); a cell's four photosites are taken in column-major order; and photosite k sits at its 0-based site's pixel centre from the cell centre, (dj − ½, di − ½) raw pixels. A lattice of samples at offset o from every cell centre maps onto the sensor by the cell grid's x ↦ 2x after its translation by o/2 cell pixels. channelview's channels, bayer_plane_map of a mosaic and of a LinearRaw, green_cell_sites and the cell kernel all read that rule; the R channel's map IS the :bin2 R plane's.

Where an image's sensor-pixel coordinates come from

ONE accessor: raw_coordinates(img, x), the point x of the image's pixel frame (COLMAP: pixel (row, column) centred at (column − ½, row − ½)) in the visible raw's PixelFrame{:colmap}, the frame every calibration of the raw is in. It is raw_frame_map(img)(x):

A position on another plane of a :bin2 image is bayer_plane_map(L, c)(x), and on a channel of a mosaic's channelview it is bayer_plane_map(m, k)(x): the planes are sampled at different points of the cell, so each has its own frame, and a composition that joins two planes' frames is refused. A mask over a plane's pixels of a region on the sensor is pixel_mask(region, bayer_plane_map(m, k), w, h): each pixel's mapped centre tested in the region's own frame. A region moves between frames by the map itself (S(rect), S(a ∩ b)).

How detectors report positions

A detector works in the pixels of the image it is given and returns COLMAP pixel centres of that image. A caller that runs it on a raw image and writes positions as raw pixels maps them through the image first:

L = load_raw(path; mode = :green_cells)        # VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures
xs = detect(L.image[:, :, 1])                  # any detector: COLMAP centres of the cell grid
raw_xs = raw_coordinates.(Ref(L), xs)          # the sensor's pixels

Writing the cell-grid positions as raw pixels unmapped puts them at half the scale; RadiometricCamera's chrome detection did exactly that with green-cell coordinates. A calibration matrix moves the same way: reframed_calibration(raw_frame_map(L), K_cells).

How the frame survives a file round trip

A file of a decoded image (a PNG of the cells) holds pixels and no frame, and a plain matrix read back from it has no raw_coordinates. The stage that writes it records the frame with it: raw_provenance(L) gives the decoder, the mode, the gain and the frame map as plain data (from, to, scale, translation), for the stage's record (RadiometricCamera's conversion.json). The stage that reads the file rebuilds the map from that record and maps every position it writes through it.

ImageCore's conventions

ImageCore is a dependency of Core, and the sensor image follows it where the concept is the same, by its own functions:

ImageCoreherewhy
channelview, channel-first C × H × Wa method, channelview(::RawMosaic), 4 × H/2 × W/2the same concept: the channels of each pixel, first, as a view
views, not copieschannelview(m), photositeview, windows by viewthe same rule
colorview (the inverse of channelview)photositeview, a name of its owncolorview(C, A) makes colorants of channels; the inverse here makes the photosite matrix of cells, no colorant
rawviewnot extendeda mosaic IS its raw UInt16 codes: there is nothing to unwrap
normedview(N0f16, A)usable on parent(m) as ImageCore defines itit is code / 65535; the linear value subtracts each channel's black level and divides by its span, so it has its own view, linearview

LinearRaw{:bin2}'s image stays H × W × 3 (planes last) in this release: moving it channel-first is a breaking change to a stored layout, left for a breaking release (Core's CHANGELOG, 0.9.2).
