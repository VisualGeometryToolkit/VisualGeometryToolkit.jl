VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras — Module
Cameras

Camera system submodule for VisualGeometryToolkitCore.

Provides complete camera modeling including:

  • Sensors: Physical CMOS sensor specifications
  • Intrinsics: Calibration matrices and intrinsic parameters (physical/logical)
  • Lens Models: forward lenses (Lens: a projection + a distortion in its frame) and lifts (LiftLens)
  • Camera Models: Composable camera models (intrinsics + lens)
  • Cameras: Complete cameras in 3D space (model + pose)
  • Stereo: Stereo camera rigs and epipolar geometry
  • Utilities: Helper functions (focal_length, lookat, P3P solvers)

  • PosedCamera, PosedPinholeCamera
  • CameraModel
  • LogicalIntrinsics

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.BoundedCamera — Type
BoundedCamera{C<:AbstractCamera, B, R} <: AbstractCamera

A wrapper around any AbstractCamera that adds pixel-space image bounds (width × height) without disturbing the underlying camera's intrinsics or units. Used by SfM/MVS dataset readers (ETH3D, ScanNet, COLMAP exports) where the camera is described by its calibration plus an explicit image extent — information the bare PosedCamera type doesn't retain.

The wrapped camera may be UNPOSED — a bare model, is_posed(bc) == false — which is COLMAP's "camera" row (intrinsics, lens and image size, no pose) and the element of a stored camera collection (VisualGeometryToolkitIO's cameras kind). BoundedCamera(PosedCamera(model(bc), pose), bc.bound; lens_coverage = lens_coverage(bc)) is its posed counterpart; the pose accessors of an unposed one are an ArgumentError. is_posed is the wrapped camera's.

The bound

BoundedCamera(camera, extent::Size2) bounds by a pixel extent; BoundedCamera(camera, sensor::Sensor) by the physical sensor, whose resolution is the extent and whose pitch (a length per pixel) carries a lens datasheet's millimetres into pixels (image_circle). Either way extent(bc) is the Size2{Int} pixel extent; sensor(bc) is the Sensor, or nothing for the extent form. The bound is ONE field, bc.bound: the extent, or the sensor whose resolution is the extent.

The lens coverage

BoundedCamera(camera, bound; lens_coverage = region) — where light reaches the sensor, in pixels: a Circle or an Ellipse (the lens's image circle, measured with a detector or from a datasheet), or a Rect2; any real element type, converted to Float64, and kept as the kind given. Omitted, it is the sensor's own Rect: the whole sensor is lit. lens_coverage(bc) reads it, with_lens_coverage replaces it. What the camera images is imaged_region(bc): the sensor ∩ the lens coverage ∩ where the lens model is valid.

Field access

  • bc.camera, bc.bound, bc.lens_coverage — direct fields; read them through model, extent/sensor and lens_coverage.

Method delegation

A BoundedCamera is THE type for pixel operations: every camera verb a CameraModel answers is forwarded to the wrapped camera (MacroTools.@forward, and @inline project/backproject for device kernels) — the maps and their Jacobians (project, backproject, backproject_with_jacobian, unproject, is_in_front, constraint_with_jacobians), the accessors (intrinsics, lens, pose, extrinsics, focal_length, model, calibration_matrix), the imaging and its domain (imaging_transform, imaging_chain, distortion_map, valid_domain, valid_rect, undistort, …) and the camera-derived solvers (p3p, transfer, radial_homography); so backproject_with_jacobian(bc, u) == backproject_with_jacobian(bc.camera, u). The bound applies to none of them (the extent is image_circle, imaged_region and lens_coverage). The model constructors (pinhole, window, with_parameters) and the parameter protocol are the model's: call them on model(bc), and rebound with with_model.

Trait

bounds_trait(::BoundedCamera) = HasBounds() — methods that need image extent dispatch on BoundsTrait: extent(bc) is the bound's pixel extent.

A device kernel's camera

The bound's kind and the region's are the type parameters B (Size2{Int}, or the stored Sensor type) and R (Rect2{Float64}, Circle{Float64} or Ellipse{Float64}), so a bounded camera is concrete, and isbits whenever the wrapped camera is (a CameraModel of an isbits lens): it travels into a GPU kernel as it is, its backproject/project the wrapped camera's. convert_eltype(Float32, bc) converts the wrapped camera and keeps the bound and the region.

Example

inner = PosedCamera(CameraModel(K; lens=Pinhole()), pose)
bc    = BoundedCamera(inner, Size2(6048, 4032))
intrinsics(bc)     # forwarded → CameraCalibrationMat
extent(bc)         # Size2(6048, 4032)
lens_coverage(bc)     # Rect2(0, 0, 6048, 4032): the whole sensor is lit

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.OrientedCamera — Type
OrientedCamera{M}

A camera that only turns about its centre: a PosedCamera whose extrinsics are a RotationMap{3}, PosedCamera{M, <:RotationMap{3}} (an alias, not a type of its own). It has an orientation and no position: extrinsics(c) is the world → camera rotation, pose(c) its inverse, and project, backproject and unproject act about the origin. Build it from the rotation of either direction: PosedCamera(model, relabel(CameraFrame, WorldFrame, RotationMap(R))) takes a camera → world orientation and inverts it, an unlabeled or world → camera RotationMap is stored as the extrinsics. What needs a position refuses one by name: a rig member, the plane-induced homography and the verbs on plane coordinates (project of a plane conic or rectangle, transfer_ellipse, HomographyMat, RadialCamera), and a non-central grid model's pose. The frames of a panorama are oriented cameras, as view_overlap takes them.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.PosedCamera — Type
PosedCamera{M, Rt} <: AbstractCamera

A camera in 3D space, consisting of a camera model (intrinsics + lens) and extrinsics (pose).

Dispatch on the lens through the model parameter: PosedCamera{<:CameraModel{<:Any, <:MyLens}} (the PosedPinholeCamera alias is that form for Pinhole).

Use project(camera, X) to map a 3D world point to 2D pixel coordinates, and backproject(camera, u) / unproject(camera, u, depth) for the inverse directions. The transform is computed on demand and inlined.

Type parameters

  • M <: AbstractCamera: an UNPOSED camera model (CameraModel, or a grid model); PosedCamera(model, X) refuses a posed one (is_posed(model)) and a BoundedCamera — the bound goes outside the pose, BoundedCamera(PosedCamera(model, X), extent)
  • Rt <: Union{EuclideanMap, RotationMap{3}}: extrinsics type. A RotationMap poses a camera that only turns about its centre, an OrientedCamera; a camera turns in three dimensions, so a RotationMap{2} is a MethodError at construction.

Fields

  • model::M: Camera model (intrinsics + lens)
  • extrinsics::Rt: Camera extrinsics (world-to-camera transform)

Examples

# Create camera with logical intrinsics
model = CameraModel(800.0px, [320.0px, 240.0px])
extrinsics = EuclideanMap(RotMatrix(I), [0.0, 0.0, 0.0])
camera = PosedCamera(model, extrinsics)

# Dispatch on lens (via the model parameter; PosedPinholeCamera is the
# ready-made alias for the Pinhole case)
foo(::PosedPinholeCamera) = "pinhole path"
foo(::PosedCamera{<:CameraModel{<:AbstractIntrinsics, <:KannalaBrandt}}) = "fisheye path"

# Project a world point
X_world = SVector(1.0mm, 2.0mm, 10.0mm)
u = project(camera, X_world)

PosedCamera(model::CameraModel, p::CameraPose) -> PosedCamera

Construct a PosedCamera from a frame-labeled map, so the caller states which frame it holds instead of relying on the positional convention.

The bare positional constructor PosedCamera(model, extrinsics::EuclideanMap) with an UNLABELED map takes the world-to-camera map (documented convention, unchecked); a caller holding a camera-to-world map (a lookat-style pose, a renderer's sensor.to_world) who supplies it positionally stores the WRONG map without any error — every accessor then silently answers for the inverse camera. VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.plane_map — Method
plane_map(camera::PosedCamera) -> CoordinateTransformations.Transformation

The board-plane → pixel map of a posed camera, for every camera model: the z = 0 plane of its world (a planar target's design plane, in its units) lifted to 3-D (q ↦ (q, 0), the target surfaces' plane lift), the camera's rigid extrinsics, then its model's projection, project(camera.model, ·) as a map (the forward twin of Backprojection). plane_map(camera)(q) is project(camera, [q; 0]), and transform_deriv(plane_map(camera), q) its 2 × 2 Jacobian with respect to the plane point, the composition's chain rule (the projection's by ForwardDiff through project, a spline model's by the implicit function theorem).

For a central CameraModel the same map, on the plane points in front of the camera, is the plane-induced homography of its pinhole twin, dehomogenised, then its lens's distortion_map composition (a pinhole: the homography map alone, PerspectiveMap() ∘ HomographyMap(camera)), and plane_map returns that form: it is the composition transfer_ellipse(camera, shape) transfers a plane conic through, stage by stage, so a conic's image is the homography's exact conic action, then the lens's local affinity. A plane point behind the camera (Z ≤ 0) has no image (project is NaN or a ray past 90°), and the homography form does not test it. A SplineLens camera whose lattice does not image ẑ (the off-axis gauge, which distortion_map refuses for an undistortion) takes the same form: the gauge's K cancels between the homography and the lens stage, so the map is the lens's own projection of the plane point.

The camera's extrinsics are plain numbers in the plane's unit: a physical camera's plain form is ustrip(m, camera).

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.CameraModel — Method
CameraModel(model::CameraModel, K′::CameraCalibrationMat) -> CameraModel

The same camera with intrinsics K′: the lens re-expressed under the new intrinsics matrix, so the pixel map is unchanged. A parametric lens shares the principal point: normalized′ = normalized / s with s = f′/f, so a scale-covariant radial lens transforms its coefficients by the law aᵢ → aᵢ·s^(2i), bⱼ → bⱼ·s^(2j) (the generalized k′ = k·s²); it throws for a moved principal point (the lens center is data, not a frame choice), for anisotropic rescaling of a radial lens (radial symmetry would be broken), and for families whose coefficients are only meaningful under a metric K. A SplineLens takes any skew-0 K′, a moved principal point included (its K is a gauge): its backward area moves by the change of the normalized plane, x′ = x / s + (c − c′)/f′, and so does a forward's output (its chart is about ẑ, which no K moves), so every projection is kept too.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.backproject_with_jacobian — Method
backproject_with_jacobian(model::CameraModel, u::StaticVector{2}) -> (f, M)

The unit bearing of pixel u and its 3×2 Jacobian M = ∂f/∂u, in ONE evaluation. f is what backproject returns; M is what propagates a PIXEL covariance onto the sphere, so the pair is the entry point for calibrated two-view geometry on bearings (the tangent Sampson residual and its rows).

M is TANGENT by construction: fᵀM = 0 to rounding — a forward lens's is built in a tangent basis, and a backward lens's is the derivative of its unit bearing. Consumers therefore need no explicit projection onto the tangent plane — which is what keeps an inner loop free of the allocation such a projection would cost.

The tangency assertion is LOAD-BEARING — do not simplify it away

fᵀM = 0 is not decorative, and it is not merely an internal consistency check. It is the ONLY test in the suite that catches a correspondence whose two views' Jacobians have been SWAPPED — M₂ handed where M₁ belongs, the natural mistake when porting a residual onto bearings.

Nothing else sees it, and that is measured rather than assumed (test/essential_tangent_mc_tests.jl). The swap is very nearly norm-preserving: the two halves of the tangent Sampson's G_x are scaled by |Eᵀf₂| and |Ef₁|, whose ratio has median 1.018 and 5–95% range [0.972, 1.066], so ‖G_x‖ barely moves and the residual stays plausible. Every Monte Carlo calibration cell keeps reporting NEES ≈ 1 — the best of them gets to 0.970, inside its own 4 SE band. Tangency, by contrast, fails loudly: fᵀM measures 4.8e-19 with the right Jacobian against 1.0e-3 with the swapped one, a factor of 2e15.

So a future cleanup that reads the tangency assertion as redundant with the statistical gates would delete the one check that catches a view swap, and the statistical gates would go on succeeding while it did.

Construction, given that a camera's forward map is the differentiable one: with B an orthonormal basis of T_f S² (s2_tangent_basis, shared with EssentialManifold5 rather than re-derived) and A = ∂project(model, ·)/∂f · B the 2×2 restriction of the projection differential to that plane, M = B·A⁻¹. The projection differential is taken by automatic differentiation through the camera's OWN project, so every forward lens — Kannala-Brandt, double sphere, the OpenCV models — is covered without a per-model derivation, the same derivation-risk-free stance retract_jacobian takes. A BACKWARD lens (a LiftLens — division, Scaramuzza — or a SplineLens), whose back-projection is the closed form and whose projection a solve, instead differentiates backproject itself: M = ∂backproject(lens, x)/∂x · ∂x/∂u at x = K⁻¹u, by the same automatic differentiation, or analytically where the lens has its own (SplineLens).

This is PoseLib's unproject_with_jac; GTSAM's nearest relative is PinholeBase::backprojectPointAtInfinity(p, Dresult_dpose, Dresult_dpoint), which returns a Unit3. GTSAM spells the pixel → calibrated step calibrate, which collides with THIS stack's task verb calibrate (estimating a camera), so the name here stays backproject.

Returns (NaN bearing, NaN Jacobian) wherever backproject does — outside the lens's domain, past a fold, or where the tangent restriction A is singular (a ray the camera cannot resolve in two directions). Never throws.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.horizon_radius — Method

The PIXEL radius of the camera's horizon: the circle at which rays reach the lens's monotone limit, beyond which nothing can be placed on a perspective plane at all. Inf for a lens with no such limit (a pinhole, a mild polynomial). This is the circle plot_rectification draws on the raw panel, and the guide for how much of a frame it is meaningful to rectify.

undistort(model::CameraModel{<:AbstractIntrinsics, <:AbstractLensModel}, img)

Remove lens distortion from an image.

For each output (undistorted) pixel, computes the corresponding source (distorted) pixel through model.lens and resamples. Uses VGC's image_warp builder to bake pixel coordinate conventions into a BoundedMap (K is assumed to be in COLMAP convention: image corner at origin, pixel centers at 0.5).

The in-place undistort! runs on dst's backend.

Undistorted image of the same size and element type.

The angle of view of a bounded camera: the FULL angle, in radians, of the scene it IMAGES in dimension — its pixel extent, bounded by its lens_coverage (where the lens covers the sensor) — read through the camera's own backproject (K⁻¹, then the lens), so a distorted or fisheye lens answers its true angle and an anisotropic K uses the focal length of that axis.   • :horizontal: the two edge points on the principal point's row, (0, c_y) and (width, c_y);
  • :vertical: the two on its column, (c_x, 0) and (c_x, height);
  • :diagonal: the four corners of the extent.

The answer is twice the SMALLEST of those points' view angles — the nearer edge when the principal point is off-centre, so the cone of that full angle about the optical axis is imaged in that dimension. A SplineLens camera reads about its gauge principal point, the pixel imaging ẑ, so the angles are about the optical axis.

The overlap of a rotating camera's views

view_overlap measures, for every pair of frames of a camera turning about its centre, the solid angle of the directions both image and its share of each frame's footprint: the overlap a panorama's pair selection reads.

VisualGeometryToolkitCore.Cameras.view_overlap — Method
view_overlap(cameras; boundary_points = 512) -> StructVector
view_overlap(a, b; boundary_points = 512) -> NamedTuple

The directions two frames of a camera turning about its centre both image, for every pair i < j of cameras: one row (; i, j, solid_angle, fraction_i, fraction_j) per pair, K(K − 1)/2 rows in the order (1, 2), (1, 3), …, (2, 3), … as a StructVector, so rows.solid_angle is a column. solid_angle is the solid angle (sr) of the intersection of the two frames' footprints on the sphere; fraction_i is it over frame i's own footprint, and fraction_j over frame j's. A pair of cameras a, b gives the one row, i = 1, j = 2. For a panorama's pair selection: a pair worth matching shares a fraction of its view.

Each camera is a BoundedCamera of an OrientedCamera, a frame posed by its orientation alone, BoundedCamera(PosedCamera(model, relabel(CameraFrame, WorldFrame, RotationMap(R_k))), extent) for a camera → world rotation R_k, d_world = R_k · d_camera, whose model is a CameraModel (another model, a grid one, is an ArgumentError). Frames may have different cameras. A camera posed by an EuclideanMap has a position, and an unposed one no orientation: both are an ArgumentError naming the fix. Frame k's footprint is the set of directions R_k · backproject(model, u) over its region Ωk, [`imagedregion](@ref)(camera): the sensor ∩ the camera's lens coverage (its image circle, [lenscoverage](@ref)) ∩ where the lens model is valid ([validdomain`](@ref)). Each frame carries its own lens coverage; a camera whose whole sensor is lit gives the extent where the lens model holds, which is not where light falls for a lens whose image circle lies inside the sensor.

The pair (i, j) is computed once, in frame i's image:

  1. Ωi is a ring of `boundarypoints` pixels along its boundary: the extent's edges (every corner exact), the circle's samples, and their convex intersection by Core's Sutherland–Hodgman.
  2. Pj is frame j's ring drawn into image i: each pixel back-projected by j, rotated by `Riᵀ Rjand projected byi, each edge split into geodesic pieces no longer in imagei` than Ωi's own edges. A direction frame i does not image (behind a perspective camera, past a lens's fold, NaN, or one whose pixel does not back-project to it within 10⁻⁶ rad) has no pixel; the ring is cut there and continued along a far circle about i's principal point, four times Ωi's radius, at the direction's azimuth, so it stays a closed curve that runs outside Ωi wherever i cannot draw it.
  3. Sutherland–Hodgman clips Pj by the convex Ωi. A curve of any shape keeps its winding number inside the clip, so the clipped ring's area is the integral of Pj's winding number over Ωi; pieces a non-convex Pj splits into come back joined by edges along Ωi's boundary, traversed once each way, which add nothing.
  4. That area is a solid angle: the clipped ring's vertices are back-projected by i to unit bearings and joined by geodesics, and their fan of signed spherical triangles from i's optical axis is summed (Van Oosterom & Strackee, tan(E/2) = a·(b × c) / (1 + a·b + b·c + c·a)). Along Ω_i's boundary the polygon follows the footprint's own geodesic edges: a vertex there is moved onto its edge's great circle, and a clipped edge along a sensor side gets the ring's vertices between its ends (a lens bends the side).
  5. The detours of item 2 are where the winding can be wrong, and only by an integer n constant over Ωi: a detour outside Ωi can only wind around all of it. One interior point of Ωi, the candidate farthest from Pj, fixes n: its winding number against whether frame j images its direction (project by j, in Ωj, back-projected within 10⁻⁶ rad). The overlap is the area less n times frame i's footprint. The same correction covers a footprint j that wraps frame i's backward direction (a wide fisheye behind a narrow lens), whose ring winds the other way round Ωi.

Each footprint's own solid angle is the same fan over its ring from its own optical axis, which needs no hemisphere: the sum is the enclosed area for any apex whose antipode is outside the region, and a footprint never reaches its camera's backward axis. So a lens past 180° (an 8 mm fisheye) is handled; a footprint whose ring comes within 0.05 rad (≈ 3°) of its camera's backward axis is an ArgumentError, as is a region whose boundary the lens cannot back-project and a principal point outside its region.

A perspective camera maps geodesics to lines, so its footprints are exact great-circle polygons and their overlap is exact at any boundary_points. A curved boundary (an image circle, a distorted side) is a polygon of boundary_points vertices, so the error is the chords' sag, O(1/boundary_points²): 1.6 × 10⁻⁵ sr of a 0.77 sr cap at 512 (2 × 10⁻⁵ relative), a quarter of it at 1024. A footprint that coincides with another (the same camera and rotation) clips to itself: fraction 1. A pair whose footprints' bounding caps about the optical axes are disjoint is 0 without clipping.
