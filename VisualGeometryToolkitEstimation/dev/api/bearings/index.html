Pose and Rotation from Directions

Estimators whose data are DIRECTIONS — unit bearings from a camera's back-projection, star or gravity directions — rather than pixels. They work on the sphere throughout (VisualGeometryToolkitCore conventions/bearings.md), so rays at and past 90° from the optical axis, as a fisheye or omnidirectional camera sees them, are ordinary data. (A camera pose from a guess and pixels is refine_absolute_pose(pose, camera, pairs), on the PnP problem's pixel rows — P3P and Absolute Pose.)

Camera pose from bearings

refine_absolute_pose(pose, cs) with cs world points paired with UNIT bearings is the pose of a camera the caller back-projects itself — a grid or non-central lens the stack holds no CameraModel of — on the tangent-plane residual of the sphere, optionally from per-ray origins (a generalized camera) and whitened into pixels by the back-projection Jacobians. Its problem:

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.BearingPoseProblem — Type
BearingPoseProblem(cs; sampler = nothing)

The absolute-pose problem on unit BEARINGS: cs pairs world points with the camera-frame unit bearings observing them, X => f (a Csponds, a StructArray or a Pair vector), for a camera the caller back-projects itself. The model is the camera extrinsics (world → camera, x_c = R·X + t, the Extrinsics3 of AbsolutePoseProblem); the residual is the tangent- plane logarithm map of the sphere at f of the predicted bearing normalize(R·X + t − o), its norm the angle between the rays.

Optional attribute COLUMNS of cs:

  • origins — each ray's origin in the camera frame: a GENERALIZED (non-central) camera. Such a problem is refine-only: there is no generalized minimal solver, so solve refuses it.
  • M — each bearing's back-projection Jacobian (3 × 2, from backproject_with_jacobian(camera, u)), tangent (fᵀM = 0, checked): the residuals are whitened into PIXELS, (BᵀM)⁻¹·r; without it they are radians.
  • weights — fixed per-correspondence weights (finite, non-negative) on the refit rows; the scoring residual is unweighted.

A bearing of norm ≠ 1 (a z = 1 chart point) is an ArgumentError, as is a non-finite point, origin or bearing. The public estimator is the bearing form of refine_absolute_pose.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.BearingPoseContext — Type
BearingPoseContext

The data of a BearingPoseProblem beside its correspondences: per correspondence the ray origin in the camera frame (zero for a central camera), the observation's tangent basis, the whitening (I₂, or (BᵀM)⁻¹ into pixels) and the caller's fixed √weight.

Rotation from direction pairs

fit_rotation is the robust rotation R with b ≈ R·a from direction pairs with gross outliers: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's kabsch, reweighted by a stack loss shape at a width that follows the robust scale of the residual angles.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_rotation — Function
fit_rotation(cs; loss, scale_multiple = nothing, max_iter, tol)
    -> FitResult{RotMatrix{3, Float64}, RotationFitMetadata}

The robust rotation R with bᵢ ≈ R·aᵢ from direction pairs cs (aᵢ => bᵢ, 3-vectors; normalized at entry, so any nonzero length is a direction), with a fraction of the pairs gross outliers. A fit — it seeds itself with VisualGeometryToolkitCore's kabsch — iterating a reweighted Kabsch: residual angles rᵢ = ∠(R·aᵢ, bᵢ), the robust scale σ̂ = median(r) / √chisq_median(2) (the median of a 2-D residual norm, over the pairs of positive prior weight), the weights of loss (an AbstractLossShape, e.g. HuberShape()) at width scale_multiple · σ̂, and the weighted Kabsch.

  • loss — the robust loss shape (default the TOML's, Huber).
  • scale_multiple — the width as a multiple of σ̂; nothing takes the shape's efficiency constant at codimension 2 (deploy's default), which only an M-estimator (AbstractMEstimator) has: any other shape (TLSShape()) requires it, an ArgumentError naming scale_multiple otherwise.
  • max_iter, tol — at most max_iter iterations; converged when one moves R by less than tol radians (defaults: configs/default.toml [fit_rotation]).
  • A weights column on cs (withattrs(cs; weights)) multiplies every pair's loss weight (fixed prior weights).

retcode is :converged, :max_iterations, or :degenerate when fewer than two pairs keep a positive weight; on exact data (every residual zero) the seed is returned, :converged after one iteration. rms is the root mean square residual angle over the n pairs (outliers included); the metadata carries the weights, the residual angles and σ̂ at the returned rotation.

Accuracy: with a minority of gross outliers the estimate reaches the noise floor of the inliers' own least-squares rotation (≈ σ/√m for m inliers at direction noise σ); the suite pins 20% random outliers among 200 pairs at σ = 1e-3 rad to within 5e-4 rad, where the plain Kabsch is off by over 1e-2.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RotationFitMetadata — Type
RotationFitMetadata

FitResult metadata of fit_rotation, all at the returned rotation: weights, the loss's IRLS weight of every pair (times its prior weight); residuals, the angle ∠(R·aᵢ, bᵢ) of every pair in radians; sigma, the robust noise scale σ̂ (radians) the final weights were computed at. They forward as properties: result.weights, result.residuals, result.sigma.

fit_rotation_averaging is the absolute rotations of a set of frames from the relative rotations of a view graph, some of them wrong: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's spectral seed, then a reweighted first-order Lie-algebra least squares over every edge (Chatterjee & Govindu), each a sparse linear solve.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_rotation_averaging — Function
fit_rotation_averaging(relative[, prior]; loss, scale_multiple = nothing, max_iter, tol)
    -> FitResult{Dict{K, RotMatrix{3, Float64}}, RotationAveragingMetadata{K}}

Robust rotation averaging on a view graph: the absolute rotations R_k of the frames (d = R_k·f_k, every frame's directions into the common frame, the first of the sorted frames the identity — the gauge) from the relative rotations relative, an AbstractDict keyed by frame pairs (i, j) whose value R_ij takes frame i's directions into frame j's (f_j = R_ij·f_i, so R_i = R_j·R_ij), some of them wrong. A fit: it seeds itself with VisualGeometryToolkitCore's spectral_rotation_averaging and iterates a reweighted first-order Lie-algebra least squares over every edge (Chatterjee & Govindu 2013), each a sparse linear solve.

  • prior — fixed non-negative edge weights (an AbstractDict with the same keys: inlier counts); all ones by default. A zero prior drops its edge: the edges of positive prior must connect every frame (an ArgumentError otherwise — a zero-prior bridge splits the graph), and an all-zero prior is refused.
  • loss, scale_multiple — the robust loss shape (default the TOML's) and its width as a multiple of σ̂ (the median of the residual angles over √chisq_median(3)); nothing takes the shape's efficiency constant at codimension 3, which only an M-estimator has.
  • max_iter, tol — at most max_iter iterations; converged when one moves every frame by less than tol radians (configs/default.toml [fitrotationaveraging]).

When the loss's weights drop every edge of a frame (a redescending loss past its cutoff), that frame is held at its current rotation for the iteration rather than left free in a singular solve; more generally, in each part of the graph that the edges of positive weight no longer tie to the gauge frame, the first sorted frame is held and the rest move with it.

retcode is :converged or :max_iterations; rms the root mean square residual angle, and n the count, over the edges of positive prior. A disconnected graph is an ArgumentError (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's seed refuses it, counting only the edges of positive prior).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RotationAveragingMetadata — Type
RotationAveragingMetadata{K}

FitResult metadata of fit_rotation_averaging at the returned rotations: per edge (i, j), residuals the angle (radians) of the residual rotation R_j·R_ij·R_iᵀ and weights the loss's IRLS weight times the edge's prior weight; sigma the robust scale σ̂ (radians). They forward as properties.
