Essential Matrix

RANSAC Problem

EssentialProblem is a type alias for MinimalSolveProblem{EssentialMat{T}, 5, T, ...} — a calibrated two-view problem with the 5-point minimal solver, on UNIT BEARINGS (3-vectors), not normalized 2-vectors. Build it from pixels and the two cameras, EssentialProblem(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs), which back-projects each view with its own camera and attaches the back-projection Jacobians M₁/M₂ that keep residuals and the threshold in PIXELS.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.EssentialProblem — Type
EssentialProblem(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs; kw...)

Build an EssentialProblem from PIXEL correspondences and the two cameras that produced them — the entry most callers want, and the only one that cannot get the frames wrong.

Each view is back-projected with its OWN camera through backproject_with_jacobian, so M₁ belongs with the source bearings and M₂ with the targets by construction rather than by the caller remembering. That matters more than it looks: swapping the two is norm-preserving and invisible to Monte-Carlo calibration, and shows only in the tangency contract.

Two cameras, not one, because they are genuinely independent — and because a single-camera convenience form would make the swap undetectable in exactly the tests that ought to catch it.

EssentialProblem(bearings; sampler=nothing, backend=NativeSolver())

Construct an EssentialProblem from unit-bearing correspondences: 3-vector rays on S², carrying their back-projection Jacobians as the attribute columns M₁/M₂ (withattrs(cs; M₁, M₂), from backproject_with_jacobian).

Differs from FundMatProblem in coordinate space — bearings, not pixels — and in minimal sample size (5 vs 7).

Why 3-vectors, and why the length is enforced

The old contract took calibrated 2-vectors and implied z = 1. That is the gnomonic chart, and it does not exist for a ray at or past the horizon — which is exactly the rays a fisheye or omnidirectional camera produces. Bearings have no such gap.

The length is a type constraint, not a convention, because the failure it prevents is silent. A consumer that indexes components 1 and 2 of a bearing and appends 1.0 gets a nearly right answer whenever z ≈ 1, so it degrades with field of view rather than failing — the same signature as the defect this contract removes. A wrong-length input is therefore a MethodError here, and there is deliberately no method accepting either length.

Why M₁/M₂ are required rather than defaulted

M = ∂f/∂u is what carries the gate's and the scoring's tolerance into PIXELS: test_sample converts an angular separation as angle / σ_max(M). M picks the unit and the call cannot — an orthonormal tangent basis would put the same threshold in radians. With no columns there is no defensible unit, so their absence throws rather than falling back; a fallback is the path by which a pixel tolerance silently becomes an angular one.

Both are per-view: M₁ belongs with the source bearings and M₂ with the targets. Swapping them is norm-preserving and invisible to Monte-Carlo calibration — measured median ratio 1.018, best cell inside a 4 SE band — and shows only in the tangency contract, fᵀM = 0, which separates a correct wiring (~1e-19) from a swapped one (~1e-3) by sixteen orders of magnitude.

E takes no measurement maps: a non-identity src_map/dst_map is an ArgumentError. Build the bearings with the cameras instead, EssentialProblem(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs).

EssentialProblem(pair::PoolPair{<:FundMat}; kw...) -> EssentialProblem

The pool-frame essential problem of a pool record: its correspondences (NormalizedImageFrame) back-projected to unit bearings by the pool-frame cameras CameraModel(Kcᵢ), Kcᵢ = Tᵢ·Kᵢ ([pool_frame_calibrations]). Residuals are the tangent Sampson whitened through Kc, so they are pool-frame quantities: the unit of the pool's stored distances, σ̂², outlier half-widths and loss widths. The sample gate's coincidence tolerance is 0.1 px in pool units (COINCIDENT_PX · pool_pixel_scale(pair)), always. kw go to EssentialProblem (e.g. a sampler).

A pool's Kc is a matrix, so this problem is pinhole by construction.

Fitting Pipeline

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.find_essential — Function
find_essential(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs; threshold=1.0,
    max_trials=10000, min_trials=100,
    local_optimization=Irls{TLSShape}(),
    scoring=nothing, config=nothing)

Robust essential-matrix estimation via 5-point RANSAC with MSAC scoring. Every default shown is configs/default.toml's ([ransac], [pipelines.essential]); a keyword overrides.

Takes PIXEL correspondences and the two cameras that produced them. Each view is back-projected with its OWN camera, so the per-view Jacobians land with the right endpoints by construction rather than by the caller remembering — a swap is norm-preserving and invisible to Monte-Carlo calibration, showing only in the tangency contract fᵀM = 0.

Two cameras rather than one because they are genuinely independent, and because a single-camera convenience form would make that swap undetectable in exactly the tests that ought to catch it.

DO NOT ADD a single-camera overload as an ergonomics improvement. Collapsing two distinct inputs into one erases the asymmetry a swap needs in order to be visible — the same reason an M-wiring harness needs two different focal lengths. A stereo pair with one camera repeated is written find_essential(cam, cam, cs), which costs three characters and keeps the asymmetry available.

Returns a RansacEstimate or nothing if fewer than 5 correspondences. iterations(result) is the number of trials (hypotheses drawn); test converged(result) before reading the model (see RansacEstimate).

Arguments

  • camera1, camera2: the cameras of the source and target views (any camera backproject_with_jacobian accepts).
  • pixel_cs: PIXEL correspondences, u₁ => u₂ in the two cameras' images (Csponds, StructArray{<:Pair} or a Pair vector); a Csponds :score column is the per-match prior (PROSAC; uniform otherwise) and travels to the bearing problem with every other attribute column.
  • threshold: the MSAC inlier threshold, in PIXELS. M picks the unit and the call cannot: the residual is whitened by the back-projection Jacobian, so the threshold it is compared against is in pixels; an orthonormal tangent basis would put the same number in radians. THE UNIT CHANGED — this was in calibrated coordinates until the bearing port, and no pixel value reproduces the old 0.02 across cameras because 0.02·f is f-dependent (≈16 px at f = 800, ≈24 px at f = 1200). Values below VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' 0.06 px refinement bound — a threshold or a scorer's outlier half-width — are REFUSED (ArgumentError) rather than silently honoured, since they are almost always a calibrated leftover — and refused by every entry that scores an EssentialProblem, not only this keyword: a scoring supplied here, or ransac / locally_optimize called directly. Builds the default scoring only; supplying it with scoring is an ArgumentError.
  • local_optimization: LO-RANSAC strategy (default Irls{TLSShape}(), as find_homography).
  • the other keywords as find_homography.

Examples

result = find_essential(cam1, cam2, pixel_pairs)
result = find_essential(cam1, cam2, Csponds(sources(pixel_pairs), targets(pixel_pairs); score = similarities))
VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.refine_essential — Function
refine_essential(E, camera1, camera2, pixel_cs; threshold = 1.0,
    local_optimization = Irls{TLSShape}(), scoring = nothing, workspace = nothing)
    -> RansacEstimate

The essential matrix E refined against the PIXEL correspondences pixel_cs of the cameras camera1, camera2: find_essential's step without the sampling, on the same bearing problem (each view back-projected with its own camera; the threshold in pixels, sub-detector values refused). local_optimization is the essential matrix's RANSAC local-optimization step, run here alone from the given model (no sampling). Every default is find_essential's ([pipelines.essential]); the keywords are those of every refine_*, documented on refine_homography. nothing for fewer than 5 correspondences.

Thresholds and operating points

find_essential's default threshold, 1 px ([pipelines.essential] in configs/default.toml), is a convention (OpenCV's findEssentialMat), not a tuned value. The paper's cross-validated operating points — the per-class scale multiples of the Welsch half-quadratic lift, with their provenance — are LocalOptimizationDoneRight's tuning constants (tuning_constant, WELSCH_HQ_SCALE_MULTIPLE_ESSENTIAL, …) and are supplied with its strategy: find_essential(cam1, cam2, cs; scoring = …, local_optimization = HQ{WelschShape}(scale_multiple = WELSCH_HQ_SCALE_MULTIPLE_ESSENTIAL)).

The Pure Rotation

A camera that only rotates between two views induces the infinite homography, a rotation R with f₂ ∥ R·f₁ on unit bearings. RotationProblem is the bearing problem of that model, built like the essential problem from the cameras' back-projection with the M₁/M₂ Jacobian columns (the whole field of a fisheye takes part, past 90°): VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Rotation2pt on two pairs, the residual the Sampson distance of the joint measurement (both images' pixels, Σ = I) to the transfer constraint on bearings, in pixels (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's constraint_with_jacobians(TransferForm(H), f₁, f₂; M₁, M₂) at H = R), and the chart-LM refit on VisualGeometryToolkitCore's SO3Manifold through the homography's transfer rows. find_rotation is its RANSAC; a scorer or a lift from another package plugs in as for find_essential.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RotationProblem — Type
RotationProblem

The RANSAC problem of a pure rotation between two calibrated views: the MinimalSolveProblem whose model is a RotMatrix{3, Float64} R with f₂ ∥ R·f₁ for unit bearings f₁ => f₂ (the infinite homography). Minimal sample 2 (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Rotation2pt), codimension 2, a 3-dof chart. Build it with RotationProblem(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs) or from a bearing Csponds carrying M₁/M₂ columns; the public estimator is find_rotation.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.find_rotation — Function
find_rotation(camera1, camera2, pixel_cs; threshold = 1.0, max_trials, min_trials,
    local_optimization = Irls{TLSShape}(), scoring = nothing, config = nothing, rng)
find_rotation(bearings::Csponds; kwargs...)

Robust estimation of the rotation R between two views of a camera that only rotated — f₂ ∥ R·f₁ for the unit bearings of a correspondence, the infinite homography — by RANSAC on the RotationProblem: 2-point samples (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Rotation2pt, kabsch on the pair), the Sampson distance of the joint measurement (both images' pixels, Σ = I) to the transfer constraint on bearings, in PIXELS through the views' back-projection Jacobians, and the local optimization's chart-LM refit on the rotation chart. A sampler (it draws hypotheses and verifies them), so a find_*.

Every default shown is configs/default.toml's ([ransac], [pipelines.rotation]); a keyword overrides. The keywords are find_homography's; a scorer or local optimization from another package (RANSACScoringDoneRight's StudentTUniformScoring(RotationProblem, a), LocalOptimizationDoneRight's lifts selected by tag) plugs in through scoring and local_optimization.

  • camera1, camera2, pixel_cs: PIXEL correspondences u₁ => u₂ and the cameras of the two views, each view back-projected with its own camera (a rotating camera is find_rotation(cam, cam, cs)); the whole field of a fisheye takes part, past 90°. A :score column is the PROSAC prior.
  • bearings: unit bearings with their M₁/M₂ Jacobian columns (see RotationProblem).
  • threshold: the MSAC inlier threshold of the default scoring, in PIXELS (the residual's unit, as find_essential's). Values below VisualGeometryToolkitFeatures' 0.06 px refinement bound are refused.

Returns a RansacEstimate (its estimate a RotMatrix{3, Float64}), or nothing for fewer than 2 correspondences; test converged(result) before reading the model.
