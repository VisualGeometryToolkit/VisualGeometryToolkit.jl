Radial and Lens Calibration

These estimators calibrate a camera with lens distortion from correspondences between planar calibration boards and their images.

Radial homography

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RadialHomographyProblem — Type
RadialHomographyProblem(pairs; center, sampler=nothing, backend=NativeSolver(),
                        extra_check=nothing, minimal=:radial)

Construct a radially distorted homography problem (one-sided division model on the target side) from 2D-2D point correspondences.

  • center: the distortion center in TARGET pixels, REQUIRED — the target side is normalized about it (Hartley, mean distance √2). Data, not configuration: the division model is meaningless without its center.
  • extra_check: true (the default when unset) keeps only the cubic root consistent with the redundant 4.5-pt equation per minimal sample (the reference's RANSAC setting); false scores all real roots. It selects a root of the 5-point kernel, so supplying it with minimal = :homography is an ArgumentError rather than a setting that silently does nothing.
  • minimal = :radial: the hypothesis path — :radial samples five correspondences for the 5-point (H, k) kernel; :homography samples four for the plain homography, the (H, k) local optimization supplying k on the consensus (header, point 4).
  • pairs (and its :score prior)/sampler/backend: as HomographyProblem. The sources are exact or plain and the targets plain (any other covariance is an ArgumentError), so the residual is the reprojection error in target pixels: with an exact source and a plain target that is the whitened residual, and its norm the Mahalanobis distance.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.find_radial_homography — Function
find_radial_homography(correspondences; center,
    scoring, extent=nothing,
    workspace=nothing, extra_check=nothing, minimal=:radial,
    local_optimization_strategy, max_trials, min_trials, config=nothing, rng=...)

scoring (its width in pixels) and local_optimization_strategy default to configs/default.toml [pipelines.radial_homography], max_trials and min_trials to its [ransac] table; a keyword overrides. A bare threshold or outlier_halfwidth keyword is refused, as find_homography. extra_check is the radial 5-point kernel's root filter (see RadialHomographyProblem) and is refused with minimal = :homography.

Robust radially distorted homography estimation (one-sided one-parameter division model on the TARGET side; Wadenbäck et al. (2026)) via RANSAC with MSAC scoring. minimal = :homography pairs the global search for a plain homography (four-point hypotheses, Homography4pt) with this problem's (H, k) local optimization: the hypothesis carries no distortion term, k is seeded on its consensus by the closed-form least squares in k given H and the joint manifold step estimates both, and the correspondences a plane homography cannot hold under distortion enter at the re-score. The default :radial samples five for the 5-point kernel, whose root pick fails on boards too small to identify k from alone.

Returns a RansacEstimate whose model is a VisualGeometryToolkitCore.RadialHomography in the DATA (pixel) frame — H, k, and the distortion center all carried in the model — the :no_model result (iterations 0) for fewer than a minimal sample (5 correspondences, 4 with minimal = :homography). iterations(result) is the number of trials (hypotheses drawn); test found_model(result) before reading the model (see RansacEstimate).

center — the distortion center in target pixels — is REQUIRED: the 5-pt solver runs in the Hartley frame about it.

local_optimization_strategy defaults to Irls{TLSShape}() (least squares on the inliers) on the 9-dof sl(3) ⊕ k manifold, via the joint 9-dof LM M-step. The chart-rows provider makes LocalOptimizationDoneRight's lifts available on the same manifold.

The other keywords as find_homography.

Units: the source points are plain numbers. A radial homography maps from the source plane, so its entries carry that plane's unit, which a matrix cannot record: physical (unitful) source points are an ArgumentError — strip them in the unit the rest of your plain data uses — metres, as the stack's boundaries strip (ustrip.(m, X)).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_radial_camera — Function
fit_radial_camera(cs::AbstractVector{<:Cspond{<:StaticVector{2}, <:StaticVector{2}}};
                  center, iterations = 2)
    -> FitResult{RadialCamera{Float64}, Nothing}

The radial camera of one board from its plane_point => image_point correspondences cs, the image points RAW as measured; center (pixels, REQUIRED — the principal point, as find_radial_camera and RadialCameraProblem require it) is the pencil's centre and rides on the returned camera, so nothing is pre-centred. There is no default: a pencil about the image corner fits raw pixels as well as any other, with no error. Each pair gives one linear constraint on the six entries of P (constraint_with_jacobians); the null vector is solved by SVD in the radial camera's normalization, CspondNormalization(RadialCamera, cs; center) (Hartley at the plane centroid, Hartley about center on the image side — the same strategy find_radial_camera's problem solves in), and composed back into pixels. Each iteration re-solves; from the second on every row is divided by the constraint's measurement-gradient norm at the previous iteration's solution (whitening_factor, the Sampson normalizer held fixed — the algebraic error becomes the exact point–radial-line distance); a weights column on cs (a Csponds attribute, e.g. withattrs(cs; weights)) multiplies on top, the row weight being √w. iterations = 1 is the plain algebraic (unwhitened) solve. This is how the BabelCalib back-end obtains the radial camera when no homography is available; when one is, its rows 1–2 are the same object (RadialCamera(H)). Five correspondences determine P (five degrees of freedom).

The FitResult's estimate is the camera, its record (ReturnCode.Success, ClosedForm) after iterations iterations (its iterations field), and rms the point–radial-line distance (px) of the n pairs at the fit, whatever the iteration count. When the constraint rows leave more than one null direction (a degenerate configuration — the plane points on a line, or a pixel on the centre) the record is (ReturnCode.InitialFailure, NoRule), the camera NaN and the rms NaN.

Units: the source points are plain numbers. A radial camera maps from the source plane, so its entries carry that plane's unit, which a matrix cannot record: physical (unitful) source points are an ArgumentError — strip them in the unit the rest of your plain data uses — metres, as the stack's boundaries strip (ustrip.(m, X)).

Division camera

fit_division_camera fits a camera with a division lens model to several board views at once. division_camera_step is the model update it repeats under its current weights. The robust loop is the stack's shared one: the posterior of a StudentTUniformScoring (the weighting keyword) on the posterior-weight iteration of a MixtureEmConfig (the mixture_em keyword), over the stack as a DivisionCameraProblem.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_division_camera — Function
fit_division_camera(radial_cameras::AbstractDict{K}, views::AbstractDict{K};
                    image_size = nothing, focal_seed = nothing, ncoef, k = nothing,
                    weighting, mixture_em,   # defaults: configs/default.toml [fit_division_camera]
                    verbose = false)
    -> FitResult{CameraModel, DivisionCameraMetadata}

The BabelCalib linear focal solve as an iterated, gradient-whitened, robustly re-weighted estimator (see the file header). Keyed by the view's identity K (the key sets must be equal — ArgumentError): radial_cameras[id] is view id's radial camera (a RadialCamera, or a homography whose rows 1–2 are read) and views[id] its plane_point => raw_pixel correspondences — per-board VALIDATED inliers (the cameras carry the pencil's shared centre) — the inputs of division_camera_step. A view's weights column, if it has one, is a prior row weight: iteration 0 is weighted by it and each later iteration by it times the posterior. k fixes the division coefficient (centred pixels); otherwise ncoef are estimated. Each iteration whitens every row at the previous solution (the row's Sampson factor held fixed) and weights it by the Student-t/uniform posterior of the previous iteration's whitened residuals.

THE ROBUST MACHINERY IS THE STACK'S SHARED ONE (configs/default.toml [fitdivisioncamera], one schema):

  • weighting — the E-step: the posterior of a StudentTUniformScoring at codimension 2 with a supplied outlier half-width, its scale prior the posterior's (default the table's weighting, read by scoring_from_toml with problem = DivisionCameraProblem; absent keys inherit [scoring.studenttuniform]). Another codimension, or one without a half-width, is an ArgumentError. A fit takes no scoring (the robust-estimation contract): the former scoring keyword is an ArgumentError naming weighting.
  • mixture_em — the loop: a MixtureEmConfig driving the posterior-weight iteration (PosteriorIrlsMethod) — its model update must be WeightedRefine (the stack is refitted each iteration), its stop ParameterChangeStop (each iteration re-whitens at the previous solution, so no cost is comparable between iterations: the fit stops when (f, k) moves by less than rtol relative and returns the LAST iterate; the incumbent rule stopped the contaminated stacks at their second iteration, f 2 % off), its nuisance policies the posterior's and its max_iter the budget (default the table's mixture_em, read by mixture_em_from_toml; absent keys inherit [mixture_em]). Anything else is an ArgumentError.

outlier_halfwidth and max_iter are no keywords since 0.9 (an ArgumentError naming the form above): the half-width is the weighting's, the cap the EM's.

INPUT CONTRACT: each view is that board's VALIDATED correspondences — the inliers its radial camera was found with — not raw detections. The Student-t/uniform posterior absorbs what validation leaves (a few per cent of ray-aligned mismatches, which a radial camera's 1-D line test cannot reject); it is not a substitute for it.

THE BRANCH AUDIT. Each view's pose branch — the tilt ambiguity of its radial camera — is chosen on the iteration that first places the view, from that view's own solve, and kept. That solve cannot separate the tilt pair by residual, so one mismatch pushed along its ray through the centre can tip it (on the MC scene at 3 % ray-aligned mismatches, 16 % of the views carrying one took a branch 48–85° off; none of the clean views), and the view's inliers then carry residuals far above sigma — after the posterior has already rejected the mismatch. So once the IRLS converges — or, if it is still moving at half the mixture_em.max_iter budget, there (a wrong branch is what keeps a stack from converging) — one iteration re-judges every view's branch at the shared (f, k), robustly; if it moves any, the IRLS resumes from there within what is left of max_iter (the audit counts as an iteration, so iterations never exceeds max_iter); if it moves none the IRLS continues unchanged, bit for bit (clean stacks). Never after :solve_failed, nor at max_iter = 1. Measured: 104 → 4 wrong views at 3 %, 66 → 1 at 5 %, no correct view moved, and the held-out residuals calibrated against ŝ²·I₂ in the tail as well as the bulk (test/ransac_mc_calibration_tests.jl (e)).

Inputs, k, the scorer's outlier_halfwidth, focal_seed and every output are in pixels; the stack is conditioned inside by one normalization (CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center) — Hartley about the radial cameras' shared centre), built once. image_size = (width, height) (pixels) of the image the views were measured in is REQUIRED for the default seed only (ArgumentError when both it and focal_seed are missing): its half-diagonal s = hypot(width, height)/2, a 90° diagonal field of view. With focal_seed given, image_size may be omitted and does not enter the result.

Iteration 0 has no previous solution: it is whitened at a SEED camera — focal focal_seed (pixels; by default s), k₀ = 0 (the given k when k fixes it — the known-k rows are exact only there), each board at the depth that camera implies — which sets the row WEIGHTS only; f and k are solved by the iteration. The plain algebraic rows it replaces weigh each board by its depth (a far board's rows ~10× a near one's), so a few mismatches on a heavy board dragged the joint solve to f ≈ 45–300 px against 1226 and half those stacks never recovered; the seed fixes that and is robust to a seed 0.5×–3× off (measured 2026-09-22; see test/ransac_mc_calibration_tests.jl (e)). Supply a better focal as data — a previous calibration, EXIF — through focal_seed.

The scorer's outlier_halfwidth (pixels) is the half-width of the uniform outlier component — the same quantity the RANSAC problems take; it must NOT be the residual scale (a half-width at the median residual makes the outlier component as dense as the inliers and zeroes every weight — measured). The posterior is seeded from the median residual (median_seeded_posterior): every point starts an inlier and the M-step shrinks ŝ as the model improves.

That seed and schedule are load-bearing, not incidental. Five alternatives were measured against RVG's synthetic (known f₀, 0.4 px noise, 8 % gross outliers) on 2026-09-10 and every one was worse: a frozen MAD anchor 6.5 % off f₀, a global Jeffreys sweep 3.5 %, a per-board sweep 5.1 %, frozen Welsch 28 %, Welsch with an adaptive scale 85 % (no uniform component ⇒ the scale update has no fixed point and ran to ŝ = 29). This estimator lands at 1.25 %. A sweep seed needs residuals that are already bimodal; iteration 0 here is one linear solve of an uncalibrated stack (whitened at a seed, not at a fit), so they are not. (Those measurements predate the seeded iteration 0.)

Iteration 0 alone — whitened at the seed and robustly unweighted — is the step

N = CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center)   # center: the cameras' shared centre
CameraModel(division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras, views; ncoef, k, whiten_at = focal_seed))

(with focal_seed the half-diagonal by default); this function always runs at least one robust iteration (mixture_em.max_iter ≥ 1), and max_iter = 1 is that step re-weighted once, with no branch audit. A stack whose iteration 0 cannot solve at all (no board admits a pose, none carries depth, the solve is singular) raises the DivisionCameraError — there is no iterate to return; a later iteration that cannot solve ends the fit on the last good one (:solve_failed).

The radial cameras are FIXED. Each view's radial camera is an input, estimated once by the caller — BlobBoards fits every one linearly about the centre, iterated to its consensus and polished by a local optimization on the exact point–radial-line distance — and this stage only turns them into an initial focal length, coefficients and depths for a bundle. It used to re-fit them per iteration under the point weights, which could not improve a nonlinear fit and did wreck one: the perturbation tipped the metric upgrade's cheirality test onto the mirrored branch for a board the shared solve had placed behind the camera, turning its pose 179.95° and its whitened residual from 1.7 px to 1102 px, and through the Student-t width that single row collapsed every weight in the solve — 18 of 106 boards lost their poses, boards whose own poses under the converged bundle camera were the BEST in the set at 0.14 px (measured 2026-09-09, fisheye hallway). Hand this function cameras you are willing to stand behind; it will not launder contaminated ones.

Returns the camera CameraModel(::DivisionCameraState) builds — K = diag(f, f, 1) at the cameras' shared centre, Division{ncoef} lens in K's chart — plus the final keyed DivisionCameraState (pixels), the views with each correspondence's IRLS row weight as their weights column, and the loop outcome — and the robust-estimation contract's record of the E-step (DivisionCameraMetadata): weights(result), each correspondence's posterior inlier probability (PosteriorWeights: inlier_mask(result) is posterior > ½), and noise_scale(result), the E-step's Student-t scale ŝ (pixels, a LINEAR scale; scale_source(result) is ScaleEstimated), with its ν. n is the support (the rows of positive weight) and rms the posterior-weighted whitened-residual RMS, √(Σwᵢrᵢ² / Σwᵢ) (pixels): a row with prior weight zero, or of a view the final iteration left without a pose, weighs 0 and is not a residual.

Units: the board points are plain numbers. The radial cameras and the board poses carry the board plane's unit, which neither a matrix nor this result records, so physical (unitful) board points are an ArgumentError: strip them in the unit the radial cameras were fit in (the stack's own boundaries strip in metres, ustrip.(m, X)).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.division_camera_step — Function
division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras::AbstractDict{K}, views::AbstractDict{K};
                     ncoef = 1, k = nothing, whiten_at)
    -> DivisionCameraState{K}

A declared INNER-LOOP piece (public, not exported): it takes the estimator's normalization N and pixel data, and returns pixel state. N is REQUIRED — CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center), the one strategy fit_division_camera builds — applied to the data inside, never built here; there is no fallback.

One iteration of the stacked linear focal solve via the division-model lift (BabelCalib's back-end back-substitution, Lochman et al. (2021)), as a DivisionCameraState: the shared unknowns plus the per-board poses and the whitened per-point residuals the next iteration needs. Square pixels are assumed. Both inputs are keyed by the view's identity K (the key sets must be equal — ArgumentError): radial_cameras[id] is view id's radial camera (a RadialCamera, or a HomographyMat whose rows 1–2 are read) in the SAME plane frame as views[id], that view's plane_point => image_point correspondences with the RAW (distorted) image point in pixels. The pencil's centre rides on the cameras, must be shared across the stack, and is carried by the result; a bare HomographyMat means the origin.

Per board, the radial camera fixes the rotation and (t₁, t₂) up to four sign candidates; the remaining unknowns are LINEAR in the division-model lift (x, y, 1 + Σₚ kₚ r²ᵖ) ∝ (X, Y, Z + t₃) (RadialLift). The candidate is chosen by cheirality and residual, and every board is stacked into one least-squares system with the shared unknowns and one t₃ per board. A view's weights column (a Csponds attribute, e.g. withattrs(cs; weights)) weighs its rows — a board-level vote folds into it losslessly, the row weight being √w; views with no admissible candidate are dropped, and a DivisionCameraError is thrown if none survive or the stack yields f ≤ 0 (the argument contract still raises ArgumentError). With k given (the one-parameter division coefficient in the centered-pixel frame, e.g. RadialHomography.k), only f and the t₃'s are estimated (ncoef must stay 1); otherwise the stack carries ncoef ≥ 1 extra shared columns λₚ = kₚ·f and estimates the coefficients of r², r⁴, … too.

N is the normalization the stack is solved in: its image side must be an isotropic scale about the radial cameras' shared centre, its plane side an isotropic similarity (ArgumentError otherwise). The state is returned in pixels regardless. whiten_at whitens every row (the rows' Sampson factor L⁻¹ of G_x·G_xᵀ, whitening_factor, frozen there) at

  • a previous iteration's DivisionCameraState — its solution, each view in the branch that iteration chose; or
  • a SEED focal length (a positive Real, data units): the pinhole f, k₀ = 0 (the given k when k fixes it), each view in its freshly chosen branch at the depth that camera implies. The seed sets the WEIGHTS only — f and k are still solved by this iteration. It puts iteration 0 in pixel currency, where the plain rows weigh each view by its depth (a far board's rows ~10× a near one's); see fit_division_camera.

The camera an iteration determines is CameraModel(state): K = diag(f, f, 1) with the principal point at the cameras' shared centre, and a Division{ncoef} lens re-expressed in that K's normalized frame (kₚ,norm = kₚ,px · f^(2p), VGC's radial-coefficient law — the lens law of the CameraModel(model, K′) constructor).

ONE ITERATION, not an estimator. Without whiten_at there is nothing to linearize at, so the SOLVE is plain algebraic least squares; the returned sq_distances, which the robust driver reweights by, are the Sampson residuals at the solution. The robust driver's iteration 0 is this step at its seed,

N = CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center)
CameraModel(division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras, views; ncoef, whiten_at = focal_seed))

(fit_division_camera builds the same normalization). For a converged estimate call the robust driver.

Units: the board points are plain numbers. The radial cameras and the board poses carry the board plane's unit, which neither a matrix nor this result records, so physical (unitful) board points are an ArgumentError: strip them in the unit the radial cameras were fit in (the stack's own boundaries strip in metres, ustrip.(m, X)).

DivisionCameraState{K}

Result of one iteration of the stacked linear focal solve, in DATA (pixel) units, keyed by the views' identity K: f; the division coefficients k (of r², r⁴, … in pixels centred on centre); the pencil's centre, the principal point every input radial camera shares; poses[id], the board pose — an EuclideanMap{3} from the board plane (z = 0) into the camera, rotation from the radial camera, translation (t₁₂, t₃) — in the plane's units, of each view that admitted one (a view without a pose this iteration is absent); and sq_distances[id], the whitened residual norms² of the view's correspondences (pixel², in its order; NaN for a view without a pose) — the currency the robust re-weighting reads. ids is the sorted key order. VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.DivisionCameraError — Type
DivisionCameraError(msg)

The stacked division-camera solve (division_camera_step) has no system to solve for the boards it was given: no board admits a pose with its points in front of the camera and imaged on the same side of the centre, no board carries depth information (every row weighted to zero or at the centre), the shared unknowns are not determined or not finite, or the focal length comes out non-positive. A condition of the DATA, not of the arguments — the argument contract (one correspondence set per camera, one shared centre, …) still raises ArgumentError — so a caller that can go on without the solve catches this type and nothing else.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RadialLift — Type
RadialLift{N,T}

The division-lift constraint model of ONE board: the partial pose (R, t12) from its radial camera and the linear unknowns θ = (f, λ₁..λ_N, t₃) — f, the lift coefficients λₚ = kₚ·f of r²ᵖ, and the board's depth. For a plane point X and its RAW centered pixel x the constraint is

g = x·(Z + t₃) − X_c·(f + Σₚ λₚ ρ²ᵖ)   (two rows; X_c = camera-frame point at t₃ = 0)

which is LINEAR in θ: g = G_θ·θ + c₀, c₀ = x·Z. constraint_with_jacobians returns g, the measurement Jacobian G_x = ∂g/∂x (2×2) and G_θ (2×(N+2)), and its frozen whitening factor (whitening_factor, L⁻¹ of G_x·G_xᵀ) turns them into pixel-currency rows. A KNOWN k folds λ₁ = k·f into the f column (chain rule) — see _lift_columns.

Camera and poses

refine_camera_and_poses refines a camera — its intrinsics and lens, on VisualGeometryToolkitCore's ParameterManifold — jointly with one pose per view (LeftRigidManifold6) against the reprojection error of 3D → 2D correspondences, from a guess of both: the bundle of a calibration with a known target, for any lens the camera's family holds (a fisheye's rays past 90° are ordinary data). free = () holds the camera and refines the poses alone. Its strategy is the refine contract's optimization_strategy — Triggs{WelschShape}() by default, run from the start with no least-squares stage, at the caller's scale = σ (a refine estimates no scale) or width = w; nothing is plain least squares. A SplineLens camera refines its backward controls instead (K frozen, the lens axis held on ẑ), by implicit differentiation of its projection solve, with a scale-free third-difference penalty over its lattice whose weight generalized cross-validation chooses by default (penalty = :gcv; a number fixes it, 0 turns it off); a forward spline is refitted after. With several rigid boards, refine_camera_and_poses(camera, poses, board_poses, views) adds one pose per board (board → world) to the same bundle, the correspondences keyed by (view, board); each connected group of views and boards keeps its gauge by a held pose (its first board by default, held_boards/held_views), exactly.

refine_camera_and_poses(camera, views::RotationCenterViews, csponds) refines the views of a camera turned about one fixed point (a tripod head) under one rigid motion model, one rotation per view and a shared centre and offset, the camera held. Its residual is each dot's BEARING for every camera: the dot back-projected once by the camera, the tangent angle to the predicted bearing whitened into pixels by the back-projection Jacobian (the BearingPoseProblem's); the camera is never projected inside the solve, and a dot it does not back-project makes no row and is counted in the metadata (n_without_bearing, without_bearing).

Refine a camera and one pose per view jointly against the reprojection error of 3D → 2D correspondences: minimize Σ ‖project(camera, pose_v(X)) − u‖² over the camera's free parameters and every view's pose, from the guesses camera and poses. A refine: both guesses are required, nothing is re-estimated from scratch and the camera keeps its family (its lens type).

Arguments

  • camera — the camera guess, a CameraModel or a BoundedCamera of one (the estimate is then bounded alike): intrinsics and lens, the parameters VisualGeometryToolkitCore's parameter_vector(camera) lists (f, aspect, skew, cx, cy, then the lens's coefficients), in pixels. A SplineLens camera takes its own route (below).
  • poses — one world → camera EuclideanMap{3} per view (X_c = R·X + t, X in the target's frame), keyed like views.
  • views — per view, its correspondences cs = [X => u, …]: X a target point (3-vector), u the pixel it is detected at (2-vector). Grouped data: an AbstractDict{K, <:AbstractVector{<:Cspond}} keyed by the view, with poses::AbstractDict{K} of the same keys (block order: the sorted keys; an ArgumentError when the key sets differ), or a vector of views with a vector of poses of the same length. Every point must project at the guess (in front of the camera, inside the lens's domain): an ArgumentError names the first that does not.

Keywords (configuration)

  • fixed — the camera parameters HELD, by name or by group (:k for k1…; VisualGeometryToolkitCore's free_parameter_names resolves it), () the default: every parameter is refined but the gauge, the skew, which is held by construction (naming it is accepted); naming every parameter holds the camera and refines the poses alone. The stack's one parameter-holding keyword (NLLSsolver's word; OpenCV's CALIB_FIX_*): free, fix, fixed_params and hold are refused, naming it.
  • box — a solver search box on the camera's parameters by name (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's ParameterManifold(camera; box)), or nothing; with every parameter fixed (nothing to bound) a box is an ArgumentError.
  • optimization_strategy — the refine contract's strategy, as every refine_* takes it: Triggs{Shape} (the default, Triggs{WelschShape}, configs/default.toml [refine_camera_and_poses]), one robustified LM (Core's loss_robustifier), starting directly from the caller's start (camera, poses, board poses): it runs no least-squares stage, so gross outliers never pull a warm-up fit; or nothing, plain least squares (it reads no scale). TOML: { type = "triggs", loss = "welsch" }. An Irls{Shape} is an ArgumentError naming Triggs{Shape} (a refine runs one LM, no reweighting cycles); any other strategy is one too (it needs a RANSAC consensus set). (The keyword was robust, then local_optimization; both are refused, an ArgumentError naming this one.)
  • scale or width — EXACTLY ONE for a robust strategy (so for the default), neither for least squares (ArgumentError otherwise; Core's scale_input): scale = σ, the detections' noise scale (pixels, per coordinate), or an upstream result whose noise_scale it is; the loss width is then the strategy's efficiency constant times σ (Core's efficiency_constant(Shape, 2) unless the strategy states one). width = w: the loss width itself (pixels). A refine estimates no scale; the metadata records the one it ran with (scale, scale_source, loss_width) and the weights ρ′/ρ′(0) at the estimate.
  • max_iter — the LM's cap on ACCEPTED iterations (NLLSsolver's count); ftol — its relative cost decrease and the stall postcondition's exact-fit test; xtol — its step tolerance (chart units); max_time — its wall-clock cap in seconds (nothing or Inf: none), for a camera whose projection makes an iteration slow; it stops with :max_time.

The estimate is (; camera, poses): the refined camera, and the refined poses keyed like the input (a Dict by the same keys, or a vector in view order, each with its input's frame labels). Its termination record (retcode, stop_rule) is the last solve's, VisualGeometryToolkitCore's nlls_termination: ReturnCode.Success only at a stationary point, else MaxIters (the iteration cap), MaxTime, Failure/InitialFailure (NonFinite) or Stalled; rms is √(Σ‖rᵢ‖² / n) over the n correspondences (pixels). The metadata (CameraPosesRefineMetadata) carries the residual rows, s², ν and the free parameter names. Plain least squares: the correspondences must be inliers.

Several boards

With board_poses, the target is several rigid boards, each with its own pose: poses maps each view to its world → camera pose, board_poses each board to its board → world pose (both AbstractDicts of plain EuclideanMap{3}), and views maps (view, board) to the correspondences X => u of that board's points (X in the board's frame) detected in that view. The residual is project(camera, pose_v(board_b(X))) − u; each board pose is one more pose chart of the same NLLSsolver problem, beside the camera's chart and the view poses (the same assembler and LM, the same retcode and metadata; ν counts 6 per free board too). A pose the data never reach is an ArgumentError, and so is a correspondence of a view or board without a pose.

The gauge: a common rigid motion of every board, undone by every view, leaves the cost unchanged, once per connected component of the view–board graph (views and boards linked by their correspondences). It is fixed EXACTLY, by holding poses at their guesses (no pin, no prior): held_boards (by default each component's first board, in sorted key order) and held_views (none by default) are held, every other pose is a variable, and a component without a held pose is an ArgumentError. Holding more than one pose of a component constrains the solve (a held pose is known). The estimate's board_poses returns a held pose as given. A parametric camera only (a SplineLens camera's gauge is its own: an ArgumentError); plain poses and points.

A SplineLens camera

Its K is a frozen gauge (skew 0) and its parameters are its backward lattice's controls at the present nodes, so its route differs (Core's IntrinsicsRole trait, GaugeIntrinsics, selects it):

  • The variables are the present nodes' controls, any norms (a fit_camera lattice's, a quasi-interpolant's: they are kept as they are, as normalizing them would change the backprojection), each c = e^ℓ·D its direction on its own sphere (the reference's chart, Core's ControlPatchManifold) and its log-scale ℓ (3 coordinates; the controls grouped into 2 × 2 patches, NLLSsolver's 10-variable block cap), and the poses; only the nodes a row reads are variables, the others kept exactly as they are (result.spline.n_nodes_unrefined counts them). The solve runs in three stages, each from the last: the poses alone through the guess's lens, then the directions and the poses with every scale held exactly (the scales are no variable of that stage), then everything (a node's scale is weakly observed and nearly collinear with its direction, so a joint solve from far off creeps, or lets the scales absorb the poses' error). fixed is () (the default: the controls refined) or (:controls,) (the poses alone, the camera's lens held as the entry gauged it); a box is an ArgumentError.
  • Each correspondence's rows are K·x* − u, x* the lens's projection solve on the correspondence's 4 × 4 window at the guess, its Jacobian analytic by implicit differentiation at x* (no automatic differentiation reaches the solve). A correspondence detected on a window with an absent node is dropped and counted (result.spline.n_dropped_window), and so is one the camera does not project when detected within one knot cell of such a window (it images it onto the absent nodes; counted apart, result.spline.n_dropped_unprojected: it can be an inlier); every other one must project at the guess (and at each rebuild's estimate: an ArgumentError names the round). A third-difference penalty (Core's difference_rows, the P-spline fit's operator, along every row and column of present nodes) holds the nodes the data reach weakly or not at all. Each of its rows is divided by the mean control length of its own 4 nodes, so it is scale-free. Its weight is the stack's normalized β (penalty_scale): λ = β · energy / N / m̄, the data's energy per parameter read as the mean diagonal of its Gauss–Newton matrix in the nodes' direction coordinates times the controls' squared mean length (scale-free too; the residuals of exact data vanish, so they cannot set it), and m̄ the mean positive eigenvalue of the Δ³ operator's DᵀD. β = 1 weighs the penalty like the data. β is set by penalty:
    • :gcv (the default): β minimizes the generalized cross-validation score GCV = n‖r‖² / (n − tr H)² of the refine's linearization (n the data rows): the whole refine solved ONCE at β = 1 (the bracket's top, where the penalty weighs like the data and no weakly held node swings), its Gauss–Newton step from there for every λ (r the data rows' residuals after the step, H = J(JᵀJ + λDᵀD)⁻¹Jᵀ, J and D the data and Δ³ rows' Jacobians there, over the variables' coordinates), from ONE generalized eigendecomposition of the pencil (DᵀD, JᵀJ + DᵀD) up to 3000 coordinates, else per λ a sparse Cholesky and a 64-probe Hutchinson trace from a fixed seed (deterministic; standard deviation ≤ √(2·tr H / 64)); minimized over log₁₀ β ∈ [−12, 0] by the stack's gcv_select (one β a decade, then Brent's method to its own floor; a minimum at a bracket end is reported, gcv_at_bound); then ONE final solve at the chosen β, from the linearization's Gauss–Newton step there (from the β = 1 estimate if that step stops projecting a correspondence).
    • a number β > 0: that weight; no GCV. On noisy detections a small weight can let the nodes the data hold weakly swing until a correspondence no longer projects: an ArgumentError says so and suggests :gcv or a larger weight.
    • 0 or nothing: no smoothness. Then every present node outside every correspondence's window is kept as it is (and counted). Without the Δ³ rows each node's log-scale is held by its data alone, nearly collinear with its own direction, so the joint solve needs ≈ 2–3× the default max_iter (≈ 210 iterations on the 3-view fixture); at the default cap it returns :max_iterations.
    The blocks are rebuilt at the estimate and solved again until every correspondence reads its own window (result.spline.rounds, at most 5; at the cap the rows, rms and the stationarity test are read on the returned camera's own windows, and windows_settled is false). iterations sums every solve.
  • First, always, the optical axis. The camera is turned so ẑ is its optical axis and its principal point is that axis's pixel, and the guess poses are turned with it, so every image is kept: optical_axis, then Core's axis_gauged. The axis is fitted over the camera's own bound, so the camera must be a BoundedCamera; a bare model is an ArgumentError. The refine runs from there. result.spline.axis_rotation is the turn, from the given camera's frame to the refined one's. The refined K is the gauged guess's.
  • The gauge: a rotation of every control together with every pose, and one common scale of every control, leave the cost unchanged. The solve fixes it exactly by the parametrization: the first view's rotation and the log-scale of the node nearest x = 0 are no coordinate of any variable, so the LM's normal matrix is full rank. It holds the frame and K the entry set, and nothing moves the estimate after the LM. K is bit-identical to the axis-gauged guess's.
  • A diagnostic only: optical_axis of the refined camera. result.spline.refined_axis_offset is how far its axis's image lies from the held principal point (px), and result.spline.refined_symmetry_residual is its symmetry residual. Nothing is re-gauged from it.
  • A forward spline is dropped for the refinement and, with refit_forward (the default), refitted over imaged_region(camera) by fit_camera(SplineLens, …; tolerance = forward_tolerance) (pixels), which needs a BoundedCamera (an ArgumentError for a bare camera model). It is never kept, even with fixed = (:controls,): the entry's turn of the frame leaves it stale.
  • The model: the target is exact, so the estimate is the maximum-likelihood one under isotropic Gaussian detection noise (penalized by the smoothness prior; with penalty = 0 the plain maximum-likelihood one). A board that departs from its design (an in-plane warp, an affine or smooth deformation, a height field) is not modelled here: that is the board-deformation solve of RadiometricCamera's calibration pipeline.
  • max_time caps the whole refine (every stage and rebuild round reads what is left of it; the record (ReturnCode.MaxTime, TimeCap) when any was cut short).
  • Device: the projections that build each problem run batched on the device (Core's default_device()); the LM is NLLSsolver's, on the CPU.

Units

Plain inputs give plain poses; physical ones (unitful poses and target points, all one form) are read in metres at entry and the poses come back physical, in metres. The residuals are pixels either way.

Penalty weights: normalization and generalized cross-validation

One convention for a penalty's weight across the stack: λ = β · energy / N / m̄ (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's penalty_scale), β relative to the data's energy per parameter and to the penalty operator's mean positive eigenvalue m̄ (Core's mean_positive_eigenvalue), so β = 1 weighs the penalty like the data. gcv_select chooses β by generalized cross-validation from a score of λ — a coarse scan of log₁₀ β, then Brent's method — the score the closed-form curve of one penalized least squares (gcv_curve: every λ from one generalized eigendecomposition). A nonlinear fit evaluates it on its linearization, as the SplineLens refine does (one solve at β = 1, the curve, one final solve). Public, not exported.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.gcv_curve — Function
gcv_curve(G, h, rr, Q, q, n) -> (λ -> (; gcv, rss, dof, y))

GCV of the penalized least squares min_y ‖J y − r‖² + λ‖D y − c‖² with the normal-equation pieces G = JᵀJ, h = Jᵀr, rr = ‖r‖², Q = DᵀD, q = Dᵀc and n data rows, as a function of λ. One Cholesky B = G + Q = L Lᵀ and one symmetric eigendecomposition L⁻¹ Q L⁻ᵀ = U diag(μ) Uᵀ — the generalized eigenproblem of the pencil (Q, B), V = L⁻ᵀ U: VᵀBV = I, VᵀQV = diag μ — diagonalize every M_λ = G + λQ at once, VᵀM_λV = diag s, sᵢ = 1 − μᵢ + λμᵢ. With zh = Vᵀh, zq = Vᵀq and w = (zh + λ zq) ./ s (the solution y = V w, returned: a nonlinear fit's Gauss–Newton step at the chosen λ): rss = rr − 2 w⋅zh + Σ (1 − μᵢ) wᵢ², dof = tr H_λ = Σ (1 − μᵢ)/sᵢ. Parameters nothing reads (zero diagonal in B) are dropped (0 in y). B must then be positive definite: a direction no row holds is a gauge, which its caller holds exactly (Core's rule: no penalty or ridge fixes it); an ArgumentError otherwise. Dense, O(p³) once in the parameters, then O(p²) per λ. (Moved from RadiometricCamera's linear solves.)

gcv_curve(J, r, D, c; exact_max = 3000, probes = 64, seed) -> (λ -> (; gcv, rss, dof, y))

The same from the rows themselves (J, D dense or sparse): the pencil above for at most exact_max parameters, the data residual r − J y read from the rows (exact where it is small: the normal-equation form's rr − 2w⋅zh + … cancels); past it, per λ one sparse Cholesky of M_λ (CHOLMOD, its symbolic analysis done once: the pattern does not depend on λ) for the solution and a Hutchinson estimate of tr H_λ = tr(M_λ⁻¹JᵀJ) from probes Rademacher vectors drawn from seed (deterministic; the probes read the symmetric zᵀJM_λ⁻¹Jᵀz, H's eigenvalues in [0, 1], so its standard deviation is ≤ √(2 tr H / probes)). Either way the parameters nothing reads are dropped (0 in y).

Camera-model regression

Three verbs regress one camera model onto a source camera over a region of its sensor, by the stack's naming rule (a fit has no guess, a refine needs one):

  • fit_camera(T, source, region; …) is LINEAR: one least-squares solve. For a lens family L it solves the focal rescale and the family's coefficients from the samples' radial relation where the family has a linear form (a lift: Division, Scaramuzza; a fixed projection with an even radial factor: Kannala–Brandt, the polynomial and rational OpenCV models, Fisheye624), and refuses a family with none (DoubleSphere, Unified, ExtendedUnified, a Tilt or FieldOfView term) with an ArgumentError naming the two verbs below. Its targets SplineLens (a CameraModel whose lens is the backward spline of rays over the image) and NoncentralSplineModel (its direction and point splines) are penalized linear least squares. pspline_grid(T, source, region, grid_size) is the spline targets' one penalized solve on a given lattice, grid_size = (columns, rows) in Core's order; fit_camera repeats it at each spacing it tries.
  • refine_camera(guess, source, region; …) is the chart LM from a guess to the exact pixel least-squares minimum of the guess's family (for a SplineLens source also its principal point, aspect and axis).
  • approximate_camera(L, source, region; …) is the two: refine_camera(estimate(fit_camera(L, source, region)), source, region), a family with no linear form starting from its anchor.

A SplineLens camera's K is a gauge, so its ẑ and principal point are wherever its frame put them. optical_axis(camera::BoundedCamera) is the one approximate_camera call that finds the lens's optical axis: the closest radially symmetric camera over the camera's own bound (a circular fisheye's image circle keeps its corners out), with its principal point and its axis free. fit_camera(SplineLens, …) and the SplineLens refine_camera_and_poses turn the camera onto that axis first (Core's axis_gauged).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_camera — Function
fit_camera(::Type{T}, source, region; …) -> FitResult{<model of T>, CameraFitMetadata}

The camera model of type T regressed onto source over region, a region of the source's sensor in its pixel frame (an imaged_region, an Ellipse, a Rect, their ∩; bounded), by ONE linear least squares — a fit has no guess. The objective is the integrated squared pixel error of the round trip, ∫ ‖π_T(π⁻¹_source(u)) − u‖² du over the region. The target type selects the family and the solve:

  • a lens family L <: AbstractLensModel (fit_camera(L, source::CameraModel, region; weight = :image, fixed = ())): a CameraModel with lens L, its focal rescale s (fx′ = s·fx₀, the skew with it) and its coefficients from the samples' radial relation — radius ρ against view angle θ — in one solve. The family's class decides the linear system:
    • a lift (LiftLens: Division, Scaramuzza, BabelCalib's rational lift): the lifted ray (x·N(ρ), D(ρ)) is parallel to the sample's ray, linear in every coefficient and the rescale, its rows weighted to the angle residual times the radius to first order;
    • a fixed projection g(θ) (Perspective, Equidistant, Stereographic, Equisolid, Orthographic) with an even radial factor N(g)/D(g) and decentering deltas — Kannala–Brandt, the polynomial and the rational OpenCV models, Fisheye624: ρ·D(g) = s·g·N(g), linear in s, s·k and d. Without a denominator the row's residual is the pixel error itself, so the fit IS the integral's least squares at a physical K's samples; with one (the OpenCV rational model) the rows are divided by the denominator of a first solve, once. The decentering deltas are zero (the radial relation does not see them);
    • no linear form: a projection with a shape parameter (DoubleSphere, Unified, ExtendedUnified), a Tilt or FieldOfView term — an ArgumentError naming refine_camera and approximate_camera.
    The samples depend on the source's K, by Core's IntrinsicsRole:
    • A PHYSICAL K (a parametric lens): the principal point and ẑ are kept. The samples are the source lens's radial profile r(θ) (its radial part: decentering terms are stripped, warned when non-negligible) on Clenshaw–Curtis nodes. The integral over the region reduces exactly to one over θ, with the density weighted by the fraction of each radius's circle about the principal point that lies in the region (the region must be star-shaped about the principal point, as a convex one containing it is). The domain is the region's farthest radius, capped by the source's own view-angle limit — a region reaching past the radii the source images is clipped and warned (its domain is then the source's own). A region reaching past the TARGET family's view-angle limit (Core's view_angle_limit: a gnomonic family applies its distortion on the perspective plane, which ends at 90°) is a ViewAngleLimitError (the region's angle and the limit in its fields): the family cannot represent the source there, and it is never fitted over a clipped field. (The region's extent is measured along 720 azimuths: a corner between two of them is reached to within half an azimuth's arc.) weight is the density: :image (uniform on the image), :solid_angle (sin θ) or :uniform (in θ).
    • A GAUGE K (a SplineLens camera source, a CameraModel or a BoundedCamera of one) has no radial profile of its own lens. The samples are the CAMERA's rays on the image over the region (the spline targets' quadrature, below), each pixel one sample of equal weight: the same image integral with the :image density (another weight is an ArgumentError). The linear fit reads their radii about the source's principal point and their angles about its ẑ; the principal point, the aspect and the axis are freed by refine_camera (optical_axis). This is the route to a parametric lens, an OpenCV or COLMAP form, for a SplineLens camera: approximate_camera(KannalaBrandt{4}, camera, region).
    • The result reports the linear fit's residual: the round trip's error of the returned camera at each sample, in pixels (metadata.residuals, max_px, max_px_edge; the result's rms their weighted root mean square). The retcode_symbol(result) is :solved, iterations 0; a degenerate solve (a non-positive or non-finite rescale) is :solve_failed (its camera's focals non-finite), never a throw.
    • fixed (fixed = (): every coefficient and the focal solved) holds parameters by name or group, resolved by Core's free_parameter_names as refine_camera's: a held coefficient's column leaves the ONE linear solve and keeps the target's value — the family's anchor (zero) for a type L, or a given LENS's, fit_camera(target::AbstractLensModel, source, region; fixed) (the target lens's family, its held values its own: a calibration's lens target); :f held is the source's focal (s = 1); fixed covering everything returns the target unchanged. The decentering deltas the radial relation does not see keep the target's values. A lift's linear form rescales every coefficient with the focal, so a lift holding a coefficient must hold :f too (an ArgumentError otherwise). The keywords free, fix, fixed_params and hold are refused, naming fixed.
    • Division alone fits Division{1}; any other type must carry its arities (KannalaBrandt{4}). A non-central source (NoncentralSplineModel) is an ArgumentError: not supported yet.
  • SplineLens (fit_camera(SplineLens, source, region; tolerance, grid_size = nothing, forward = true, device = default_device())): a CameraModel with a VisualGeometryToolkitCore SplineLens, its K the source's own (skew 0). Its BACKWARD spline is Core's full-image lattice on the source's image (spline_lattice; the source is not sampled: the solve sets every control), fitted to the source's rays at the normalized points x = K⁻¹u by linear least squares with the P-spline penalty — pspline_grid at grid_size (columns, rows; reported back in the metadata in that order, as Core's grid_size(camera)) when given, else at the lattice THE SPACING SEARCH chooses: the spacing is doubled down from 4 cells along the longer side and then bisected to the LARGEST spacing whose held-out maximum error is at most tolerance (px), retcode_symbol :converged; a fit that cannot reach it at the finest spacing of the bracket (a cell of three quadrature strides) returns retcode_symbol :max_iterations with what it reached. The region restricts the quadrature only, never the lattice: a node whose support misses it is held by the penalty. The tolerance bounds the held-out DIRECTION error in pixels, the non-central target's metric (below). A SplineLens source's backward is kept exactly (its controls ==; a grid_size is then an ArgumentError) and only the forward is fitted. A gauge-K source (a SplineLens camera) is first turned onto its optical axis, ALWAYS: its K's principal point moves to the axis's pixel, every ray turns by the optical_axis rotation (an approximate_camera; Core's axis_gauged, exact; the metadata's rotation, its symmetry_residual), and the forward is fitted there. A physical source's lattice takes its own K and ẑ, already the axis. Its FORWARD spline (forward = true) is a ChartSpline{2} from the equidistant direction chart about ẑ to x, fitted to the camera's own rays over the region by the same penalized solve and the same spacing search, to tolerance in pixels of the held-out round trip; the camera projects through it (NaN outside the fitted chart). Each fit of a search is an outer iteration, counted in the metadata; iterations(result) is 0; the retcode is :converged when every search met the tolerance.
  • NoncentralSplineModel (fit_camera(NoncentralSplineModel, source, region; tolerance, grid_size = nothing, device = default_device())): the same lattice and search on the source's observation LINES — a non-central model's Ray3s, or a central source's rays through its centre. Both splines, the unit directions and the points, come from ONE penalized solve on the same lattice. A line's point is pinned by an anchor: c, Core's nearest_point of all the quadrature lines, and each line contributes its point closest to c, o + ((c* − o)·d) d — bounded for rays at and past 90°, where a plane anchor (the pupil plane) runs off. The points are in the source's frame (frame in = frame out; plain metres, the model's unit), c* in the metadata; a central source's are all c*. The tolerance bounds the held-out DIRECTION error in pixels: the direction's deviation mapped through the source's back-projection Jacobian (the round trip through the source to first order); the line offset is reported beside it, in metres.

The spline targets' source is a BoundedCamera — the lattice is laid over its image (a bare model has none: an ArgumentError) — of a CameraModel, and for the non-central target also of a NoncentralSplineModel. Their quadrature is on the image (they take no weight). Their per-point steps run as batches: a SplineLens source's backprojection is Core's KernelAbstractions kernel on the backend device (default_device() by default: an accelerator when one is functional), its rays brought back to the host; any other source's backprojection, the chart, the design rows, the evaluation and the errors run on the host (pointwise!), and the penalized normal equations are solved by a sparse Cholesky factorization.

The metadata (CameraFitMetadata) records the region, the round trip's residuals, max_px and max_px_edge, the family's own record and no scale (ScaleNone: no camera regression has a robust loss); the result's rms is the round trip's root mean square in pixels. For the spline targets these are their held-out errors.

region = imaged_region(with_lens_coverage(camera, image_circle(camera, 8.0mm)))
kb = fit_camera(KannalaBrandt{4}, model(camera), region)                       # a parametric lens, linearly
kb = approximate_camera(KannalaBrandt{4}, model(camera), region)               # and its exact pixel least squares
sl = fit_camera(SplineLens, camera, region; tolerance = 1.0e-3)                 # its backward grid and forward spline
project(estimate(sl), ray)                                                     # its pixel through the forward, NaN outside the fitted chart

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.refine_camera — Function
refine_camera(guess::CameraModel, source, region; fixed = (), weight = :image, max_iter, device = default_device(),
              optimization_strategy = nothing) -> FitResult{<:CameraModel, CameraFitMetadata}

The camera of guess's lens family polished onto source over region (a region of the source's sensor in its pixel frame, as fit_camera takes it): the exact minimizer of the integrated squared pixel error of the round trip, ∫ ‖π(π⁻¹_source(u)) − u‖² du, by the stack's chart LM from guess. It is a refine: it needs the guess, and it converges to the minimum nearest it. fit_camera gives a guess where the family has a linear form; approximate_camera is the two in one call.

  • What moves: everything but fixed, the guess's parameters held by name (parameter_names(guess): the intrinsics' :f, :aspect, :cx, :cy, then the lens's) or by group (a coefficient family's stem: :k the radial numerator, :d its denominator, :p the tangential terms, :sx/:sy the thin prism's); the gauge's skew is held by construction. A held parameter keeps the guess's value. :f is the focal rescale s from the guess's focal (relative to the source's, fx′ = s·fx₀); a forward lens's radial factor moves its numerator and its denominator (fixed = (:d,) holds the denominator); a lift moves every coefficient. A name or group the guess does not have is an ArgumentError, and so are the keywords free, fix, fixed_params and hold (naming fixed).
  • A physical K (a parametric source): the principal point, the aspect and ẑ are the source's, data, so the guess's principal point must be the source's (an ArgumentError otherwise) and :aspect, :cx, :cy are held, as are the decentering terms (tangential, thin prism) its azimuth-0 profile does not see. The samples are the source lens's radial profile (its radial part: decentering terms are stripped, warned when non-negligible) on Clenshaw–Curtis nodes, the exact reduction of the image integral to θ, with the density weight (:image, :solid_angle or :uniform) and the region's coverage of each radius (the region star-shaped about the principal point).
  • A gauge K (a SplineLens camera, a CameraModel or a BoundedCamera of one): the principal point, the aspect and the axis are free, from the guess's principal point and aspect and from the source's ẑ. The samples are the camera's own rays on the image over the region, each pixel one sample of equal weight (the :image density; another weight is an ArgumentError). The fitted camera's principal point is the axis's pixel, and metadata.rotation is the turn from the source's frame to the fitted one; metadata.symmetry_residual (px) is the azimuthal misfit no radially symmetric camera represents. optical_axis is this refine of the Fisheye624 family.
  • The residual is one 2-D residual per sample: a forward lens's is its pixel minus the sample's (projected_radius along the ray's azimuth, finite past a fold of its profile); a lift's is angle-style, in the equidistant chart about ẑ, scaled by the sample's normalized radius.
  • The device. Each sample's residual and its Jacobian rows, and their reduction to the normal equations, are one KernelAbstractions kernel on the backend device; only the chart LM's N × N step is the host's.
  • The loss. optimization_strategy = nothing, the plain least squares, is the only one: a camera-from-camera regression has no outliers. It takes no scale or width (one given would be ignored: an ArgumentError), and the result records no scale (scale_source ScaleNone, scale NaN; noise_scale refuses it).

The domain is the region's farthest radius, capped by the source's own view-angle limit, as fit_camera's; a region past the family's view-angle limit (the perspective plane's 90° for a gnomonic family) is a ViewAngleLimitError. At most max_iter accepted iterations (configs/default.toml [refine_camera]); the termination record (retcode, stop_rule) is the LM's, a refine that ends at a non-finite focal rescale is (ReturnCode.Failure, NonFinite) (:solve_failed), never a throw, and one with nothing free (the guess returned, no LM) is a closed form, (ReturnCode.Success, ClosedForm). The metadata is CameraFitMetadata: the round trip's errors per sample in residuals (px), their maximum and edge-band maximum, the result's rms their weighted root mean square.

A NoncentralSplineModel source is an ArgumentError: not supported yet.

start = estimate(fit_camera(KannalaBrandt{4}, camera, region))       # the linear fit: the guess
kb = refine_camera(start, camera, region)                              # its exact pixel least squares
kb = approximate_camera(KannalaBrandt{4}, camera, region)              # the same two steps

approximate_camera(::Type{L}, source, region; weight = :image, max_iter, device = default_device())
    -> FitResult{<:CameraModel, CameraFitMetadata}

The camera of lens family L closest to source over region, from no guess: refine_camera(estimate(fit_camera(L, source, region)), source, region) — the linear fit's camera is the refine's start (fit_camera, refine_camera). VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.optical_axis — Function
optical_axis(camera::BoundedCamera) -> FitResult{<:CameraModel, CameraFitMetadata}

The radially symmetric camera closest to camera over the camera's own bound, its imaged_region (the sensor ∩ its lens coverage ∩ the model's valid domain). It is approximate_camera of the stack's Fisheye624 family (COLMAP's RAD_TAN_THIN_PRISM_FISHEYE, Lens(Equidistant(), Additive(Tangential, ThinPrism{2}) ∘ Radial{Even, 6}), its decentering held at zero) onto camera, that one call, nothing more: the linear fit of its radial coefficients and focal, then the refine with the principal point and the axis free.

  • The region is the bound, never a default. A circular fisheye's lens coverage (its image circle) keeps the corners out, where a grid's rays are its extrapolation. A whole-sensor fit is a camera whose bound is the whole sensor.
  • A bare model has no bound: an ArgumentError. Wrap it in BoundedCamera(model, size; lens_coverage).
  • For a SplineLens camera (a gauge K), the principal point and the axis are free. Read them from the result.
    • The optical axis in the camera's frame is rotation(metadata(r).rotation)' * ẑ.
    • Its pixel is the fitted camera's principal point.
    • metadata(r).symmetry_residual (px) is the azimuthal misfit that no radially symmetric camera represents. A decentred lens reports it.
  • A physical K is its own axis: the identity rotation and the source's principal point.

Core's axis_gauged(camera, rotation(metadata(r).rotation), calibration_matrix(estimate(r))) then turns the camera so ẑ is the axis. fit_camera(SplineLens, …) and the SplineLens refine_camera_and_poses do this first, always.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.pspline_grid — Function
pspline_grid(T, source, region, grid_size; device = default_device()) -> FitResult{<:model of T, CameraFitMetadata}

The P-spline (penalized B-spline least squares, Eilers–Marx) of a spline camera's lattice onto source over region, on the GIVEN lattice: T is SplineLens (a CameraModel with a SplineLens, its backward spline, no forward) or NoncentralSplineModel (its direction and point splines). The lattice is Core's full-image layout of grid_size = (columns, rows) nodes on the source's image (spline_lattice, the source's own K), the region restricting the quadrature only, ONE penalized solve with the third-difference penalty fit_camera uses — the non-central model's points anchored on c*, its directions and points one right-hand side — in the source's frame. No spacing search and no tolerance: the one deterministic least-squares step fit_camera(T, source, region; tolerance) repeats at each spacing it tries (the same implementation), so pspline_grid(T, source, region, (gw, gh)) at the lattice fit_camera chose is its backward.

source is a BoundedCamera (the lattice needs the image: a bare model is an ArgumentError) of a CameraModel or, for the non-central target, also of a NoncentralSplineModel. The result's retcode_symbol is :solved, iterations 0; its metadata is fit_camera's, the held-out errors (the direction error in pixels; for the non-central model also the line offsets in metres, and center) and family = (; λ, grid_size, stride, n_held_out_dropped, …) with grid_size the lattice's (columns, rows), as fit_camera reports it, and no tolerance or outer_iterations.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.CameraFitMetadata — Type
CameraFitMetadata{R, F}

FitResult metadata of fit_camera, refine_camera and approximate_camera: the region fitted over, the weight measure (:image for every target but a lens family), the round trip's error in pixels — residuals (per sample), max_px (their maximum) and max_px_edge (their maximum within 100 px of the region's edge); √ of their weighted mean square, the objective's quantity, is the result's rms — the target family's own record family, a NamedTuple, and:

  • rotation: from the source's camera frame to the fitted camera's, a frame-labelled RotationMap (ViewFrame{:source} → ViewFrame{:fitted}; one type for every fit). For a source whose K is physical it is exactly the identity: its ẑ is the axis, not fitted. A gauge-K (SplineLens) source's refine gives the fitted rotation, for a lens family and for the SplineLens target (its axis gauge) alike; a linear fit keeps the source's ẑ.
  • symmetry_residual: px. The root mean square of the round trip's azimuthal component about the fitted principal point, the misfit that no radially symmetric camera about the fitted axis represents. It is NaN where not measured: a physical K's radial profile, a lattice fit of a physical source.
  • weights, scale, scale_source: the robust stages' weight and scale record. No camera regression has a robust loss: its weights are Core's lazy unit_weights (one per sample, binary by weight_kind), scale is NaN and scale_source ScaleNone, so noise_scale(result) refuses to hand it on.

The family records:

  • a lens family: (; focal_scale, aspect, θ_max, θ_source, clipped). These are the joint focal rescale s, the aspect fy′/fy (1 for a physical K and for a linear fit), the fitted and the source's angular domains (radians), and whether the region reached past the radii the source images (its domain then the source's own). The errors are at the quadrature's samples;
  • SplineLens: (; λ, grid_size, stride, outer_iterations, n_held_out_dropped, tolerance, forward), forward being (; spacing, counts, λ, outer_iterations, max_px, max_px_edge, rms) or nothing — the backward lattice's P-spline weight λ (nothing for a SplineLens source's kept backward), its (columns, rows) (Core's grid_size), the quadrature's stride in pixels, the spacing search's fits (0 for a given or kept lattice), the held-out pixels dropped (below) and the tolerance asked for; then the forward spline's knot spacing h (radians in the chart), its control counts per chart axis, its λ, its search's fits and its held-out round trip ‖K·forward(chart(ray)) − u‖ in pixels (maximum, edge-band maximum, rms) — forward is nothing with forward = false. The result's errors (residuals, max_px, max_px_edge, rms) are the backward's held-out direction error, as the non-central target's (below), over the held-out pixels where the source's back-projection Jacobian is finite — n_held_out_dropped counts the others;
  • NoncentralSplineModel: (; λ, grid_size, stride, tolerance, outer_iterations, center, max_offset, rms_offset, max_offset_edge, n_held_out_dropped) — center the anchor c* (the least-squares point nearest to the source's quadrature lines, in the source's frame, plain metres as the model's points), and the held-out line offset ‖(I − ddᵀ)(o_fit − o_src)‖ (plain metres); the pixel errors are the held-out DIRECTION error, mapped to pixels through the source's back-projection Jacobian.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.ViewAngleLimitError — Type
ViewAngleLimitError <: Exception

The refusal of a camera-from-camera regression (fit_camera, refine_camera, approximate_camera) whose region reaches past the target family's view-angle limit: the family cannot represent the source there, and it is never fitted over a clipped field. Its fields carry the measurement, so a caller can catch it and record the case (a fisheye × rational cell as not applicable, quoting the angle) without reading the message:

  • family::Type: the target lens family;
  • field_angle::Float64: the region's largest view angle off the source's axis, in RADIANS (the source's own limit where the region reaches past the radii it images);
  • limit::Float64: the family's view-angle limit, in RADIANS (Core's view_angle_limit of the family: the perspective plane's π/2 − 10⁻³ for a gnomonic family, which applies its distortion on that plane).

showerror prints the refusal: for a gnomonic family, "this target applies its distortion on the perspective plane (θ < 90°), but the region reaches <θ>°. Use a lift (division) model, or the same radial factor in an equidistant frame"; for any other, its limit and the region's angle.

try
    fit_camera(L, camera, region)
catch e
    e isa ViewAngleLimitError || rethrow()
    rad2deg(e.field_angle), rad2deg(e.limit)        # the measured field and the family's limit, degrees
end
