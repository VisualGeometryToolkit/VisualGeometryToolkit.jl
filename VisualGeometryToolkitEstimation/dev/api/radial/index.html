Radial and Lens Calibration

These estimators calibrate a camera with lens distortion from correspondences between planar calibration boards and their images.

Radial homography

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RadialHomographyProblem — Type
RadialHomographyProblem(pairs; center, sampler=nothing, backend=NativeSolver(),
                        extra_check=nothing, minimal=:radial)

Construct a radially distorted homography problem (one-sided division model on the target side) from 2D-2D point correspondences.

  • center: the distortion center in TARGET pixels, REQUIRED — the target side is normalized about it (Hartley, mean distance √2). Data, not configuration: the division model is meaningless without its center.
  • extra_check: true (the default when unset) keeps only the cubic root consistent with the redundant 4.5-pt equation per minimal sample (the reference's RANSAC setting); false scores all real roots. It selects a root of the 5-point kernel, so supplying it with minimal = :homography is an ArgumentError rather than a setting that silently does nothing.
  • minimal = :radial: the hypothesis path — :radial samples five correspondences for the 5-point (H, k) kernel; :homography samples four for the plain homography, the (H, k) local optimization supplying k on the consensus (header, point 4).
  • pairs (and its :score prior)/sampler/backend: as HomographyProblem.
VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.find_radial_homography — Function
find_radial_homography(correspondences; center,
    threshold, extent=nothing,
    workspace=nothing, extra_check=nothing, minimal=:radial,
    local_optimization, max_trials, min_trials, config=nothing, rng=...)

threshold and local_optimization default to configs/default.toml [pipelines.radial_homography], max_trials and min_trials to its [ransac] table; a keyword overrides. threshold builds the default scoring only; supplying it with scoring is an ArgumentError. extra_check is the radial 5-point kernel's root filter (see RadialHomographyProblem) and is refused with minimal = :homography.

Robust radially distorted homography estimation (one-sided one-parameter division model on the TARGET side; Wadenbäck et al. (2026)) via RANSAC with MSAC scoring. minimal = :homography pairs the global search for a plain homography (four-point hypotheses, Homography4pt) with this problem's (H, k) local optimization: the hypothesis carries no distortion term, k is seeded on its consensus by the closed-form least squares in k given H and the joint manifold step estimates both, and the correspondences a plane homography cannot hold under distortion enter at the re-score. The default :radial samples five for the 5-point kernel, whose root pick fails on boards too small to identify k from alone.

Returns a RansacEstimate whose model is a VisualGeometryToolkitCore.RadialHomography in the DATA (pixel) frame — H, k, and the distortion center all carried in the model — or nothing for fewer than 5 correspondences. iterations(result) is the number of trials (hypotheses drawn); test converged(result) before reading the model (see RansacEstimate).

center — the distortion center in target pixels — is REQUIRED: the 5-pt solver runs in the Hartley frame about it.

local_optimization defaults to Irls{TLSShape}() (least squares on the inliers) on the 9-dof sl(3) ⊕ k manifold, via the joint 9-dof LM M-step. The chart-rows provider makes LocalOptimizationDoneRight's lifts available on the same manifold.

The other keywords as find_homography.

Units: the source points are plain numbers. A radial homography maps from the source plane, so its entries carry that plane's unit, which a matrix cannot record: physical (unitful) source points are an ArgumentError — strip them in the unit the rest of your plain data uses — metres, as the stack's boundaries strip (ustrip.(m, X)).

refine_radial_homography(model, correspondences; threshold = 3.0, extent = nothing,
    local_optimization = Irls{TLSShape}(), scoring = nothing, workspace = nothing)
    -> RansacEstimate

The radially distorted homography model (VisualGeometryToolkitCore RadialHomography: H, k and the distortion centre, in the data frame) refined against correspondences: find_radial_homography's step without the sampling. The problem's distortion centre is the model's own (center(model)); minimal and extra_check choose find_radial_homography's hypotheses and have no meaning here. local_optimization is the radial homography's RANSAC local-optimization step, run here alone from the given model (no sampling). Radial camera

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RadialCameraProblem — Type
RadialCameraProblem(pairs; center, sampler = nothing)

The radial-camera problem from plane_point => raw_pixel pairs of one board. center — the distortion centre in pixels — is REQUIRED (data, not configuration); a Csponds :score attribute column drives PROSAC sampling, and every attribute column travels with the data.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.find_radial_camera — Function
find_radial_camera(correspondences; center,
    threshold = 3.0, extent = nothing, workspace = nothing,
    local_optimization, max_trials = 10000, min_trials = 100,
    config = nothing, rng = ...)

Robust estimation of one board's 1-D radial camera (VisualGeometryToolkitCore RadialCamera: rows 1–2 of the plane homography, invariant to focal length and to any radially symmetric distortion about the centre) from plane_point => raw_pixel correspondences, via RANSAC on the linear 5-point solve with MSAC scoring on the point–radial-line distance. Returns a RansacEstimate whose model lives about the distortion centre center (pixels, REQUIRED), or nothing for fewer than 5 correspondences. iterations(result) is the number of trials (hypotheses drawn); test converged(result) before reading the model (see RansacEstimate).

The uncalibrated verification model for board correspondences: no distortion parameter is estimated per board (that is the focal stack's job, across boards), so the solve is closed form with no root to pick. threshold and local_optimization default to configs/default.toml [pipelines.radial_camera] — least squares on the inliers, Irls{TLSShape}() — and the trial bounds to its [ransac] table. threshold builds the default scoring only; supplying it with scoring is an ArgumentError. The other keywords as find_homography.

Units: the source points are plain numbers. A radial camera maps from the source plane, so its entries carry that plane's unit, which a matrix cannot record: physical (unitful) source points are an ArgumentError — strip them in the unit the rest of your plain data uses — metres, as the stack's boundaries strip (ustrip.(m, X)).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_radial_camera — Function
fit_radial_camera(cs::AbstractVector{<:Cspond{<:StaticVector{2}, <:StaticVector{2}}};
                  center, whitening = SampsonWhitening(), iterations = 2)
    -> FitResult{RadialCamera{Float64}, Nothing}

The radial camera of one board from its plane_point => image_point correspondences cs, the image points RAW as measured; center (pixels, REQUIRED — the principal point, as find_radial_camera and RadialCameraProblem require it) is the pencil's centre and rides on the returned camera, so nothing is pre-centred. There is no default: a pencil about the image corner fits raw pixels as well as any other, with no error. Each pair gives one linear constraint on the six entries of P (constraint_with_jacobians); the null vector is solved by SVD in the radial camera's normalization, CspondNormalization(RadialCamera, cs; center) (Hartley at the plane centroid, Hartley about center on the image side — the same strategy find_radial_camera's problem solves in), and composed back into pixels. Each iteration re-solves; from the second on, every row is whitened under whitening at the previous iteration's solution (SampsonWhitening turns the algebraic error into the exact point–radial-line distance); a weights column on cs (a Csponds attribute, e.g. withattrs(cs; weights)) multiplies on top, the row weight being √w. This is how the BabelCalib back-end obtains the radial camera when no homography is available; when one is, its rows 1–2 are the same object (RadialCamera(H)). Five correspondences determine P (five degrees of freedom).

The FitResult's estimate is the camera, retcode :solved after iterations iterations (its iterations field), and rms the point–radial-line distance (px) of the n pairs at the fit, whatever the whitening policy. When the constraint rows leave more than one null direction (a degenerate configuration — the plane points on a line, or a pixel on the centre) the retcode is :degenerate, the camera NaN and the rms NaN.

Units: the source points are plain numbers. A radial camera maps from the source plane, so its entries carry that plane's unit, which a matrix cannot record: physical (unitful) source points are an ArgumentError — strip them in the unit the rest of your plain data uses — metres, as the stack's boundaries strip (ustrip.(m, X)).

Division camera

fit_division_camera fits a camera with a division lens model to several board views at once. division_camera_step is the model update it repeats under its current weights.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_division_camera — Function
fit_division_camera(radial_cameras::AbstractDict{K}, views::AbstractDict{K};
                    image_size = nothing, focal_seed = nothing, ncoef, k = nothing, max_iter, outlier_halfwidth,   # defaults: configs/default.toml [fit_division_camera]
                    whitening = SampsonWhitening(), verbose = false)
    -> FitResult{CameraModel, DivisionCameraMetadata}

The BabelCalib linear focal solve as an iterated, gradient-whitened, robustly re-weighted estimator (see the file header). Keyed by the view's identity K (the key sets must be equal — ArgumentError): radial_cameras[id] is view id's radial camera (a RadialCamera, or a homography whose rows 1–2 are read) and views[id] its plane_point => raw_pixel correspondences — per-board VALIDATED inliers (the cameras carry the pencil's shared centre) — the inputs of division_camera_step. A view's weights column, if it has one, is a prior row weight: iteration 0 is weighted by it and each later iteration by it times the posterior. k fixes the division coefficient (centred pixels); otherwise ncoef are estimated. Each iteration whitens every row at the previous solution under whitening and weights it by the Student-t/uniform posterior of the previous iteration's whitened residuals.

INPUT CONTRACT: each view is that board's VALIDATED correspondences — the inliers its radial camera was found with — not raw detections. The Student-t/uniform posterior absorbs what validation leaves (a few per cent of ray-aligned mismatches, which a radial camera's 1-D line test cannot reject); it is not a substitute for it.

THE BRANCH AUDIT. Each view's pose branch — the tilt ambiguity of its radial camera — is chosen on the iteration that first places the view, from that view's own solve, and kept. That solve cannot separate the tilt pair by residual, so one mismatch pushed along its ray through the centre can tip it (on the MC scene at 3 % ray-aligned mismatches, 16 % of the views carrying one took a branch 48–85° off; none of the clean views), and the view's inliers then carry residuals far above sigma — after the posterior has already rejected the mismatch. So once the IRLS converges — or, if it is still moving at half the max_iter budget, there (a wrong branch is what keeps a stack from converging) — one iteration re-judges every view's branch at the shared (f, k), robustly; if it moves any, the IRLS resumes from there within what is left of max_iter (the audit counts as an iteration, so iterations never exceeds max_iter); if it moves none the IRLS continues unchanged, bit for bit (clean stacks). Never after :solve_failed, nor at max_iter = 0. Measured: 104 → 4 wrong views at 3 %, 66 → 1 at 5 %, no correct view moved, and the held-out residuals calibrated against ŝ²·I₂ in the tail as well as the bulk (test/ransac_mc_calibration_tests.jl (e)).

Inputs, k, outlier_halfwidth, focal_seed and every output are in pixels; the stack is conditioned inside by one normalization (CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center) — Hartley about the radial cameras' shared centre), built once. image_size = (width, height) (pixels) of the image the views were measured in is REQUIRED for the default seed only (ArgumentError when both it and focal_seed are missing): its half-diagonal s = hypot(width, height)/2, a 90° diagonal field of view. With focal_seed given, image_size may be omitted and does not enter the result.

Iteration 0 has no previous solution: it is whitened at a SEED camera — focal focal_seed (pixels; by default s), k₀ = 0 (the given k when k fixes it — the known-k rows are exact only there), each board at the depth that camera implies — which sets the row WEIGHTS only; f and k are solved by the iteration. The plain algebraic rows it replaces weigh each board by its depth (a far board's rows ~10× a near one's), so a few mismatches on a heavy board dragged the joint solve to f ≈ 45–300 px against 1226 and half those stacks never recovered; the seed fixes that and is robust to a seed 0.5×–3× off (measured 2026-09-22; see test/ransac_mc_calibration_tests.jl (e)). Supply a better focal as data — a previous calibration, EXIF — through focal_seed.

outlier_halfwidth (pixels) is the half-width of the uniform outlier component — the same quantity the RANSAC problems take; it must NOT be the residual scale (a half-width at the median residual makes the outlier component as dense as the inliers and zeroes every weight — measured). The posterior is seeded from the median residual (median_seeded_posterior): every point starts an inlier and the M-step shrinks ŝ as the model improves.

That seed and schedule are load-bearing, not incidental. Five alternatives were measured against RVG's synthetic (known f₀, 0.4 px noise, 8 % gross outliers) on 2026-09-10 and every one was worse: a frozen MAD anchor 6.5 % off f₀, a global Jeffreys sweep 3.5 %, a per-board sweep 5.1 %, frozen Welsch 28 %, Welsch with an adaptive scale 85 % (no uniform component ⇒ the scale update has no fixed point and ran to ŝ = 29). This estimator lands at 1.25 %. A sweep seed needs residuals that are already bimodal; iteration 0 here is one linear solve of an uncalibrated stack (whitened at a seed, not at a fit), so they are not. (Those measurements predate the seeded iteration 0.)

max_iter = 0 returns iteration 0 alone — whitened at the seed and robustly unweighted, retcode :max_iterations, no branch audit — which is

N = CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center)   # center: the cameras' shared centre
CameraModel(division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras, views; ncoef, k, whiten_at = focal_seed))

bit for bit (with focal_seed the half-diagonal by default). The robust re-weighting this function exists for begins at iteration 1. A stack whose iteration 0 cannot solve at all (no board admits a pose, none carries depth, the solve is singular) raises the DivisionCameraError — there is no iterate to return; a later iteration that cannot solve ends the fit on the last good one (:solve_failed).

The radial cameras are FIXED. Each view's radial camera is an input, estimated once by the caller — BlobBoards fits every one linearly about the centre, iterated to its consensus and polished by a local optimization on the exact point–radial-line distance — and this stage only turns them into an initial focal length, coefficients and depths for a bundle. It used to re-fit them per iteration under the point weights, which could not improve a nonlinear fit and did wreck one: the perturbation tipped the metric upgrade's cheirality test onto the mirrored branch for a board the shared solve had placed behind the camera, turning its pose 179.95° and its whitened residual from 1.7 px to 1102 px, and through the Student-t width that single row collapsed every weight in the solve — 18 of 106 boards lost their poses, boards whose own poses under the converged bundle camera were the BEST in the set at 0.14 px (measured 2026-09-09, fisheye hallway). Hand this function cameras you are willing to stand behind; it will not launder contaminated ones.

Returns the camera CameraModel(::DivisionCameraState) builds — K = diag(f, f, 1) at the cameras' shared centre, Division{ncoef} lens in K's chart — plus the final keyed DivisionCameraState (pixels), the views with each correspondence's posterior weight as their weights column, the Student-t scale sigma = ŝ (pixels — a LINEAR sigma), ν, and the loop outcome. rms (pixels) and n count the rows that entered the fit: a row with prior weight zero, or of a view the final iteration left without a pose, is not a residual.

Units: the board points are plain numbers. The radial cameras and the board poses carry the board plane's unit, which neither a matrix nor this result records, so physical (unitful) board points are an ArgumentError: strip them in the unit the radial cameras were fit in (the stack's own boundaries strip in metres, ustrip.(m, X)).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.division_camera_step — Function
division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras::AbstractDict{K}, views::AbstractDict{K};
                     ncoef = 1, k = nothing, whiten_at, whitening)
    -> DivisionCameraState{K}

A declared INNER-LOOP piece (public, not exported): it takes the estimator's normalization N and pixel data, and returns pixel state. N is REQUIRED — CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center), the one strategy fit_division_camera builds — applied to the data inside, never built here; there is no fallback.

One iteration of the stacked linear focal solve via the division-model lift (BabelCalib's back-end back-substitution, Lochman et al. (2021)), as a DivisionCameraState: the shared unknowns plus the per-board poses and the whitened per-point residuals the next iteration needs. Square pixels are assumed. Both inputs are keyed by the view's identity K (the key sets must be equal — ArgumentError): radial_cameras[id] is view id's radial camera (a RadialCamera, or a HomographyMat whose rows 1–2 are read) in the SAME plane frame as views[id], that view's plane_point => image_point correspondences with the RAW (distorted) image point in pixels. The pencil's centre rides on the cameras, must be shared across the stack, and is carried by the result; a bare HomographyMat means the origin.

Per board, the radial camera fixes the rotation and (t₁, t₂) up to four sign candidates; the remaining unknowns are LINEAR in the division-model lift (x, y, 1 + Σₚ kₚ r²ᵖ) ∝ (X, Y, Z + t₃) (RadialLift). The candidate is chosen by cheirality and residual, and every board is stacked into one least-squares system with the shared unknowns and one t₃ per board. A view's weights column (a Csponds attribute, e.g. withattrs(cs; weights)) weighs its rows — a board-level vote folds into it losslessly, the row weight being √w; views with no admissible candidate are dropped, and a DivisionCameraError is thrown if none survive or the stack yields f ≤ 0 (the argument contract still raises ArgumentError). With k given (the one-parameter division coefficient in the centered-pixel frame, e.g. RadialHomography.k), only f and the t₃'s are estimated (ncoef must stay 1); otherwise the stack carries ncoef ≥ 1 extra shared columns λₚ = kₚ·f and estimates the coefficients of r², r⁴, … too.

N is the normalization the stack is solved in: its image side must be an isotropic scale about the radial cameras' shared centre, its plane side an isotropic similarity (ArgumentError otherwise). The state is returned in pixels regardless. whiten_at whitens every row under whitening (the residual-currency policy, SampsonWhitening() by default) at

  • a previous iteration's DivisionCameraState — its solution, each view in the branch that iteration chose; or
  • a SEED focal length (a positive Real, data units): the pinhole f, k₀ = 0 (the given k when k fixes it), each view in its freshly chosen branch at the depth that camera implies. The seed sets the WEIGHTS only — f and k are still solved by this iteration. It puts iteration 0 in pixel currency, where the plain rows weigh each view by its depth (a far board's rows ~10× a near one's); see fit_division_camera.

The camera an iteration determines is CameraModel(state): K = diag(f, f, 1) with the principal point at the cameras' shared centre, and a Division{ncoef} lens re-expressed in that K's normalized frame (kₚ,norm = kₚ,px · f^(2p), VGC's radial-coefficient law — the lens law of the CameraModel(model, K′) constructor).

ONE ITERATION, not an estimator. Without whiten_at there is nothing to linearize at, so the SOLVE is plain algebraic least squares (whitening still sets the currency of the returned sq_residuals, which the robust driver reweights by). The robust driver's iteration 0 is this step at its seed,

N = CspondNormalization(DivisionCameraState, views; center)
CameraModel(division_camera_step(N, radial_cameras, views; ncoef, whiten_at = focal_seed))
estimate(fit_division_camera(radial_cameras, views; ncoef, focal_seed, max_iter = 0))

the same camera, bit for bit. For a converged estimate call the robust driver.

Units: the board points are plain numbers. The radial cameras and the board poses carry the board plane's unit, which neither a matrix nor this result records, so physical (unitful) board points are an ArgumentError: strip them in the unit the radial cameras were fit in (the stack's own boundaries strip in metres, ustrip.(m, X)).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.DivisionCameraState — Type
DivisionCameraState{K}

Result of one iteration of the stacked linear focal solve, in DATA (pixel) units, keyed by the views' identity K: f; the division coefficients k (of r², r⁴, … in pixels centred on centre); the pencil's centre, the principal point every input radial camera shares; poses[id], the board pose — an EuclideanMap{3} from the board plane (z = 0) into the camera, rotation from the radial camera, translation (t₁₂, t₃) — in the plane's units, of each view that admitted one (a view without a pose this iteration is absent); and sq_residuals[id], the whitened residual norms² of the view's correspondences (pixel², in its order; NaN for a view without a pose) — the currency the robust re-weighting reads. ids is the sorted key order. Feed it back as whiten_at for the next iteration; CameraModel(state) is the camera it determines.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.DivisionCameraError — Type
DivisionCameraError(msg)

The stacked division-camera solve (division_camera_step) has no system to solve for the boards it was given: no board admits a pose with its points in front of the camera and imaged on the same side of the centre, no board carries depth information (every row weighted to zero or at the centre), the shared unknowns are not determined or not finite, or the focal length comes out non-positive. A condition of the DATA, not of the arguments — the argument contract (one correspondence set per camera, one shared centre, …) still raises ArgumentError — so a caller that can go on without the solve catches this type and nothing else.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.RadialLift — Type
RadialLift{N,T}

The division-lift constraint model of ONE board: the partial pose (R, t12) from its radial camera and the linear unknowns θ = (f, λ₁..λ_N, t₃) — f, the lift coefficients λₚ = kₚ·f of r²ᵖ, and the board's depth. For a plane point X and its RAW centered pixel x the constraint is

g = x·(Z + t₃) − X_c·(f + Σₚ λₚ ρ²ᵖ)   (two rows; X_c = camera-frame point at t₃ = 0)

which is LINEAR in θ: g = G_θ·θ + c₀, c₀ = x·Z. constraint_with_jacobians returns g, the measurement Jacobian G_x = ∂g/∂x (2×2) and G_θ (2×(N+2)), and whiten turns them into pixel-currency rows. A KNOWN k folds λ₁ = k·f into the f column (chain rule) — see _lift_columns.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.division_coeffs — Function

The iteration's division coefficients k (of r², r⁴, … in pixels about the centre).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.board_sq_residuals — Function
board_sq_residuals(state::DivisionCameraState{K}) -> Dict{K, Vector{Float64}}

The whitened residual norms² of every correspondence, per view in its order (pixel²) — the currency the robust re-weighting reads.

Camera and poses

refine_camera_and_poses refines a camera — its intrinsics and lens, on VisualGeometryToolkitCore's ParameterManifold — jointly with one pose per view (LeftRigidManifold6) against the reprojection error of 3D → 2D correspondences, from a guess of both: the bundle of a calibration with a known target, for any lens the camera's family holds (a fisheye's rays past 90° are ordinary data). free = () holds the camera and refines the poses alone.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.refine_camera_and_poses — Function
refine_camera_and_poses(camera::CameraModel, poses, views; free, box = nothing,
                        max_iter, ftol, xtol, max_time = nothing)   # defaults: configs/default.toml [refine_camera_and_poses]
    -> FitResult{<:NamedTuple{(:camera, :poses)}, CameraPosesRefineMetadata}

Refine a camera and one pose per view jointly against the reprojection error of 3D → 2D correspondences: minimize Σ ‖project(camera, pose_v(X)) − u‖² over the camera's free parameters and every view's pose, from the guesses camera and poses. A refine: both guesses are required, nothing is re-estimated from scratch and the camera keeps its family (its lens type).

Arguments

  • camera::CameraModel — the camera guess: intrinsics and lens, the parameters VisualGeometryToolkitCore's parameter_vector(camera) lists (f, aspect, skew, cx, cy, then the lens's coefficients), in pixels.
  • poses — one world → camera EuclideanMap{3} per view (X_c = R·X + t, X in the target's frame), keyed like views.
  • views — per view, its correspondences cs = [X => u, …]: X a target point (3-vector), u the pixel it is detected at (2-vector). Grouped data: an AbstractDict{K, <:AbstractVector{<:Cspond}} keyed by the view, with poses::AbstractDict{K} of the same keys (block order: the sorted keys; an ArgumentError when the key sets differ), or a vector of views with a vector of poses of the same length. Every point must project at the guess (in front of the camera, inside the lens's domain): an ArgumentError names the first that does not.

Keywords (configuration)

  • free — the camera parameters refined, by name (every parameter but the skew by default); () holds the camera fixed and refines the poses alone.
  • box — a solver search box on the camera's parameters by name (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's ParameterManifold(camera; box)), or nothing; with free = () (nothing to bound) a box is an ArgumentError.
  • max_iter — the LM's cap on ACCEPTED iterations (NLLSsolver's count); ftol — its relative cost decrease and the stall postcondition's exact-fit test; xtol — its step tolerance (chart units); max_time — its wall-clock cap in seconds (nothing or Inf: none), for a camera whose projection makes an iteration slow; it stops with :max_time.

The estimate is (; camera, poses): the refined camera, and the refined poses keyed like the input (a Dict by the same keys, or a vector in view order, each with its input's frame labels). retcode is :converged only at a stationary point (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's nlls_retcode), else :max_iterations, :max_time, :solve_failed or :stalled; rms is √(Σ‖rᵢ‖² / n) over the n correspondences (pixels). The metadata (CameraPosesRefineMetadata) carries the residual rows, s², ν and the free parameter names. Plain least squares: the correspondences must be inliers.

Units

Plain inputs give plain poses; physical ones (unitful poses and target points, all one form) are read in metres at entry and the poses come back physical, in metres. The residuals are pixels either way.

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.CameraPosesRefineMetadata — Type
CameraPosesRefineMetadata

FitResult metadata of refine_camera_and_poses: residuals, the reprojection rows at the estimate (pixels, two per correspondence: views in block order, each view's correspondences in their order); s², the residual variance Σ‖rᵢ‖² / ν (px²); ν = 2n − p, its degrees of freedom (p the free camera parameters plus 6 per view); and free, the names of the camera parameters refined. All forward as properties: result.residuals, ….

Lens fitting

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.fit_lens — Function
fit_lens(::Type{L}, model::CameraModel;
         max_view_angle = nothing, max_radius_px = nothing, n = 128, weight = :image,
         fix_focal = false, max_iter, direction = :forward)   # max_iter: configs/default.toml [fit_lens]
    -> FitResult{CameraModel, LensFitMetadata}

Regress lens family L against model's lens by minimizing the integral of the squared radial-profile difference over the shared view-angle domain (see the file header: Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature on Chebyshev nodes, so the discrete objective IS the integral). The focal scale s is fit jointly (frames differ in slope at the center — DoubleSphere especially), so the result is a full CameraModel: K scaled by s, principal point kept.

  • max_view_angle: optional cap on the fit domain, a HALF-angle — the largest view angle θ from the optical axis fitted, in radians (VGC view_angle, view_angle_limit); not the full angle of view, Core's fov(camera). Until 2026-10-27 this keyword was fov.
  • max_radius_px: optional cap by the sensor's coverage — the farthest LIT image point's distance from the principal point in pixels. For a full-field lens that is the frame corner (hypot(cx, cy) for a centered pp); for a CIRCULAR fisheye it is the image-circle radius — for a division-family source, f·monotone_radius(lens) IS the 180° edge, and the model past it is unconstrained extrapolation no rigid target should be asked to follow. Folded into the SOURCE domain, so it does not count as clipping.
  • n: quadrature order (n+1 nodes).
  • weight: the density w(θ) — :image (pixel-area, r·r′; the right default when the target replaces the source on the same camera), :solid_angle (sin θ), or :uniform.
  • fix_focal: freeze s = 1.
  • max_iter: the LM's cap on ACCEPTED iterations (NLLSsolver's count; one accepted iteration may hide ~8–10 rejected candidate steps).

Returns a FitResult{CameraModel, LensFitMetadata}: estimate is the fitted camera (rescaled K + the fitted lens), retcode the LM's (VGC lm_retcode: :converged, :max_iterations, :max_time, :stalled — stopped on its step size at a non-stationary point — or :solve_failed, the cost or the step non-finite outside a step-size stop), rms the weighted profile RMS in px, and the metadata carries s, the angular domains, max_px, the fit direction and weight; clipped is true when the target family or max_view_angle cut the source's own domain (warned).

Targets: any lens TYPE with its arities — KannalaBrandt{4}, Division{2} (Division alone fits Division{1}), Scaramuzza{3}, LiftLens{Radial{Even, P, Q}}, Lens{Perspective, Radial{Even, 3, 0}} (OpenCV's radial polynomial), a double sphere Lens{DoubleSphere}, Pinhole and the pure projections (scale-only), or the type of a lens with decentering terms. A forward target moves its projection's coefficients and its radial factor's numerator — its denominator and decentering terms stay zero; a lift moves every coefficient. Sources: any lens with a projected_radius profile — a lift evaluates past 90° through its ray lift, and a lens with decentering terms contributes its radial part (warned when non-negligible).

VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation.LensFitMetadata — Type
LensFitMetadata

FitResult metadata of a lens-family regression: the joint focal rescale s, the fitted/covered angular domains (θ_max, θ_source, clipped), the worst node error max_px, and the fit direction (:forward — the family's own profile; :inverse — the family fitted to the SOURCE's inverse profile, giving an analytic complement with the same parameterization) and weight measure.
