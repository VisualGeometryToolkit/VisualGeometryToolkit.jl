Gauge Invariance of the Predictive Constraint Covariance

For homogeneous models.

We show that the predictive constraint covariance $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ (3), and hence the predictive marginal score (RANSACScoringDoneRight's StudentTUniformScoring{Predictive}, a predictive scorer of VisualGeometryToolkitCore's scoring contract), is gauge-invariant for homogeneous geometric models such as the projective homography $\mathbf{H}$. The proof relies on the degree-1 homogeneity of the algebraic constraints $\bg_i$ in the model parameters $\theta$, which causes the gauge direction to lie in the null space of the whitened model Jacobian. The singular 9×9 estimation covariance shape $\btSigma_\theta$ may therefore be used directly — no explicit gauge fixing, pseudoinverse, or reduced parametrization is required.

Notation: $\partial_{\mathbf{x}} \bg_i$ and $\partial_\theta \bg_i$ are the Jacobians of $\bg_i$ with respect to the measurements and the model.

1. Setup and Notation

Consider $N$ observations $\{\bx_i\}_{i=1}^N$ and a geometric model $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{n_\theta+1}$ defined only up to scale (e.g. $\theta = \vect(\mathbf{H})$ for a $3\times3$ homography, with $n_\theta = 8$ free parameters and one gauge freedom). Each observation imposes $d_g$ algebraic constraints

\[\bg(\bx_i, \theta) = \mathbf{0}, \tag{1}\]

and we write $\bg_i \coloneqq \bg(\bx_i, \hat\theta)$ for the constraint evaluated at the estimated model.

For the projective homography ($d_g = 2$, $n_\theta = 8$, $\dim\theta = 9$), the two constraints encoding $\tilde{\bx}' \propto \mathbf{H}\,\tilde{\bx}$ are

\[g_1 = \mathbf{h}_1^\top\tilde{\bx} - x'\,\mathbf{h}_3^\top\tilde{\bx}, \qquad g_2 = \mathbf{h}_2^\top\tilde{\bx} - y'\,\mathbf{h}_3^\top\tilde{\bx}, \tag{2}\]

where $\mathbf{h}_k^\top$ is the $k$-th row of $\mathbf{H}$, $\tilde{\bx} = (x,y,1)^\top$ is the source point in homogeneous coordinates, and $(x', y')$ is the target.

Covariance decomposition. The observation noise covariance is $\bSigma_{\bx_i} = \sigma^2\,\btSigma_{\bx_i}$ (known shape, unknown global scale), and the model estimation covariance is $\bSigma_\theta = \sigma^2\,\btSigma_\theta$. A first-order expansion of $\bg_i$ about the true values gives the constraint covariance

\[\bSigma_{\bg_i} \;=\; \partial_{\bx}\bg_i\,\bSigma_{\bx_i}\, \bigl(\partial_{\bx}\bg_i\bigr)^\top \;+\; \partial_\theta\bg_i\,\bSigma_\theta\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top, \tag{3}\]

with covariance shape $\btSigma_{\bg_i} = \bSigma_{\bg_i}/\sigma^2$. The shape-whitened residual $\br_i = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\bg_i$ (where $\mathbf{L}_i\mathbf{L}_i^\top = \btSigma_{\bg_i}$) satisfies $\br_i \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2\mathbf{I})$ under $H_0$. When $\btSigma_{\bx_i} = \mathbf{I}$ (isotropic noise) and $\btSigma_\theta = \mathbf{0}$ (model certain), $\|\br_i\|^2$ reduces to the squared Sampson distance.

2. Homogeneity of the Algebraic Constraints

The key structural property is that the constraints (2) are homogeneous of degree 1 in $\theta$:

\[\bg(\bx_i, \lambda\theta) = \lambda\,\bg(\bx_i, \theta) \qquad \forall\, \lambda \neq 0. \tag{4}\]

This holds because $\bg_i$ is linear in the entries of $\mathbf{H}$. By Euler's theorem for homogeneous functions,

\[\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\theta = \bg_i. \tag{5}\]

At the true model $\theta^*$, the constraints vanish ($\bg_i = \mathbf{0}$ for noiseless observations), so

\[\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\theta^* = \mathbf{0}. \tag{6}\]

That is, the true parameter vector ``\theta^$lies in the null space of the model Jacobian*$\partial\theta\bgi``.

Remark 1 (Generality)

Property (4) holds for any model whose algebraic constraints are linear in $\theta$: the fundamental matrix ($\mathbf{x}'^\top\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{x} = 0$), the essential matrix, the absolute conic, and all DLT-type problems. The results of this note apply to all such models.

3. Gauge Invariance of the Prediction Covariance

We now show that the constraint covariance shape $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ (3) is invariant under reparametrization $\theta \to \lambda\theta$.

3.1 Scaling of Individual Quantities

Under $\theta \to \lambda\theta$ (with measurements $\bx$ fixed):

  1. Constraints: $\bg_i \to \lambda\,\bg_i$ (degree-1 homogeneity).

  2. Measurement Jacobian: $\partial_{\bx}\bg_i \to \lambda\,\partial_{\bx}\bg_i$, since $\bg_i$ is linear in $\theta$ and the mixed partial $\partial_{\bx}\bg_i$ inherits the factor $\lambda$.

  3. Model Jacobian: $\partial_\theta\bg_i$ is independent of $\theta$, since $\bg_i$ is linear in $\theta$ (the derivative of a linear function is constant).

  4. Projected measurement covariance (Sampson matrix):

    \[\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} \coloneqq \operatorname{propagate}\!\bigl( \partial_{\bx}\bg_i,\;\btSigma_{\bx_i}\bigr) \;\to\; \lambda^2\, \btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)}. \tag{7}\]

  5. Cholesky factor: $\mathbf{L}_i \to \lambda\,\mathbf{L}_i$ (from $\mathbf{L}_i\mathbf{L}_i^\top = \btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} \to \lambda^2\,\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)}$).

  6. Solver Jacobian: The solver maps measurements to the normalized parameter: $\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta \to \lambda\, \partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta$, since the unnormalized solution scales linearly with $\lambda$. Hence the estimation covariance shape:

    \[\btSigma_\theta = \operatorname{propagate}\!\bigl( \partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta,\;\btSigma_{\bx_\cS}\bigr) \;\to\; \lambda^2\,\btSigma_\theta. \tag{8}\]

3.2 Invariance of the Whitened Residual

The whitened residual transforms as

\[\br_i = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\,\bg_i \;\to\; (\lambda\mathbf{L}_i)^{-1}(\lambda\bg_i) = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\bg_i = \br_i. \tag{9}\]

The $\lambda$-factors cancel: the whitened residual is gauge-invariant.

3.3 Invariance of the Constraint Covariance Shape

The model-uncertainty term in (3) transforms as

\[\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\btSigma_\theta\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top \;\to\; \partial_\theta\bg_i\,(\lambda^2\btSigma_\theta)\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top = \lambda^2\, \partial_\theta\bg_i\,\btSigma_\theta\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top. \tag{10}\]

(Recall $\partial_\theta\bg_i$ is independent of $\theta$.) Together with the measurement term (7):

\[\bSigma_{\bg_i} \;=\; \sigma^2\Bigl( \underbrace{\partial_{\bx}\bg_i\,\btSigma_{\bx_i}\, (\partial_{\bx}\bg_i)^\top}_{\to\;\lambda^2(\cdots)} \;+\; \underbrace{\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\btSigma_\theta\, (\partial_\theta\bg_i)^\top}_{\to\;\lambda^2(\cdots)} \Bigr) \;\to\; \lambda^2\,\bSigma_{\bg_i}. \tag{11}\]

Both terms scale identically as $\lambda^2$, so the shape (i.e. $\bSigma_{\bg_i}$ up to an overall scalar) is invariant.

Proposition 1 (Gauge invariance of the prediction covariance)

For any model whose algebraic constraints are homogeneous of degree 1 in $\theta$, the constraint covariance shape $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ and the shape-whitened residual $\br_i$ are invariant under the gauge transformation $\theta \to \lambda\theta$ for all $\lambda \neq 0$. Consequently, the marginal score $S(\theta,\cI)$ and the predictive Mahalanobis distance $q_i = \br_i^\top\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{-1}\br_i$ are gauge-invariant.

Proof. From (9), $\br_i$ is invariant. From (11), $\btSigma_{\bg_i} \to \lambda^2\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ and the Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}_i \to \lambda\mathbf{L}_i$. But the whitened residual $\br_i = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\bg_i$ is gauge-invariant (9), and so is $\|\br_i\|^2$, hence the RSS and the score $S$.

For the predictive score: $q_i = \br_i^\top\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{-1}\br_i \to \br_i^\top (\lambda^2\btSigma_{\bg_i})^{-1}\br_i = \lambda^{-2}\,\br_i^\top\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{-1}\br_i$. But recall that the whitening is by the covariance shape $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$, not the full covariance $\sigma^2\btSigma_{\bg_i}$. Both the shape and the residual inherit the same $\lambda^2$ scaling from the overall covariance, which factors out identically. Explicitly:

\[q_i = \bg_i^\top \underbrace{\bigl(\sigma^2\btSigma_{\bg_i}\bigr)^{-1}}_{= \bSigma_{\bg_i}^{-1}}\, \bg_i \;\to\; (\lambda\bg_i)^\top (\lambda^2\bSigma_{\bg_i})^{-1} (\lambda\bg_i) = q_i. \qquad \square\]

4. The Null-Space Argument

Proposition 1 shows invariance via the scaling cancellation. A complementary perspective explains why the singular estimation covariance causes no difficulty.

Proposition 2 (Null-space annihilation)

At the true model $\theta^*$, the gauge direction $\theta^*$ lies in the null space of the whitened model Jacobian $\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i$. Consequently, for any symmetric matrix $\mathbf{A} \in \mathbb{R}^{(n_\theta+1)\times(n_\theta+1)}$, the component of $\mathbf{A}$ along $\theta^*$ does not contribute to the projected form $\bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\,\mathbf{A}\,\bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top$.

Proof. By Euler's theorem (5), $\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\theta^* = \bg_i\big|_{\theta=\theta^*} = \mathbf{0}$. Hence $\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\theta^* = \mathbf{0}$. Now decompose $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}_\perp + \alpha\,\theta^*\theta^{*\top}$ where $\mathbf{A}_\perp$ has no component along $\theta^*$. Then

\[\bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\,\mathbf{A}\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top\mathbf{L}_i^{-\top} = \bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\, \mathbf{A}_\perp\, \bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top\mathbf{L}_i^{-\top},\]

since $\bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\,\theta^*\theta^{*\top}\,\bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top\mathbf{L}_i^{-\top} = \mathbf{0}\cdot\mathbf{0}^\top = \mathbf{0}$. $\square$

Corollary 1

The estimation covariance shape $\btSigma_\theta = \partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta\,\bigl(\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta\bigr)^\top$ is a singular $(n_\theta{+}1) \times (n_\theta{+}1)$ matrix (with $\theta^*$ as a null eigenvector under Frobenius normalization). This singularity is harmless: substituting $\mathbf{A} = \btSigma_\theta$ in Proposition 2, the gauge direction contributes nothing to $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$. Adding arbitrary variance $\alpha > 0$ along the gauge direction — i.e. replacing $\btSigma_\theta$ by $\btSigma_\theta + \alpha\,\theta^*\theta^{*\top}$ — leaves the prediction covariance $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ unchanged.

This means:

  1. The singular $\btSigma_\theta$ may be used directly in the quadratic form (3) without pseudoinverse or reduced parametrization.
  2. No explicit gauge fixing is needed beyond whatever normalization the solver uses (e.g. Frobenius normalization $\|\theta\| = 1$ with sign convention).
  3. Whether the gauge direction carries zero, finite, or infinite variance in $\btSigma_\theta$, the result $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ is identical.

5. Implementation: Two Equivalent Representations of $\btSigma_\theta$

5.1 Gram Inverse (WLS / Local Optimization)

When $\theta$ is estimated by weighted least squares from an observation set $\cS$ with block-diagonal weight matrix $\bW = \operatorname{blockdiag}(\btSigma_{\bg_1}^{-1}, \ldots, \btSigma_{\bg_{|\cS|}}^{-1})$, the estimation covariance shape is the Gram inverse:

\[\btSigma_\theta = \Bigl(\bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_\cS\bigr)^\top \bW\, \partial_\theta\bg_\cS\Bigr)^{-1}. \tag{12}\]

This is the form used during local optimization, where $\partial_\theta\bg_\cS$ is the stacked design matrix and the SVD of $\widetilde\bW^{1/2}\partial_\theta\bg = \mathbf{U}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{V}^\top$ yields $\btSigma_\theta = \mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-2}\mathbf{V}^\top$.

For homogeneous models, the Gram matrix $\bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_\cS\bigr)^\top\bW\,\partial_\theta\bg_\cS$ is rank-deficient (rank $n_\theta$, with null space along $\theta$), so the "inverse" (12) is understood as a pseudoinverse or, equivalently, the inverse restricted to the $n_\theta$-dimensional subspace orthogonal to $\theta$.

5.2 Solver Jacobian (Minimal Sample)

When $\theta$ is determined by a minimal solver from $m$ points with $d_g m = n_\theta$ measurement coordinates, the solver defines a differentiable map $\bx_\cS \mapsto \theta(\bx_\cS)$. Under isotropic noise ($\btSigma_{\bx_i} = \mathbf{I}$), the estimation covariance shape is

\[\btSigma_\theta = \partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta\, \bigl(\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta\bigr)^\top, \qquad \partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{(n_\theta+1) \times 2md}. \tag{13}\]

For the 4-point homography solver, $\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 16}$ and $\btSigma_\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 9}$ has rank 8.

Equivalence. Both representations yield the same $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ (3). To see this, note that the implicit-function theorem applied to the $n_\theta$ constraints $\bg_\cS(\bx_\cS, \theta) = \mathbf{0}$ gives

\[\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta = -\Bigl(\partial_\theta\bg_\cS\Bigr)^{\dagger} \partial_{\bx}\bg_\cS, \tag{14}\]

where $(\cdot)^\dagger$ denotes the pseudoinverse (needed because the system has one more column than rank in $\theta$). Substituting into (13) and using $\btSigma_{\bx_i} = \mathbf{I}$ recovers the Gram inverse (12) with $\bW = \mathbf{I}$.

5.3 Why Sign Normalization Is Needed in the Solver

Although the prediction covariance is gauge-invariant (Section 3), the solver itself must impose a consistent normalization to ensure that $\theta(\bx_\cS)$ is a continuous, differentiable function of the measurements. Without sign normalization, the solver output is discontinuous when $\theta_{n_\theta+1}$ (e.g. $H_{3,3}$) crosses zero: an infinitesimal perturbation flips the sign of the entire output, making the solver Jacobian $\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta$ undefined.

Frobenius normalization ($\|\theta\| = 1$) combined with a sign convention (e.g. $H_{3,3} \geq 0$) ensures continuity almost everywhere. The sign normalization is a solver-level convenience, not a mathematical necessity: any smooth section of the projective equivalence class suffices, and the downstream covariance $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ does not depend on the choice (Proposition 1).

6. Whitened Prediction Covariance: Origin of the $\mathbf{I}$ Term

The prediction covariance (3) is $d_g \times d_g$ and in general dense. The scoring code works entirely in whitened coordinates, where the measurement contribution collapses to a scalar multiple of the identity. This section derives the whitened form and maps each step to the implementation.

6.1 Derivation

Define the Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}_i$ by the measurement-only covariance shape (the Sampson matrix):

\[\mathbf{L}_i\mathbf{L}_i^\top = \btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} \coloneqq \partial_{\bx}\bg_i\,\btSigma_{\bx_i}\, \bigl(\partial_{\bx}\bg_i\bigr)^\top. \tag{15}\]

For isotropic noise $\btSigma_{\bx_i} = \mathbf{I}$, this is $\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} = \partial_{\bx}\bg_i\,\bigl(\partial_{\bx}\bg_i\bigr)^\top$.

Now whiten both terms of the full covariance shape (3) by pre- and post-multiplying $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ by $\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}$ and $\mathbf{L}_i^{-\top}$:

\[\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\,\btSigma_{\bg_i}\,\mathbf{L}_i^{-\top} = \underbrace{ \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\, \btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)}\, \mathbf{L}_i^{-\top} }_{ = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}(\mathbf{L}_i\mathbf{L}_i^\top) \mathbf{L}_i^{-\top} = \mathbf{I}} \;+\; \underbrace{ \bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\, \btSigma_\theta\, \bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top }_{\text{model-uncertainty in whitened coords}}. \tag{16}\]

With the full covariance (including $\sigma^2$), the $d_g \times d_g$ prediction covariance in whitened coordinates is therefore

\[\boxed{ \widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i = \sigma^2 \mathbf{I} \;+\; \bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)\, \bSigma_\theta\, \bigl(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i\bigr)^\top. } \tag{17}\]

The $\mathbf{I}$ appears because $\mathbf{L}_i$ is defined as the Cholesky factor of precisely the measurement-noise term; whitening maps that term to the identity by construction.

Remark 2 (RANSAC convention)

In the scoring code, $s^2 = 1$ is supplied in place of $\sigma^2$ and the unscaled shape $\btSigma_\theta$ (instead of $\bSigma_\theta = \sigma^2\btSigma_\theta$) is used, giving $\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i = \mathbf{I} + (\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i)\,\btSigma_\theta\,(\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i)^\top$. This differs from the properly-scaled form (17) by an overall factor of $\sigma^2$, which cancels in the Mahalanobis distance $q_i = \br_i^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i^{-1}\br_i$ since the whitened residual $\br_i$ also absorbs a factor of $\sigma$ (i.e. $\br_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$ so that $\br_i^\top(\sigma^2 \widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i)^{-1}\br_i = \br_i^\top \widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i^{-1}\br_i$ up to the overall $\sigma^2$).

6.2 The propagate Primitive

Every covariance computation in the pipeline is an instance of the same bilinear map:

\[\operatorname{propagate}(A,\, \bSigma) \;\coloneqq\; A\,\bSigma\,A^\top, \tag{18}\]

where $A$ may be rectangular. This unifies:

  • Measurement → constraint: $\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} = \operatorname{propagate}\!\bigl(\partial_{\bx}\bg_i,\; \btSigma_{\bx_i}\bigr)$
  • Measurement → parameter: $\btSigma_\theta = \operatorname{propagate}\!\bigl(\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta,\; \btSigma_{\bx_\cS}\bigr)$
  • Projective conic transfer: $C^{*\prime} = \operatorname{propagate}(\mathbf{H},\, C^*)$ ($A$ square)

The function propagate(A, Σ) dispatches on the type of $\bSigma$ for compile-time specialization:

$\bSigma$ typeComputation
I (UniformScaling{Bool})$A\,A^\top$
$\lambda\mathbf{I}$ (UniformScaling)$\lambda\,(A\,A^\top)$
Diagonal$A\,D\,A^\top$ (column scaling)
General matrix$A\,\bSigma\,A^\top$

When $d_g = 1$, the Cholesky reduces to a square root and the whitening to scalar division; the compiler eliminates this automatically since $d_g$ is a compile-time constant.

The interface is built around one problem method, constraint_with_jacobians(problem, model, i), which returns the algebraic constraint $\bg_i$ and its Jacobians $\partial_{\bx}\bg_i$, $\partial_\theta\bg_i$ at data point $i$. Every downstream quantity is derived from it.

Residuals path (model-certain scoring). For each observation $i$, residuals! writes whitened_residual(problem, ctx, i), which by default whitens $(\bg_i, \partial_{\bx}\bg_i)$ through whiten: the norm of $\br_i = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\bg_i$, whose square is the Mahalanobis distance $\bg_i^\top\bigl(\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)}\bigr)^{-1}\bg_i$. A problem may override it with a cheaper route to the same quantity.

Predictive scoring path (model-uncertain). For a hypothesis with model uncertainty, the pipeline for observation $i$ is:

  1. constraint_with_jacobians(problem, model, i) returns $(\bg_i,\, \partial_{\bx}\bg_i,\, \partial_\theta\bg_i)$.
  2. whiten forms $\btSigma_{\bg_i}^{(\bx)} = \operatorname{propagate}(\partial_{\bx}\bg_i,\, \btSigma_{\bx_i})$, takes its Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}_i$, and returns the whitened residual $\br_i = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\bg_i$ and whitened model Jacobian $\partial_\theta\bg_{i,w} = \mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i$.
  3. The predictive score assembles $\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i = \mathbf{I} + \operatorname{propagate}(\partial_\theta\bg_{i,w},\, \btSigma_\theta)$ and evaluates $q_i = \br_i^\top\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i^{-1}\br_i$ and $\log\lvert\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i\rvert$. The $\mathbf{I}$ is correct precisely because step 2 whitened by the measurement covariance.

The model covariance $\btSigma_\theta$ is computed once per hypothesis by solver_covariance from the constraint rows of its index set: on a minimal sample the system is square and the result is the implicit-function covariance of the minimal solve (13)–(14); on an overdetermined set (a local optimization's consensus set) it is the covariance of the whitened-constraint estimator (12). It is a unit-scale shape — the physical covariance is $\bSigma_\theta = s^2\btSigma_\theta$, the convention of Remark 2. For the projective models it is computed in the model's minimal chart and carried to $\vect(\mathbf{H})$ (or $\vect$ of $\mathbf{F}$, $\mathbf{E}$) through the chart's differential, so the gauge direction carries zero variance — one of the equivalent choices of Corollary 1.

7. Monte Carlo Validation

The gauge-invariance results were validated numerically for the homography with five tests:

  1. Solver Jacobian: $\partial_{\bx_\cS}\theta$ of the 4-point solver matches forward-mode automatic differentiation through the solver to relative error $< 10^{-15}$.
  2. Constraint derivatives and $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$: The whitened model Jacobian $\mathbf{L}_i^{-1}\partial_\theta\bg_i$ matches forward-mode automatic differentiation to relative error $< 10^{-15}$, and the composed prediction covariance $\btSigma_{\bg_i}$ agrees to the same precision.
  3. Full Criminisi MC (noisy training + noisy test): 200k trials with $\sigma = 0.5$, $n_{\mathrm{train}} = 4$. Eigenvalue ratios (empirical/analytic) $\approx 0.98$–$0.99$.
  4. Noiseless test MC (noisy training, exact test): Isolates the model-uncertainty term $\partial_\theta\bg_i\,\btSigma_\theta\,(\partial_\theta\bg_i)^\top$. Eigenvalue ratios $\approx 0.98$–$0.99$.
  5. Predictive score $\chi^2$ test: Under the RANSAC convention ($s^2 = 1$, unscaled $\btSigma_\theta$), $\mathbb{E}[q_i] = \sigma^2 d_g$. Under full scaling ($s^2 = \sigma^2$), $q_i \sim \chi^2(d_g)$ with $\mathbb{E}[q_i] = d_g$, $\operatorname{Var}(q_i) = 2d_g$. Both verified to $< 5\%$ relative error.

The package's test suite keeps the predictive calibration under test for every covariance-bearing problem (homography, fundamental and essential matrices, absolute pose, hand-eye, lines): a held-out point's $\br_i^\top(\sigma^2\widetilde{\mathbf{V}}_i)^{-1}\br_i$ is gated against $\chi^2(d_g)$ in test/ransac_mc_calibration_tests.jl.
