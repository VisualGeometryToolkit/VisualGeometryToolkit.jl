Robot–World Hand–Eye Calibration

The fit_robot_world_hand_eye estimator: staged linear robot–world / multi-marker hand–eye calibration.

1. Problem

The robot–world calibration with $M$ markers sharing one camera transform:

\[A_i\, X_m \;=\; Y\, B_{im}, \qquad i = 1,\dots,N, \quad (i,m) \in \mathcal{O} , \tag{1}\]

where

  • $A_i = (R_{A_i}, \boldsymbol{t}_{A_i}) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ — rig pose in the reference frame $W$ at frame $i$ (motion capture; treated as exact, its noise being $\sim\!100\times$ below detection noise);
  • $B_{im} = (R_{B_{im}}, \boldsymbol{t}_{B_{im}}) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ — pose of marker $m$ in the camera at frame $i$ (PnP detection; the measurements);
  • $X_m = (R_{X_m}, \boldsymbol{t}_{X_m}) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ — marker $m$ in the rig ($M$ unknowns);
  • $Y = (R_Y, \boldsymbol{t}_Y) \in \mathrm{SE}(3)$ — camera in the reference frame (one shared unknown).

Unlike the classical ratio form $A_{ij}X = XB_{ij}$ (which eliminates $Y$ and therefore lives in correlated relative-motion space), $Y$ is kept as a parameter and all $M{+}1$ transforms are estimated jointly from the per-observation poses. With a pose prior (mocap), no RANSAC layer is used.

2. Estimator: the Staged Linear Solve

All data, no sampling:

  1. $R_Y$ by Kabsch. Left-relative motions $M^{A}_{ij} = A_i A_j^{-1}$, $M^{B}_{ij} = B_{im} B_{jm}^{-1}$ conjugate by the shared $Y$ only, so their angle-scaled axes satisfy $\omega^{B} = R_Y^{\top}\, \omega^{A}$. Pooled over all markers and frame pairs with $\lVert\omega^A\rVert$ above a small-angle gate: $R_Y^\top = \arg\min_R \sum \lVert \omega^{B} - R\,\omega^{A} \rVert^2$ (one SVD).
  2. $R_{X_m}$ by chordal mean. From $R_{X_m} = R_{A_i}^{\top} R_Y R_{B_{im}}$ per observation, the chordal $L_2$ mean (SVD projection of the sum onto $\mathrm{SO}(3)$) per marker.
  3. All translations jointly. The translation part of (1) is linear in $(\boldsymbol{t}_Y, \boldsymbol{t}_{X_1}, \dots, \boldsymbol{t}_{X_M})$: $\boldsymbol{t}_{B} = R_Y^{\top} R_{A} \boldsymbol{t}_{X_m} - R_Y^{\top} \boldsymbol{t}_Y + R_Y^{\top} \boldsymbol{t}_{A}$; one least-squares solve in $3(M{+}1)$ unknowns — exactly optimal in the translations given the rotations.

Each stage optimizes a different functional, so the combined estimate is not the stationary point of any single scalar objective; empirically (Section 4) this is a feature, not a defect, under the actual error structure of the data.

3. Diagnostic Residual, Covariance, Observability

For reporting and uncertainty, the per-observation decoupled $6$-vector residual in the tangent at the prediction $\hat B_{im} = Y^{-1} A_i X_m$ is evaluated at the estimate:

\[r_{im} \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} \operatorname{Log}\!\bigl(\hat{R}_{im}^{\top} R_{B_{im}}\bigr)/\sigma_R \\[1.2ex] \bigl(\boldsymbol{t}_{B_{im}} - \hat{\boldsymbol{t}}_{im}\bigr)/\sigma_t \end{bmatrix} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{6}, \tag{2}\]

with $\operatorname{Log} : \mathrm{SO}(3) \to \mathbb{R}^3$ the rotation-vector logarithm. When not supplied, $\sigma_R, \sigma_t$ are profiled as the $\operatorname{RMS}$ of the respective blocks of the unscaled residuals at the estimate.

Because the rotation stages do not minimize (2), the estimate is not a stationary point of the residual sum of squares (only the translation block is), and the residual's Gauss–Newton covariance $\hat s^{2}\,(J^{\top} J)^{-1}$ would understate the scatter. The reported covariance is instead that of the staged estimate itself, first order in the observation noise $\hat s^{2}\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_R^2 I_3,\, \sigma_t^2 I_3)$ of each pose observation, with

\[\hat s^{2} \;=\; \frac{\sum \lVert r_{im}\rVert^2}{6n - 6(M{+}1)} . \tag{3}\]

The implicit function theorem on the stages' own stationarity conditions (the Kabsch rotation, the chordal means, the translation normal equations) carries that noise through to $(Y, X_1, \dots, X_M)$. The covariance lives in the tangent coordinates (rad, translation units) of one rigid-motion chart per block, in the order $[\,Y \,|\, X_1 \cdots X_M\,]$. The marginal covariance of $Y$ is the corresponding $6{\times}6$ sub-block of the joint covariance, which accounts for the $X_m$ coupling.

With $J = \partial r / \partial \delta$ the Jacobian of (2) over the same chart, the smallest eigenpair of $J^\top J$ is an observability diagnostic: a collapsed eigenvalue names the unconstrained direction (e.g. the yaw $\oplus$ $\boldsymbol{t}$ family of single-distance calibration data) instead of hiding it. When $J^\top J$ is numerically rank-deficient the covariance is reported unbounded ($\infty$).

4. Why There Is No Pose-Residual NLLS Refinement

A joint NLLS refinement of (2) (Levenberg–Marquardt over the stacked MRP $\oplus$ translation increment; the MLE under an isotropic, homoscedastic pose-noise model) was implemented, validated on synthetic data (noiseless exactness, Monte-Carlo $\chi^2$ covariance calibration, degeneracy regression), and then removed after evaluation on the motivating dataset:

  • In-sample it behaved exactly as theory predicts: cost decreased by $\Delta C \approx 283$ from the staged linear solution, consistent with the quadratic model for the observed $\approx 0.17^\circ$ parameter shift ($\approx 17\sigma$ statistical).
  • Out-of-sample it extrapolated worse: common-mode translation bands across the evaluation range grew from $2.8/4.2/6.7$ mm (staged linear) to $3.3/6.3/10.4$ mm.

This is the signature of model misspecification: the pose-noise model (2) is isotropic and homoscedastic, while the real PnP errors are neither (depth-vs-lateral anisotropy; a $4$-blob sliver counts like a $1000$-blob board). The MLE of a misspecified model absorbs the unmodeled systematic along weakly constrained parameter directions — in-sample optimality, out-of-sample bias. The staged linear solve, which never optimizes the misspecified likelihood, is the calibration of record.

The sound upgrade is not a better pose-noise model but a measurement-level objective: minimize the reprojection error of the extracted image features (blob centroids, tag corners) over $(Y, X_1,\dots,X_M)$, initialized at the staged linear solution. Per-feature pixel residuals make heteroscedasticity automatic (each board contributes as many residuals as it has features) and remove PnP as an intermediate estimator entirely. This is the objective refine_robot_world_hand_eye minimizes.
