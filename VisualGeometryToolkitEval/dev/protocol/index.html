The experimental protocol

This page is the one statement of the evaluation protocol of the stack. A paper's drivers and the package's own sources point here, and their headers state only what is specific to them: their command line, schemas and stages.

The mechanisms live in this package's modules: Tuning (the cross-validation specification), Stats (the statistics), Evaluation (the composition evaluator and the single cross-validation path), Experiments (run records), Render (figures), and Pools and Datasets (data). Every parameter of record is in configs/default.toml, configs/tuning.toml or configs/splits.toml.

Abbreviations used on this page:

AbbreviationMeaning
CVcross-validation
LOlocal optimization, the refinement step after RANSAC selects a model
GTground truth
BCabias-corrected and accelerated (bootstrap interval)

0. Cross-validation: tuning against reporting

Every tuning names its data in its [tuning.<name>] spec, as a cross_validation value (CrossValidation; the schema is in The [tuning] schema). The spec has three parts.

  • Scenes. A RegisteredSceneSplit is a split that the spec names and that is listed once, in configs/splits.toml, the one file of record of every scene list of the stack. Validation scenes are the tuning side, where every hyperparameter is validated. Test scenes are the reporting side. The two lists must be disjoint. The parser writes the resolved lists into the complete spec, so they enter the spec hash, the run key and every tuning artifact.
  • Units. Each side has a UnitSampling over the data's sampling unit. A dataset declares its unit with sampling_unit: an image pair for a two-view dataset, a query for an absolute-pose one. The samplings are ExhaustiveSampling, StratifiedSampling (a fraction of each scene, then at most cap_per_scene, which preserves the mix of scenes) and UniformSampling (a fraction of the side's pooled units, then at most cap). Every sampler permutes, keeps the fraction, applies the cap, and only then sorts back into data order, so a cap never keeps a prefix of the data.
  • Seeds. Every draw is seeded by stable_seed, the SHA-256 hash of the spec's seed and of the name it applies to, such as a scene name or a budget and draw index. Base.hash is never used, because it is not stable across Julia versions. seed + index is never used either: with a named seed, adding, removing or reordering scenes leaves every other scene's sample unchanged.

A driver accepts the samplings it supports through the signature of the method it calls, for example cv::CrossValidation{<:SceneSplit, StratifiedSampling}. An unsupported spec is then a MethodError and is never silently reinterpreted. No driver, paper config or package source states a scene list, a split, a pair prefix or a sampling seed in code. Run directories come from the run records (run_key, experiment_dir, results_root).

1. The fairness axis: the scale multiple

Methods are compared at the same operating point, not at arbitrary native values. The shared axis is the scale multiple: the number of inlier-noise standard deviations σ at which a method's inlier/outlier decision flips. It is anchored at the reference scale ŝ = √median σ̂² of the validation data (reference_scale). The maps from a scale multiple to a native width are VisualGeometryToolkitCore's (to_native, native_map, decision_width), one per width axis:

axisnative width at scale multiple k
LossWidth (threshold family)loss_width = k·ŝ (the truncated loss: identity)
OutlierHalfwidth (Student-t marginal)outlier_halfwidth = ŝ·√(π/2)·e^{k²/2} at codimension d_g = 1
a lifted lossthe half-weight width of its shape

One refit path, one scale port. Every refiner runs through the one refit layer (refit_imc! and VisualGeometryToolkitCore's RefitFamily, with the values NativeRefit and ExternalRefit). The deployed native width reaches a refiner only as its width on the axis it consumes (consumed_axis, consumed_native). A loss-width refiner that rides on an outlier-half-width selector grid converts at the refit protocol's fixed OPERATING_SCALE_MULTIPLE (configs/default.toml, table [refit]). No refiner estimates its own scale from a plug-in window.

The outlier-half-width axis carries a codimension. The marginal map takes the residual codimension d_g = codimension(problem_type): 1 on the E and F tracks and 2 on H. At ŝ = 1 over the window [0.8, 6.0], the upper edge moves from 8.2293e7 (d_g = 1) to 1.0156e4 (d_g = 2), a change of 3.91 decades. A native grid and the window searched inside it must therefore be cut at the same d_g. The coverage window rule cuts the grid through the same map that the search window uses, and this is what makes its coverage guarantee hold. d_g has no default anywhere, and every tuning artifact persists it as "d_g".

2. Tuning: the single CV path

Every hyperparameter is cross-validated by one discipline, the coarse-to-fine search (coarse_fine_search, with a [tuning.<name>] spec of scheme joint_coarse_fine, or coarse_fine for one scalar). It runs in three steps:

  1. It evaluates a coarse product grid. Whenever the joint argmin lies within edge_margin points of a face, the grid extends that axis.
  2. It evaluates a fine product grid over the bracket of the coarse argmin on every axis.
  3. It takes the free argmin of the validation objective on the fine grid.

Windows are located by expansion; they are never pinned or widened by hand. Several searches share one sweep of a staged validation pool (the search keys of one call, or coarse_fine_cv_batched for scalars), and an expensive axis is evaluated one value at a time. The artifact (save_coarse_fine_artifact) stores the per-unit sufficient statistics of each cell, from which the bootstrap of the selection is exact; deployed_setting reads the selection. A paper tunes a scorer and LO arm as a sequence of such scalar CVs: first the selection width, then the refinement width.

The tuned point is persisted as a tuning artifact (save_tuning_artifact): the spec, its hash, the grid specs, the sampling unit and the code identity. Only the deployed readers read it (deployed_arm_settings, deployed_budget_settings, deployed_basin_settings). They refuse a tuned point on a boundary of its window (the artifact's boundary_hits, or enforce_probe_interior for a probe artifact), because a window that clips the argmin has not located it.

3. Miscalibration: the fixed-scale-multiple protocol

Each method is tuned once at the true validation scale. Then the deployed plug-in scale is corrupted, σ̂ → ζσ̂, while the tuned scale multiple is held fixed (corrupt_native). Every native parameter is homogeneous of degree 1 in σ̂, so the corruption multiplies each method's tuned parameter by the same factor ζ. Each method thus suffers the same relative error in its own units. A tuned scale multiple is anchored on the data's own partition and is not corrupted. Test data is untouched. Evaluation reads the grid column nearest to ζ·native* (nearest_column). This lookup is for evaluation only; a tuning never takes a nearest column.

4. The validation budget: the tuning-budget sweep

Draws come from the band-eligible validation units: those with a finite σ̂² inside the band [ŝ_min², ŝ_max²] of scale_bounds. The draws are the spec's BudgetSampling (budget_units). Draw r of budget n takes n units without replacement from rng(stable_seed(seed, "budget", n, r)) and sorts them.

For each draw, the protocol computes the reference scale ŝ(n) of the drawn units, takes the free argmin inside the window at ŝ(n), and evaluates the chosen point on the test data (WindowedGridMethod, compute_method_rows). The reported point is the mean over draws of the per-draw test medians. The interval is a BCa interval over draws, resampled from resampling_rng(budget, n), that is rng(stable_seed(seed, "resampling", n)), a stream of its own and never a draw's.

5. The sample budget

evaluate_sample_budget walks each pair's shared hypothesis stream with running-best selectors. Selection checkpoints come every batch_size samples, and LO checkpoints on the lo_every grid (SampleBudgetConfig). Every arm's selection and refinement widths come from its tuning artifact through deployed_budget_settings. There is no fallback and no self-tuning. Every LO call runs within the evaluation's equal budget of model updates (lo_iteration_budget, configs/default.toml, table [local_optimization]).

6. The metric: pose error, with the F upgrade

All pose errors use the coherent metric max(ΔR, Δt) in degrees over the single best decomposition (VisualGeometryToolkitCore's imc_pose_error, named after the Image Matching Challenge, IMC). Pool models are pool-frame F containers on both tracks. A pool model is therefore always scored through the intrinsics upgrade model_imc(F, geom): K₂ᵀFK₁ → E → the pose that resolves cheirality. It is never scored with the bare imc_pose_error(θ, R, t), which treats its argument as a metric essential matrix. That misuse once inflated the oracle floor of the ETH3D E track from 0.19° to 0.73°.

7. The oracle floor: the selection lower bound

For each miscalibration cell, the floor is floor = median_i min_{θ ∈ pool_i} imc(θ) over the test pairs (the budget engine's OracleSelector). Oracle selection consumes no plug-in scale, so the floor does not depend on ζ. It bounds selection only: a refit may go below it by leaving the discrete hypothesis pool. A miscalibration panel carries its floor (the oracle keyword of emit_miscal_faceted).

8. Statistics

Point estimates and intervals go through Stats only:

  • finite_median. Argmin objectives use finite_median(...; empty = Inf), so that a column of only NaN never wins.
  • bootstrap_ci, the BCa interval.
  • scene_mean_median_rows!, the mean of per-scene medians, with a BCa interval over scenes resampled from stable_seed(seed, "scene_mean_median", j).

The mAA follows one rule, eligible_maa. A pair with ground truth and enough valid correspondences to run is eligible, and a run on it that yields no model scores 0 in the denominator. A pair that cannot be processed is skipped. pair_outcomes reports the counts beside every mAA. The mAA is the continuous AUC of the accuracy curve up to maa_threshold_deg (configs/default.toml, table [metrics]; VisualGeometryToolkitCore's mean_average_accuracy).

9. Figure conventions

The Render emitters enforce a house style, so that figures are uniform across papers:

  • Confidence intervals are shaded bands, never error bars: scene-clustered BCa 95% intervals (section 8).
  • Labels and markers come from the framework, never from hand-written tables: VisualGeometryToolkitCore's lo_label(lo) and lo_marker(lo). A partition-anchored scale is marked -\hat s, a fixed cross-validated scale is unmarked, and a self-scaling LO carries no suffix. A renderer never spells out a method's name.
  • One arm has one colour everywhere; the marker shape is the solver family.
  • The oracle (the GT-best model) is a solid black line with no markers.
  • Basin panels (emit_basin_faceted): the left panel is the success rate P(e ≤ succ°) on [0, 1]. The right panel is the conditional mean E[e | e ≤ succ°] on a linear axis with degree ticks, masked where the success rate is below min_success_for_cond_mean (configs/default.toml, table [render]). The reference arm without LO is the perturbed seed.
  • Median-error panels keep a log axis, since the median error is unbounded there.
