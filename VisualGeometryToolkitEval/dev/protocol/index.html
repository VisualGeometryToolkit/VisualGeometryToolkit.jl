The experimental protocol

This page is the one statement of the evaluation protocol of the stack. A paper's drivers and the package's own sources point here; their headers state only what is specific to them (their CLI, schemas and stages). The mechanisms live in this package's modules: Tuning (the cross-validation specification), Stats (the statistics), Evaluation (the composition evaluator and the single CV path), Experiments (run records), Render (figures), Pools and Datasets (data). Every parameter of record is in configs/default.toml, configs/tuning.toml or configs/splits.toml.

0. Cross-validation: tuning against reporting

Every tuning names its data in its [tuning.<name>] spec, as a cross_validation (Tuning.CrossValidation; the schema is in The [tuning] schema):

  • Scenes. A RegisteredSceneSplit: a split NAMED by the spec and listed once, in configs/splits.toml, the one file of record of every scene list of the stack. Validation scenes are the tuning side (every hyperparameter is validated there), test scenes the reporting side; the two lists are disjoint (refused otherwise). The parser writes the resolved lists into the complete spec, so they are in the spec hash, the run key and every tuning artifact.
  • Units. Per side, a UnitSampling over the data's sampling unit (a dataset declares it with sampling_unit: an image pair of a two-view dataset, a query of an absolute-pose one): ExhaustiveSampling, StratifiedSampling (a fraction of each scene, then at most cap_per_scene; the scene mix is preserved) or UniformSampling (a fraction of the side's pooled units, then at most cap). Every sampler permutes, keeps the fraction, caps, then sorts back into data order LAST, so a cap never keeps a prefix.
  • Seeds. Every draw is seeded by Stats.stable_seed (SHA-256 of the spec's seed and the NAME it applies to: a scene name, a budget and draw index), never Base.hash (not stable across Julia versions) and never seed + index (so adding, removing or reordering scenes leaves every other scene's sample unchanged).

A driver accepts the samplings it supports by the SIGNATURE of the method it calls (cv::CrossValidation{<:SceneSplit, StratifiedSampling}), so an unsupported spec is a MethodError, never a silent reinterpretation. No driver, paper config or package source states a scene list, a split, a pair prefix or a sampling seed in code. Run directories come from the run records (Experiments.run_key, experiment_dir, results_root).

1. The fairness axis: the scale multiple

Methods are compared at the SAME operating point, not at arbitrary native values. The shared axis is the scale multiple (the number of inlier-noise σ at which a method's inlier/outlier decision flips), anchored at the reference scale ŝ = √median σ̂² of the validation data (Stats.reference_scale). The scale-multiple → native maps are VisualGeometryToolkitCore's (to_native, native_map, decision_width), one per width axis:

axisnative width at scale multiple k
LossWidth (threshold family)loss_width = k·ŝ (the truncated loss: identity)
OutlierHalfwidth (Student-t marginal)outlier_halfwidth = ŝ·√(π/2)·e^{k²/2} at codimension d_g = 1
a lifted lossthe half-weight width of its shape

One refit path, one scale port. Every refiner runs through the one refit layer (Evaluation.refit_imc!, VisualGeometryToolkitCore's RefitFamily: NativeRefit or ExternalRefit). The deployed native reaches a refiner solely as its width on the axis it consumes (consumed_axis, consumed_native); a loss-width refiner riding an outlier-half-width selector grid converts at the refit protocol's fixed OPERATING_SCALE_MULTIPLE (configs/default.toml [refit]). No refiner estimates its own scale from a plug-in window.

The outlier-half-width axis carries a codimension. The marginal map takes the residual codimension d_g = codimension(problem_type): 1 on the E and F tracks, 2 on H. At ŝ = 1 over the window [0.8, 6.0] the upper edge moves from 8.2293e7 (d_g = 1) to 1.0156e4 (d_g = 2), 3.91 decades. A native grid and the window searched inside it must be cut at the SAME d_g: the coverage window rule cuts the grid through the very map the search window uses, which is what makes its coverage guarantee hold. d_g has no default anywhere, and every tuning artifact persists it as "d_g".

2. Tuning: the single CV path

Every hyperparameter is cross-validated by ONE discipline, the coarse-fine search (Evaluation.coarse_fine_search, a [tuning.<name>] spec of scheme joint_coarse_fine, or coarse_fine for one scalar): a coarse product grid that EXTENDS an axis whenever the joint argmin lies within edge_margin points of a face, then a fine product over the coarse argmin's bracket on every axis, then a FREE argmin of the validation objective. Windows are located by expansion, never hand-pinned and never hand-widened. Several searches share one sweep of a staged validation pool (the search keys of one call; coarse_fine_cv_batched for scalars), and an expensive axis is evaluated one value at a time. The artifact (save_coarse_fine_artifact) stores each cell's per-unit sufficient statistics, from which the bootstrap of the selection is exact; deployed_setting reads the selection. A paper tunes a score+LO arm as a sequence of such scalar CVs (the selection width, then the refinement width), each of them this path.

The tuned point is persisted as a tuning artifact (save_tuning_artifact: the spec, its hash, the grid specs, the sampling unit, the code identity), and read only by the deployed readers (deployed_arm_settings, deployed_budget_settings, deployed_basin_settings), which refuse a tuned point on a boundary of its window (the artifact's boundary_hits; enforce_probe_interior for a probe artifact): a window that clips the argmin has not located it.

3. Miscalibration: the fixed-scale-multiple protocol

Tune each method ONCE at the true validation scale, then corrupt the DEPLOYED plug-in scale, σ̂ → ζσ̂, holding the tuned scale multiple fixed (Tuning.corrupt_native). Every native parameter is degree-1 homogeneous in σ̂, so the corruption multiplies each method's tuned parameter by the SAME factor ζ: the identical relative error in each method's own units. A tuned scale multiple is anchored on the data's own partition and is not corrupted. Test data is untouched. Evaluation reads the grid column nearest ζ·native* (nearest_column; evaluation only, a tuning never takes a nearest column).

4. The validation budget: the tuning-budget sweep

Draws come from the band-eligible validation units (a finite σ̂² inside the [ŝ_min², ŝ_max²] band of Stats.scale_bounds). The draws are the spec's BudgetSampling (budget_units: draw r of budget n is n units without replacement from rng(stable_seed(seed, "budget", n, r)), sorted). Per draw: the reference scale ŝ(n) of the drawn units, the free argmin inside the window at ŝ(n), and test evaluation at every chosen point (WindowedGridMethod, compute_method_rows). The point is the mean over draws of the per-draw test medians; the interval is BCa over draws, resampled from resampling_rng(budget, n) (rng(stable_seed(seed, "resampling", n)), its own stream, never a draw's).

5. The sample budget

Evaluation.evaluate_sample_budget walks each pair's shared hypothesis stream with running-best selectors; selection checkpoints every batch_size samples, LO checkpoints on the lo_every grid (SampleBudgetConfig). Every arm's (selection, refinement) widths come from its tuning artifact through deployed_budget_settings; there is no fallback and no self-tuning. Every LO call runs at most the evaluation's equal model-update budget (lo_iteration_budget, configs/default.toml [local_optimization]).

6. The metric: pose error, with the F upgrade

All pose errors are the coherent metric max(ΔR, Δt) in degrees over the single best decomposition (VisualGeometryToolkitCore imc_pose_error). Pool models are pool-frame F containers on both tracks, so a pool model is ALWAYS scored through the intrinsics upgrade model_imc(F, geom) (K₂ᵀFK₁ → E → cheirality-resolved pose), never the bare imc_pose_error(θ, R, t), which treats its argument as a metric essential matrix (that misuse once inflated an ETH3D-E oracle floor from 0.19° to 0.73°).

7. The oracle floor: the selection lower bound

Per miscalibration cell, floor = median_i min_{θ ∈ pool_i} imc(θ) over the test pairs (the budget engine's OracleSelector). Oracle selection consumes no plug-in scale, so the floor does not depend on ζ and bounds SELECTION only: a refit may cross it by leaving the discrete hypothesis pool. A miscalibration panel carries its floor (Render.emit_miscal_faceted's oracle).

8. Statistics

Point estimates and intervals go through Stats only: finite_median (argmin objectives use finite_median(...; empty = Inf) so an all-NaN column never wins), bootstrap_ci (BCa), scene_mean_median_rows! (the mean of per-scene medians with BCa over scenes, resampled from stable_seed(seed, "scene_mean_median", j)). mAA follows ONE rule, eligible_maa: a pair with ground truth and enough valid correspondences to run is ELIGIBLE (a run that yields no model scores 0 in the denominator), and a pair that cannot be processed is SKIPPED; pair_outcomes reports the counts beside every mAA. mAA is the continuous AUC up to [metrics] maa_threshold_deg (VisualGeometryToolkitCore mean_average_accuracy).

9. Figure conventions

House style, enforced by the Render emitters so figures are uniform across papers:

  • Confidence intervals are shaded bands, never error bars: scene-clustered BCa 95 % (§8).
  • Labels and markers come from the framework, never from hand tables: VisualGeometryToolkitCore lo_label(lo) and lo_marker(lo). A partition-anchored scale is marked -\hat s; a fixed cross-validated scale is unmarked; a self-scaling LO carries no suffix. A renderer never spells a method string.
  • One arm is one colour everywhere; the marker shape is the solver family.
  • The oracle (GT-best) is a solid black line with no markers.
  • Basin panels (Render.emit_basin_faceted): the left panel is the success rate P(e ≤ succ°) on [0, 1]; the right panel is the conditional mean E[e | e ≤ succ°] on a linear axis with degree ticks, masked where the success rate is below [render] min_success_for_cond_mean; the no-LO reference arm is the perturbed seed.
  • Median-error panels keep a log axis (median error is unbounded there).
