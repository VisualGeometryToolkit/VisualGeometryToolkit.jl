Detection

The ASIFT detector

asift_detect is the package's detector. It implements IMAS-25, the affine-invariant SIFT of the IMAS family. The image is resampled under 25 simulated camera tilts (the identity, 8 tilts of 2.62 and 16 tilts of 5.18 at evenly spaced longitudes). DoG extrema are found in each warp, refined to sub-pixel accuracy, mapped back to the input image as elliptic frames, and merged across warps by a non-maximum suppression. The whole pipeline runs as KernelAbstractions kernels, so the same code runs on the CPU (the default) and on any GPU backend (device = CUDABackend(), for instance).

The tilt schedule is a keyword. tilts = [(1.0, 0.0)], the identity alone, is plain DoG-SIFT:

dets = asift_detect(img)                          # IMAS-25
sift = asift_detect(img; tilts = [(1.0, 0.0)])    # DoG-SIFT

The other keywords choose the non-maximum suppression (nms = :mxm, :ellipse or :none), gate by polarity (polarity = :dark or :light), restrict suppression to features of similar scale (band_octaves), and set the detection thresholds. The asift_detect docstring lists them.

Plans and their cache

The detector's buffers for one image size, device and tilt schedule are an AsiftPlan. asift_detect builds plans on demand and keeps the most recent one: a plan for a large image under the full schedule holds several GiB of device memory. Process frames grouped by resolution to reuse it. set_max_cached_plans! keeps more plans, and clear_asift_plans! releases them. To manage a plan yourself, build it and call asift_detect!, which returns the raw ImasFeatureKA records; asift_detections converts them.

Pixel origin

Every detector of the package returns COLMAP pixel centres. The top-left pixel's centre is (0.5, 0.5), so the pixel img[r, c] has its centre at (c − 0.5, r − 0.5). A center is (x, y): x along the image columns and y along the rows.

asift_detect(img; raw = true) returns the raw features instead, in 0-indexed OpenCV coordinates (top-left pixel centre at (0, 0)), which are 0.5 less on both axes.

The package follows the stack's rule that a function returns results in the frame its inputs came in: pixels in, pixels out. The matchers work in descriptor space and never touch coordinates, so nothing here normalizes.

Feature frames are shapes

A Detection composes a feature frame with the detector's metadata. "Frame" is meant in the VLFeat and Kornia sense: the shape of a detected feature, not a coordinate system. The frames are VisualGeometryToolkitCore types, ordered by degrees of freedom:

FrameDOFShape
Disc3centre and isotropic scale
OrientedDisc4centre, scale and orientation
EllipticFrame5centre and moment matrix (anisotropic)
AffineFrame6centre and full affine shape

asift_detect returns Detection{EllipticFrame}. The accessor is feature_frame(d). Properties of the frame and of the metadata read directly: d.center, d.σ (the characteristic scale), d.Σ (the moment matrix), d.response.

Coordinate-frame tags, such as ImageFrame and NormalizedImageFrame, are a separate vocabulary that lives in VisualGeometryToolkitCore. The two share the word "frame" and nothing else.

Polarity

ASIFT records the sign of the DoG response in the metadata field laplacian_scale_score: positive for a dark blob on a lighter background and negative for a light one. is_dark and is_light read it. Detections whose metadata has no such field are neither; the trait polarity_trait tells the two kinds of metadata apart.

Accuracy of the sub-pixel refinement

The quadratic sub-pixel refinement pulls a centre toward the nearest pixel centre. On noise-free isotropic Gaussian blobs on the CPU, measured by the test suite:

  • a blob centred on a pixel centre comes back exact, within 0.005 px;
  • between pixel centres the pull is at most 0.03 px at σ = 4, and at most 0.06 px for σ from 2 to 8.

Larger blobs and clutter do worse. The Monte Carlo study in the package's benchmark/mc directory measured a centre bias of 0.1 to 0.2 px on clean blobs with σ ≥ 6.5, and up to about 1 px in clutter. Treat 0.06 px as the bound for small, isolated blobs only.

Keypoint uncertainty

asift_detect(img; uncertainty = FeatureCovariance()) returns each detection with its frame as VisualGeometryToolkitCore's Uncertain(frame, Σ̃). cov(d) is a 3×3 covariance over (x, y, log σ) in the image frame, for unit iid pixel noise in the detector's intensity units (the [0, 1] scale for 8-bit input). The physical covariance is σ_n²·Σ̃ for a noise standard deviation σ_n. Consumers that need only the position take the top-left 2×2 block.

The covariance follows from the implicit function theorem at the refined DoG extremum; its noise term is tabulated exactly for the discrete pyramid once per plan. The FeatureCovariance docstring states the model. The Monte Carlo study that calibrates it is benchmark/mc/keypoint_uncertainty_mc.jl, and benchmark/mc/zeisl_comparison.jl compares it with the covariance of Zeisl et al. (BMVC 2009). The default, uncertainty = nothing, compiles the detector exactly as without the option.
